## 1. User: why can't the model do both (wide beam → spall, narrow beam → vapour)? (advisor)

The race: the surface temperature ramps up and passes the spall onset (~850 K) before melt (~1500 K) or vapour
(~3300 K) at ANY intensity; the model's flake leaves instantly when the criterion passes, so the hot layer is always
stripped first and the surface never reaches melt. Intensity only changes how fast it climbs (and the removal rate).
Half-space constant-flux estimate (k 2.5, ρc 2.7e6, no radiation): t_onset = π·kρc·(557/2q)²; t_melt (1207 K rise)
= 4.7 × t_onset. q 1 / 10 / 50 / 100 MW/m² → t_onset 1.6 s / 16 ms / 0.66 ms / 0.16 ms; onset→melt gap 6.1 s / 61 ms
/ 2.4 ms / 0.6 ms. ⇒ HYPOTHESIS (not a result): a finite flake detachment time τ ~ 1 ms would let the surface melt
first above ~50 MW/m², near the weak laser spall→melt estimate (5×10⁷). Other candidates: a minimum flake thickness
(a grain-scale floor would forbid Höser's 4–7 MW/m² spalling, so not grain-size), softening near the solidus.
Same-power example: 1 kW at w 20 mm → 1.6 MW/m² peak; at w 3 mm → 71 MW/m². τ is measurable with a high-speed
camera on the user's rig (Höser's 10,000 fps barely resolved ejection).

## 2. User is sceptical of the detachment-time idea; other hypotheses for the spall→melt switch (advisor)

Why τ is weak: a free parameter fitted to the switch (matching, not predicting); ejection at < 0.1 ms (Höser 10 kfps)
would push the switch far higher; a cracked flake shields the rock beneath it.
Candidates:
1. DELAMINATION ENERGY (thin-film mechanics, Hutchinson & Suo): a compressed layer peels off only if its stored
   elastic energy pays the fracture energy, G_ss = (1−ν²)σ²h/(2E) ≥ Γ ⇒ a MINIMUM flake thickness h_min. With
   Γ = K_Ic²(1−ν²)/E = 38 J/m² and σ = UCS 169 MPa (R's calibration): h_min 0.10–0.16 mm ⇒ the switch at
   q ≈ 3–5 MW/m² absorbed at onset stress, higher if the stress keeps rising (×~4 by the solidus) or Γ is lower at
   grain boundaries ⇒ a few to a few tens of MW/m². No free parameter; the partner of buckling (max thickness) ⇒ a
   two-sided thickness window. Observed flakes 0.2–0.5 mm sit above h_min. Predicts the spall T rising with intensity
   before melting takes over.
2. Hot layer thinner than the flaws/grains (size effect): partly in the model already (the depth scan); with an 8 µm
   flaw it bites only at ~10² MW/m².
3. Rising steady spalling-surface temperature (MEASURED, Wilkinson: Barre 500 → 930 °C over 0.6 → 2.9 MW/m²) + softening
   near the solidus (direction step B).
4. A cracked/damaged skin insulates (Woskov's "sand-like rubble"): conductivity drops, the surface overheats, melts.
5. Flakes not cleared under a beam (no gas jet): retained flakes absorb and melt in place.
6. 10 GHz specific: pore water/steam spalling at low intensity (microwaves heat water; concrete's explosive spalling)
   — test oven-dried vs wet samples; and the block heated through (finite body) at low intensity vs local melt at high.
Advisor: model candidate = 1 (physics, measured inputs, same family as B); rig tests = flake thickness vs intensity,
dry vs wet, pyrometer spall T vs intensity.
