## 1. The physics of the lever, and why it is the flaking temperature

Steady floor recession R = h (T_g − T_s) / (ρ c (T_s − T_0)). Conductivity does not enter; ρc is
known to ±5 %; E, α, ν, K_Ic and flaw size enter **only through T_s**. So a material-property band is
a band on T_s, and T_s is what the literature measured. Relative to the present T_s:

| T_s [K] | (T_g−T_s)/(T_s−T_0) at T_g 1464 | floor factor | at T_g 1680 | floor factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 823 (present) | 1.21 | 1.00 | 1.62 | 1.00 |
| 870 (Kant mean, 600 °C) | 1.03 | 0.85 | 1.40 | 0.87 |
| 950 | 0.78 | 0.65 | 1.11 | 0.69 |
| 1000 | 0.66 | 0.54 | 0.96 | 0.59 |
| 1020 | 0.61 | 0.50 | 0.91 | 0.56 |
| 1100 | 0.45 | 0.37 | 0.72 | 0.44 |
| 1190 (Rauenzahn top) | 0.31 | 0.25 | 0.55 | 0.34 |

⚠ The floor gas temperature in the map legs must be read from the run (the marched jet
temperature column), not assumed; the two columns bracket it. Item 0 recomputes this table with the
measured value.

**The photograph gives a two-sided window on the floor factor.** With the map's decay
(`pow(0.09/max(0.09,s),1)`) and `R90` ≈ 3.3 m/h (D2ac implied), the equilibrium standoff at 1.5 m/h
is s* = 0.09 · 3.3 · f / 1.5:

| floor factor f | s* [mm] | reading |
|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | 168 | fails the ≤100 ceiling |
| 0.65 | 129 | fails |
| 0.54 | 107 | borderline |
| **0.50** | **99** | passes the ceiling |
| **0.36** | 71 → flux undecayed; full-flux floor 0.36 × 4.2 = 1.5 = feed | **lower edge: below this the burner catches the floor and rests** |

So "hung, floor ≤ 100 mm" pins f to **0.36–0.50**, i.e. T_s ≈ **1020–1110 K at T_g 1464** (higher
at 1680). That is a calibration target with a width, not a fit to a point. Against the literature
(`meier_thesis_notes.md:123`; `validation/kant/...plan.txt:116-122`): Rauenzahn band 590–1190 K,
Kant mean ≈ 870 K, Kant minimum ≈ 770 K, melting ≈ 1273 K. The target sits in the upper part of the
measured range and below melting. **State that plainly; do not call it central.**

## 2. What sets T_s in the code, and the key to vary

T_s is **emergent**: a cell fires when K_I(σ_thermal, a_f) ≥ K_Ic(T). K_Ic(T) is the Nasseri 2007
Westerly table (`src/Numeric/SpCriterion.H:91-116`): 1.43 / 1.35 / 0.98 / 0.43 / 0.22 MPa√m at
25 / 250 / 450 / 650 / 850 °C, times `spallation.sp.kic.scale` (parse `:8194`, firing site
`MMWSpalling/Removal.H:759`, diagnostic `:5421`). The table's collapse between 450 and 650 °C is why
the firing window is narrow: measured on `FH` 150–400 s, **mean 823 K, p10–p90 815–831 K, 30,716
firings** (`FH_removal_events.csv`, column `T_top`). ⚠ `spall.t_spall` is a *time*, not a
temperature (`Removal.H:48`) — not the lever. Flaw sizes (`weibull.a0_ig` 4 µm, `a0_gb` 20 µm,
`m` 20, `V0` = V_cell) are the other route; prefer the toughness scale, one number, physically
motivated (Nasseri's values are post-thermal-treatment, room-T tests — damaged rock).

Hand mapping from the table, with K_I ∝ ΔT at fixed flaw (c = 0.705/530 MPa√m per K):

| target T_s [K] | K_Ic(T) table | needed `kic.scale` |
|---|---|---|
| 870 | 0.576 | 1.33 |
| 950 | 0.402 | 2.2 |
| 1000 | 0.349 | 2.7 |
| 1050 | 0.297 | 3.4 |
| 1100 | 0.244 | 4.4 |

⚠ Approximate (±10 %): K_I also depends on the stress profile through the skin, which changes with
T_s. **Do not trust the mapping; measure it** — item 0 below.

* (f) SCORED — power identity ≤ 0.02 W; (g) identity `S0` byte-identical, tree gate `--force`.

**Pre-registered predictions.** P1 the window (a) is met by at least one of `K1050`/`K1100` and by
neither `K870` nor `G1450`. P2 the passing leg's Ø lands 82–87 mm (exposure scaling: widening
∝ floor factor ⇒ 7.1 mm × 0.4–0.5 ≈ 3–4 mm over Ø80). P3 the firing window stays ≤ 30 K at every
scale. P4 `R_cal` lands 1.3–1.6 m/h. P5 volume on the passing leg 2.6–3.0 L per 0.5 m (below 3.42,
shortfall = mouth + pause). Refutations: if the window is met only with contact (s* < 71 before
≤ 100 is reached) the decay law is wrong, not T_s; if P3 fails the lever is illegitimate.

**Stage 2 (same packet or next): the band.** Around the calibrated point run the corners
T_s {calibrated − 50, + 50 K} × nozzle T {1450, 1850} — 4 legs — and report **intervals** on

headline** (a T_s band 770–1190 K alone spans 0.9–4.4 m/h in feet mode; that contains everything).

## 6. Literature check on the rock (added 29 Sep, after the brief above) — CORRECTS §1

Meier's block is "Grimsel granite" (thesis p. 208). The ETH spallation group's own reference rock,
Central Aare granite, is sampled 1 km north of the Grimsel Test Site; the Grimsel site rock mass is
Central Aar granite / Grimsel granodiorite. Measured properties of that rock:

| property | measured (site rock) | Meier Tab. 7.1 (what the model uses) | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| density | 2550 (Kant), 2660 ± 24 (Central Aar), 2706 ± 14 (granodiorite) kg/m³ | 2750 | Kant 2017 GRL; Krietsch 2018 |
| heat capacity | 0.768 ± 0.048 kJ/kgK at RT | 0.790 | Kant 2017 GRL |
| conductivity | 3.25 ± 0.07 W/mK at RT intact, → 2.4 after thermal cracking, ≈2 at 500 °C | 1.5 | Kant 2017 GRL |
| Young's modulus | 53.3 ± 11 GPa (Central Aar), 47.3 ± 15 (granodiorite); 53 used by Kant | **30** | Krietsch 2018; Kant 2017 |
| Poisson | 0.37 ± 0.12 / 0.33 ± 0.15 | 0.30 | Krietsch 2018 |
| tensile strength | 9.1 ± 1.5 / 9.5 ± 2.2 MPa | — | Krietsch 2018 |
| UCS | 169 ± 37 / 117 ± 48 MPa | — | Krietsch 2018 |
| linear thermal expansion | not found for this rock; granites 5.5–9e-6 /K at RT, rising steeply above ~400–500 °C (quartz) | 8e-6 constant | general |
| K_Ic at RT | not found for Grimsel (paywalled); granites typically 1.0–2.0 MPa√m; code comment cites 1.5 for Central Aare | Nasseri Westerly table ×1.0 | — |
| K_Ic AT temperature | rises slightly to ~50–120 °C then declines gradually to 200–400 °C (Lanhelin 1.52 → 1.31 MPa√m at 200 °C; Westerly, Meredith & Atkinson 1985, to 400 °C). **No at-temperature granite data above 400 °C.** Nasseri's 0.43/0.22 at 650/850 °C are post-treatment, room-T tests | Nasseri table used as K_Ic(T) | Chandler et al. 2017 review |

**Directly measured spallation surface temperatures for granites:**

| source | rock | value |
|---|---|---|
| Wilkinson & Tester 1993 | Barre, Westerly | 400–550 °C (≈ 670–820 K), below 520 °C without quartz transition |
| Kant & von Rohr 2016 (repo file) | Gotthard, Bethel | surfaces 500–511 °C at onset; mean θ_SP ≈ 600 °C; minimum ≈ 500 ± 50 °C |
| Kant et al. SGW 2017 model, **Central Aare granite** | Central Aare | Θ_SP = **550 °C rise** at the failure plane (≈ 840 K surface) |
| Rauenzahn compilation (thesis Fig. 2.12) | Barre, Westerly | rise 300–900 °C, i.e. 590–1190 K, scatter bounded by Weibull m 15–25 |

**Consequence.** The model's 823 K (550 °C, rise 530 K) sits **in the middle of the direct
measurements**, and matches the value Kant used for this very rock. The 1020–1110 K target derived
in §1 lies **above every direct measurement** and inside only the upper tail of Rauenzahn's scatter.
The rock-side lever therefore honestly buys **≈ 15–25 %** (T_s up to Kant's 600–650 °C mean), not the
factor of two. The measured E (47–53 GPa vs 30) and a rising α(T) both push the thermal stress UP
and T_s DOWN, i.e. the wrong way; the only rock-side quantity that could push T_s up is the
at-temperature toughness of intact granite above 400 °C, which is unmeasured.

**Where the factor of two must then come from:** the floor heat delivery. Kant's burner at
0.45–0.87 MW/m² (h 630–1010 W/m²K, flame 1100–1450 °C) spalls granite; Meier's energy balance needs
a face-mean ≈ 0.5–0.6 MW/m²; the model delivers ≈ 1.2 MW/m² mean over Ø85 at Meier's standoff.
So the packet's calibration target should be reframed: **calibrate the floor flux level (nozzle
temperature within 1450–1850 K, and/or the impingement coefficient within Meier Ch. 3's measured
0.4–1.6 kW/m²K and Kant's 630–1010) on the standoff, with T_s held in 770–920 K.** Run the T_s legs
anyway (cheap, and they falsify the rock-side story quantitatively), but pre-register that a required
T_s > 920 K is a **refutation of the rock-side explanation**, not a calibration.

**Planner: §3's leg table should become** T_s ∈ {823 control, 870, 920} × floor-flux factor
∈ {1.0, 0.7, 0.5} (via nozzle T or the map's h scale, whichever the parse block supports as ONE key),
scored on the same window. Nine legs at 800 s ≈ 5 h with 2 jobs; or the 3 + 3 edges first.
