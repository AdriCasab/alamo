# 2026-09-18 — Advisor's spallation model (F. Monmont, Quaise) vs. MMWSpalling

Source material in `franck_material/`: "Thermal spallation drilling deck 1.pdf" (12 slides, physics
and drilling performance, 100 GHz granite/basalt), "Plasma_presentation (6).pdf" (18 slides,
1 Sept 2026, origins of plasma), `bpm_workflow_diagram.html` (the most detailed description of
his code: modules bidirectional_bpm.py → bidirectional_bpm_cyl.py → dust_plasma.py →
demo_spallation_cyl.py).

## His model in one paragraph
2D axisymmetric (r, z) Python model. Two-level time loop: bidirectional beam propagation (Hankel
transform, Fresnel reflection at every index jump, standing wave in front of the face, measured
complex permittivity 293–1273 K, CRIM mixing with pore water and damage cracks) refreshed every
0.1–0.25 s; heat conduction (finite volume, water-modified ρc and k, adiabatic surface), pore
pressure (liquid expansion / Clausius–Clapeyron / supercritical), closed-form principal effective
stresses in the skin (EαΔT/(1−ν) − curvature relief + Kirsch in-situ + shed load − α_b Δp),
Mohr–Coulomb damage with tension cutoff and a rate law dD/dt = over^n/τ, and detachment by
Hutchinson–Suo buckling-driven delamination (G = G_ss[1 − (σ_buck/σ)²] ≥ G_c, whole damaged layer
leaves as one chip) every 4–6 ms substep. Beam gated by a melt thermostat and a plasma interlock.
Dusty plasma: ejecta pools, thermionic seeding, avalanche margin with field enhancement β at chip
tips, Drude permittivity written into gas cells (shielding loss). Outputs ROP, TSE, spall T, chip
thickness, power budget, plasma trips.

Headline numbers: flat-top 2.5 kW granite: ROP ≈ 3–9 m/h rising with depth (0–3 km, 80 °C/km),
spall T 650–760 K, peak T 1200–1270 K, TSE 0.5–1.1 kJ/cm³ dry; with plasma trips at 2 bar ROP
4.1 m/h, TSE 2.2 kJ/cm³. Measured reflectance 15–19 %, 1/e depth 1–3 mm at 100 GHz. Sidewall
breakout at 3 km. No comparison with experiments shown; the plasma deck lists experiments to run.

## Most consequential
1. **Detachment.** His: damage alone frees nothing; a compressed layer is clamped until it buckles,
   then G ≥ G_c releases it as one chip with definite thickness (chip thickness = his key comparable
   observable). Ours: onset of flaw growth, no explicit release statement; coherence cap and removal
   increment stand in. His lacks flaw statistics/size effect; ours lacks buckling physics.
2. **Source fidelity.** Standing-wave hot spot raises peak intensity ×1.7–2.3; irrelevant for the
   flame validation, first-order for any MMW case.
3. **Regulation.** Both find mean spall T far below peak (his 650–760 K spall vs 1200+ K peak; ours
   820–850 K pinned). Agreement worth noting; consistent with the Rauenzahn band.
4. **Downhole vs lab.** His depth trends and breakout are untestable against our references;
   his model has no microstructure or mesh-convergence story.
5. **TSE** 0.5–1.1 kJ/cm³ (dry, 2.5 kW) is a cross-check number for the Meier specific energy.

## Recommendation
Bring across the Hutchinson–Suo release criterion as the physical replacement for the coherence
cap: K_I onset decides that a layer has delaminated, buckling G decides when it leaves, and the chip
thickness becomes a scored observable against collected spalls. Pore pressure and in-situ stress
next if the programme leaves lab scale. Optics and plasma are a separate project.

# D2d mesh failure: diagnosis from the run logs (reviewer, 2026-09-18)

Data: `studies/d2c_steady/output/{R7b_2mm,R7_1mm}` thermo.dat, `*_removal_events.csv`,
`*_jet_profile.csv`; plus `R7b_2mm_dt16` / `_dt8` (timestep study, other session).

## Facts
1. **Time step is not it.** R7b at 16 / 8 / 4 ms: burner ROP 1.483 / (n/a) / 1.486 m/h over
   150–300 s; 0–50 s block 1.255 / 1.259 / 1.247. Identical T_rec and reach.
2. **The gap opens before the gas side diverges.** Burner ROP 1 mm vs 2 mm by 50 s blocks:
   1.31/1.25 (+5 %), 1.76/1.56 (+13 %), 1.89/1.50 (+26 %), 2.00/1.46, 2.13/1.49, 2.22/1.51 (+47 %).
   Until 150 s the 1 mm run has the *cooler* recirculation (T_rec 1534 vs 1546 K) and the same
   ring gas temperatures (T_in at 30/40 mm: 1673/1629 vs 1674/1631 K at 100 s). Firing
   temperature identical (821 K). Bin radii and s_c are not the cause.
3. **The burner rate is the pad rim.** Reconstructing the feet rule from the removal log
   reproduces `foot_z` to 0.3 mm. The 0.9-quantile pad height H is *always* the 38–40 mm band
   (86–100 % of that band sits at H; no other band does). All mechanical clearance (regime 4)
   is in that band. Rim recession = spall + clip: 2 mm 14 + 11 = 25 mm/min (flat over 300 s);
   1 mm 12→17 spall + 11→19 clip = 26→37 mm/min. So the burner rate is the clip-and-spall
   cycle of the slowest 19 % of pad columns, a cell-quantised process (whole-cell clip throws
   away the heating cell; the fresh cell's preheat is a fixed fraction of a ~3 mm thermal
   layer, so the restart penalty is larger at 2 mm). That is the seed: +5 % at 0–50 s, +11 %
   at 50–100 s, with identical gas.
4. **Pad shape.** At 2 mm the pad is a cone (28→40 mm: 37→25 mm/min) that persists; at 1 mm it
   flattens (37 mm/min everywhere by 300 s). The interior at 2 mm slows to 32 because its
   stand-off grows with the cone (power law h ∝ 1/s), while at 1 mm s stays 50 mm.
5. **The amplifier is the gas closure loop.** Faster burner → nozzle plane deeper → flank
   columns (r > 40 mm) excluded by s ≤ 0 (`jet_r_reach`: 2 mm 71→53 mm, 1 mm 63→40 mm over
   150–300 s; 40–60 mm band removal 1 mm 1.37→0.04 cm³/s vs 2 mm 1.88→1.22) → less enthalpy
   extracted → hotter exhaust → hotter recirculation (T_rec 1686 vs 1623 K) → hotter
   stagnation (1796 vs 1745 K) and less entrained mass (s_c 133 vs 152 mm) → faster ring and
   centre → deeper nozzle. At 300 s the 1 mm run absorbs *less* jet power (1012 vs 1309 W)
   but spends all of it inside r < 40 mm; the 2 mm run spends a third widening the flank.
   The 1 mm hole below the funnel is exactly Ø 80 = the burner.
6. Domain-height effect (+4 % at 0.40 vs 0.70 m) is the same loop with a smaller seed.

## What it is not
Not bin radii, not s_c from the solid top, not the pinned-share criterion as such, not dt.
The S1/D2b mesh checks (trimmed, ring average) tested the mean front, not the slow tail that
the 0.9-quantile rule selects.

## Recommendation for D3 (controlled pairs, 2 mm vs 1 mm to 300 s, matched window)
- P-a: `foot_body_clearance=0` (D2b rule) — isolates the clip cycle (seed).
- P-b: recirculation off / fixed T_mix (e.g. 1550 K) — isolates the loop (amplifier).
- P-c: `foot_rule=mean` — isolates the slow-tail selection.
- Physics that removes both: the tube wall (OD 80 / ID 56, three slots). Rock under the 12 mm
  rim is shielded from the jet (pad shielding) and the gas cannot reach r > 40 mm except
  through the slots (confinement, the user's hypothesis). That kills the free-surface funnel
  in the first 120 s (the 40–60 mm band removes 1.3–1.9 cm³/s then), removes the flank
  "switch", and makes the burner rate a lateral-undercut problem rather than a rim-clip
  problem. Rank it first, not fourth.
