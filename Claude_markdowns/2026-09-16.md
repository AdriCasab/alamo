## D2a: what I disagree with

### 1. The hole-width claim for J-5 / J-10 is an artefact of radially uniform T_gas, in its worst form the s ≤ 0 clamp

`T_gas = T_ent + (T_ref − T_ent)·min(1.4, 7D/max(2D, s))` has **no radial
dependence**, and `h` is clamped to `s ∈ [2D, 12D]`. Consequences:

- Rock at any radius that is within 5 D vertically of the nozzle exit plane,
  or above it (s ≤ 0, clamped to 2 D), receives the **potential-core gas
  temperature** (1983 K for J-M, 1563 K J-5, 1283 K J-10) and the 2 D value
  of h. In the 1.5 m/h runs the nozzle passes the original surface at 119 s,
  so for the last 180 s of a 300 s run the whole unrecessed outer top sits in
  that regime (`s ∉ [2,12] D` share 0.65 for JM_f15, 0.93 for JM_f30).
- Hand check with the study's own profile at the clamp (s = 2 D):

  | anchor | closed-form ROP at r = 150 mm, s clamped | R_h reported |
  |---|---|---|
  | J-10 | 1.67 m/h ≥ 1.5 | 170 mm = domain edge |
  | J-5 | 1.32 m/h < 1.5 | 149 mm |
  | J-M | 0.59 m/h | 64 mm |

  That reproduces the reported R_h ordering and values. The RESULTS explanation
  ("their h at r = 100 mm is still ≈ 2 kW/m²K") is not the mechanism; at the
  true stand-off (7 D) J-5's flux at 100 mm gives ≈ 1.0 m/h and would not keep
  pace. **R_h for J-5 / J-10 is set by rock-above-the-nozzle being heated at
  core temperature.** J-M's 64 mm is the same effect one step milder: the
  bowl's outer ring is within 5 D of the nozzle, so it also gets T_core at
  9 D radial distance. A point 9 D off-axis is not in a jet's potential core.

### 2. Face power 21–29 kW is not "domain-truncated information", it violates the jet's own energy budget

Maximum the gas can deliver to a surface pinned at 821 K:

| T at nozzle exit | ṁ·cp·(T − 821 K) |
|---|---|
| 1900 K (adiabatic) | 19.2 kW |
| 1500 K | 12.1 kW |
| 1436 K (chamber TC, measured) | 10.9 kW |
| rock-side from Meier's data | 2.8 kW |

J-5 / J-10 runs deposit 28.8–29.0 kW; J-M 20.9 kW. The reason is the same as
above: h ∝ 1/r with radially uniform (T_gas − T_s) makes ∫q·2πr dr grow
**linearly with radius**, so "face power" is unbounded and only the domain
edge stops it. Closed-form check at s = 7 D:

| anchor | P over Ø85 face | over Ø340 (≈ domain) |
|---|---|---|
| J-M | 3.8 kW | 16.6 kW |
| J-5 | 7.0 kW | 32.3 kW |
| J-10 | 6.6 kW | 30.3 kW |

Two corrections to my own 09-15b §1 follow: the "low-bracket stagnation flux
fits the volume rate within 35 % untuned" statement was a stagnation-point
statement; integrated over the Ø85 face with this profile the bracket anchors
give 2.4–2.5× the data and J-M 1.4×. And the "face-averaged flux 0.5 MW/m²
forced by the data" is reproduced in the runs only as the prescribed-feed
identity (the study says this correctly).

### 3. What this means for D2b

If D2b adds the feet rule on top of the D2a profile as is, every anchor will
give a hole far wider than Ø85–93 and an outer face heated by gas that does not
exist. The failure would be blamed on the anchors when the cause is the
missing radial cooling of the wall jet. **D2b needs an energy-conserving
T_gas(r, s) before the feet rule.** Not tuning: it is a conservation law plus a
measured input (chamber TC 1436 K, or adiabatic 1900 K as the upper bracket).

Cheapest closure, no free parameter: enthalpy depletion of the wall jet,
marching outward from the stagnation zone,
`ṁ·cp·dT_gas/dr = −q(r)·2πr`, with `q = h(r,s)(T_gas − T_s) − losses`.
Entrainment of cold air is neglected, so this is an **upper bound** on reach.
With h ≈ h_ref·r_ref/r beyond 2.5 D the excess temperature decays as
exp(−(r − r₀)/L), L = ṁcp/(2π h_ref r_ref):

| anchor | L |
|---|---|
| J-M (1527) | 104 mm |
| J-5 (5000) | 32 mm |
| J-10 (10000) | 16 mm |

So under energy conservation the ranking **inverts**: the bracket anchors
spend the jet within ~2 L of the stagnation zone (hole Ø ≈ 90–130 mm), while
Martin's weak h leaves the gas hot out to 200+ mm. That is a testable
prediction for D2b, and it makes T_ref an output: the anchors reduce to h
(Martin vs bracket) with T_jet from the thesis TC. It also removes the
J-M 1983 K > adiabatic inconsistency automatically.

Also for D2b:
- columns with s ≤ 0 (rock above the nozzle exit plane) should get no jet
  flux (or a spent-exhaust T), not the 2 D clamp;
- keep `nozzle_collision_radius`, but the abort should stay meaningful only
  because of the point above;
- report face power against the 10.9–19.2 kW cap as a hard check, not as a
  percentage of 38 kW.

**Six things I would change before `/implement`:**

1. **`foot_rule = max` over the whole annulus stalls on one column.** A rigid
   tripod sits on the plane through three contact points: max under each pad,
   mean over the three pads. Max over 2 mm columns across the whole annulus
   means a single never-fired column (D2a2 saw 6–23 in the bracket runs)
   freezes the burner for the whole run, and that is a discretisation
   artefact, not a result. Use a pad-max / azimuth-mean rule (or p90 over the
   annulus as the proxy) as baseline, log how many columns carry the burner,
   and have `run.py` stop a run whose `foot_stalled_steps` exceeds ~60 s of
   wall time so a stall does not burn an hour.
2. **Hole diameter under the feet rule is geometry, not h.** Any column with
   v(r) < v_ring falls 50 mm behind after 50 mm/(v − v(r)) and freezes (no
   flux above the nozzle plane). With Martin's 1/r shape, dv/v ≈ −2.5 %/mm at
   r = 40 mm, so the frozen depth is d(r) = 50·(1 − k·Δr)/(k·Δr): Ø 93 at

   toward Ø 80, and Meier's 85–93 is what the feet + 50 mm stand-off + 1/r

   the depth-mean Ø, not a band, (b) pre-register d(r) from the hand profile,

3. **Stage A will likely reject all three treatments by its own rule.** A3

   recession, so accept the treatment whose violation does not touch the ring

   applied to the whole hole, where conduction cancels, not per bin.
4. **T_ent = 293 K is a structural low bias in a blind hole.** Below the first
   few centimetres the only gas the jet can entrain is its own exhaust
