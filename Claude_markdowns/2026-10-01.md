## 1. What the P1 packet proposes (ACTIVE_STEP.md, 30 Sep 23:21)

**Change the driving stress from absolute to differential.** Today, at both firing sites
(`Removal.H` `sigma_at_depth` inside `k_i_at_zcrack`, and the `Sp_field` site in
`UpdateSpAfterMechanics`), the stress is

    σ(z) = E·β·(T(z) − T_ref)/(1−ν) + σ_conf        (E 30 GPa, β 8e-6, ν 0.30 ⇒ 0.343 MPa/K)

loaded onto a per-cell Weibull flaw a (8 µm, 40 µm on grain boundaries) through the Tada
integral K_I = 2√(a/π)∫σ(ξa)g(ξ)dθ, firing when K_I ≥ K_Ic(T) (Nasseri: 1.43 MPa√m at 298 K,
0.98 at 723, 0.43 at 923, 0.22 at 1123). Because a ≪ dz the integral samples one point, so the
criterion is a function of the surface temperature alone. The packet adds
`spall.driving_stress = absolute | differential`; under `differential`

    σ(z) = max(0, E·β·(T(z) − T(z + d_ref))/(1−ν))     with d_ref = spall.driving_ref_depth = 1 mm

at both sites (and σ_xx(z) − σ_xx(z + d_ref) on the mechanics-solve branch), reads the
reference through the continuous profile (never by cell-index stepping, which at 1 mm vs 2 mm
cells lands in the same cell and makes the key silently inert), refits the flaw size once
against the Kant onset (estimate a0_ig 8 → 27 µm), adds a unit test (a column uniformly at the
firing temperature must fire under `absolute` and never under `differential`), sweeps d_ref over
0.5 / 1 / 2 mm, and re-runs Meier. Pre-registered: wall firings beyond 40 mm fall ≥ 2×, cap
grip beyond 40 mm < 20 %, bore on the 150–300 mm section 88–96 mm, rate falls. It forbids tying
d_ref to h_f or ℓ_eff as "exactly inert", and forbids enlarging the flaw as a gradient mechanism
(correctly: through the Tada integral a 1 mm flaw gives a sharp front 3.2× LESS drive than a
flat column — the wrong sign for suppressing preheated slabs).

**This is the gradient-dependent spallation the user asked for on 30 September** (the stress
at a point is local in temperature — half-space exact — so the gradient can only enter through
a temperature DIFFERENCE over a finite length; the packet supplies that length). The structure
of the packet is right. One line must change and one guardrail must be reversed.

## 2. The correction: reference the stress jump across the FLAKE, not a fixed depth

### 2.1 Two gradient routes with opposite signs

* **Drive.** No temperature drop across the flake ⇒ no stress mismatch with the substrate ⇒ no
  delamination (buckling / energy-release, Evans & Hutchinson 1984). A uniformly preheated slab
  does not spall. The packet captures this.
* **Statistics / toughness.** A steeper gradient ⇒ a thinner stressed layer ⇒ fewer flaws
  sampled ⇒ a HIGHER spall temperature. Wilkinson (MIT 1989) Figs 1.8/1.9 measured exactly
  this: T_s 480 °C at 0.3 MW/m² → 700–830 at 1.4 → 930 at 2.9, Weibull-fitted m ≈ 8 (Barre) /
  13 (Westerly). Kant 2017's validated local form is flux-BLIND.

A FIXED d_ref implements the first route with the anti-second sign. On an exponential front the
drive fraction is 1 − e^{−d_ref/ℓ}: 0.91 at ℓ 0.42 mm, 0.63 at 1.0, 0.49 at 1.5, 0.13 at 7 mm.
A sharper (faster) front gets MORE drive and fires COOLER — opposite to the data — and at the
axis it is a positive feedback (faster ⇒ sharper ⇒ fires cooler ⇒ more flux ⇒ faster).

### 2.2 Pre-registered failure of the packet's own Kant criterion (c)

The anchor's two legs are cold-start erfc profiles: at onset √(κt) = 3.7 mm (h150, ≈ 20 s) and
1.0 mm (h500, ≈ 1.5 s), κ = 6.9e-7 m²/s. Temperature drop over the first 1 mm as a fraction of
the superheat: 0.22 (h150) vs 0.64 (h500). Refit the flaw so h500 fires at 461 K and solve
C·f·ΔT = K_Ic(T0 + ΔT) for h150 with the Nasseri table:

| d_ref | drop fraction h150 / h500 | h150 fires at superheat |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 mm | 0.11 / 0.38 | ≈ 665 K |
| 1.0 mm | 0.22 / 0.64 | ≈ 635 K |
| 2.0 mm | 0.41 / 0.91 | ≈ 598 K |

All outside 390–560 K. (The toughness falling with T rescues it from a 2.9× to a 1.4× ratio,
not to 1.) The packet names P6 (the column level) as its most likely failure; it is (c).

### 2.3 The fix

    z_ref = h_f = c_f·ℓ_eff, capped by spall.flake_h_max  (the shipped flake thickness)

i.e. the stress jump between the candidate flake and the rock directly beneath it. The packet
forbids this because on every exponential front the fraction is the constant 1 − e^{−c_f} =
0.33, independent of ℓ. That constancy is not inertness — it is Kant's flux-blindness,
reproduced with NO refit: through the erfc anchor profiles the fractions are 0.32 (h150, h_f =
1.5 mm capped) and 0.29 (h500, h_f = 0.36 mm). The discrimination the packet needs is against
NON-exponential profiles, and it is delivered: on the rev-7 wall columns (T_surf − T_top ≈
30 K, flat beneath) the drop across 1.5 mm is ≈ 0.075 of the superheat vs 0.33 on the floor —
4.4× less drive. The uniform-slab unit test passes identically (zero jump). The floor's firing
temperature, the rate and the P0b energetics are untouched; only non-self-similar columns stop
firing. Wilkinson's T_s(q) rise is the second route and is a SEPARATE additive step (per-grain
or stressed-volume flaw draws), not a competitor to this one.

User's position (1 Oct): agrees with the flake-scale reference.

  `driving_ref_depth` for the (g) sensitivity sweep; `flake` needs no new length and no
  refit (verify, do not assume: criterion (c) must still be run).
* ⚠ Read the reference temperature from the EXPONENTIAL reconstruction
  T0 + (T_surf − T0)·e^{−z/ℓ_eff}, not from the piecewise-linear `T_at_zv`: below dz/2 that
  routine interpolates between cell centres, so the same physical depth reads differently at
  2 / 1 / 0.5 mm and the column criterion (d) will show it as a mesh spread.
* `max(0, ·)` stays; confining pressure cancels under the subtraction (state it in the comment).
* The `stress_mf` branch is the MMW route: σ_xx(z) − σ_xx(z + h_f) from the mechanics solve,
  same criterion. See §3 for the caveats.
* Witness that the key is live: log the per-firing ratio (T_surf − T(h_f))/(T_surf − T0). It
  must read ≈ 0.33 on the floor and ≪ 0.1 on the wall band; one value everywhere means the
  reference is not being resolved. Second witness: at the surface dT/dz = q/k, so on the floor
  the drive should be ≈ E·β·h_f·q/(k(1−ν)).
* Do NOT use a within-cell temperature gradient as the stress variable (user's question, 1 Oct):
  stress is local in T; a gradient becomes a stress only when multiplied by a length, the only
  physical length is h_f, and gradient·h_f is merely the small-flake limit of the difference
  form (20 % high on an exponential at c_f 0.4, wrong when the profile curves within the
  flake). The cell has no within-cell gradient anyway — only the two-cell reconstruction, from
  which both derive — and a gradient form does not transfer to the mechanics-solve branch.

## 3. Is this consistent with the unified MMW model? (user's question, 1 Oct)

Yes at the criterion level: drive = stress jump across the candidate flake, with σ(z) from the
local thermoelastic formula where the heated depth is small against the heated radius (flame,
δ 0.3–1.6 mm vs R 40 mm) and from the mechanics solve where it is not (MMW, 1/α 3–30 mm vs spot
10–20 mm, in-plane constraint relieved). The packet's deliverable 1 already carries both
branches. Caveats to carry into the MMW steps:
* Volumetric heating with surface losses can put the temperature maximum BELOW the surface; the
  surface flake is then cooler than the rock beneath, the jump is negative and the clamp gives
  zero drive. That is correct for surface flaking; failure in that regime is a subsurface
  tensile mode (the MMW gradient gate), a different criterion.
* The flake-thickness law (c_f·ℓ_eff from a two-cell EXPONENTIAL fit) is flame-specific and
  meaningless for a volumetric profile. For MMW h_f must come from the stress profile itself
  (e.g. the depth where the stress jump peaks, or an energy-release criterion). The criterion
  unifies now; the thickness law does not yet.

## 4. How sub-cell spallation works in the model (user's question, 1 Oct)

Cells are 2 mm, flakes 0.3–1 mm. Four pieces, none of which change the conduction grid:
1. **A sub-cell profile, reconstructed** from the top two cell averages (exponential fit ⇒ ℓ_eff
   and T_surf; on the Meier floor x = dz/ℓ ≈ 1–2, so the surface is 100–250 K hotter than its
   cell). This is what made the firing criterion mesh-independent (P0).
2. **A criterion evaluated on that profile**, never on a cell average; with §2.3 the reference
   at depth h_f is read from the same reconstruction.
3. **A sub-cell removal that conserves energy**: shift the column's enthalpy profile up by h_f
   using the exponential sub-cell shape; the top h_f leaving is the sink; a per-column offset
   records the true surface inside the top cell; void the cell when the offset crosses dz and
   hand the remainder down. The ledger closes to ≤ 5e-4 (column) and 1e-4 (Meier).
4. **A whole-cell thermal frame**: the conduction kernel never reads the partial fraction; the
   top cell is thermally a full dz whose top face is the surface; the true surface sits up to
   one cell lower. Price: lateral conduction between neighbours can be misaligned by up to one
   cell (same class as the existing staircase approximation).
Consequences: after a flake leaves the new surface is at T(h_f) on the shifted profile and
reheats (0.5–1 s at Meier flux); the construction rests on the profile being near-exponential
within the top two cells. Where it is not — side-heated wall columns, the first second of a
cold start — the fit returns a long or clamped ℓ_eff and the sub-cell picture degrades. That is
exactly the population §2 addresses; the alternative, resolving the layer with a finer surface
band (AMR), is parked by the user.

## 5. Effective temperature scale

For a given profile shape the criterion collapses to a surface-temperature threshold: stress is
linear in the superheat and toughness falls with temperature, so "K_I reaches K_Ic" is one
temperature — ≈ 760 K (465 K superheat) for Grimsel with a0_ig = 8 µm, set by E, β, the flaw
size and the toughness curve and by nothing about the flame. With the flake-scale differential
form that remains true on every self-similar front (the drop across the flake is always 0.33 of
the superheat) and stops being true exactly where it should: a flat slab at 760 K has no drop
and does not fire. The rate then follows from energy, v = q_net/(ρc·(T̄_flake − T0)), with the
threshold entering only through the driving ΔT = T_g − T_s and the enthalpy per volume.

## 8. User objection: "the stress is computed solely on the cell below" — a better datum (expanded)

**The problem.** Cells are 2 mm, flakes 0.3–1 mm; everything that decides spalling lives inside
the top cell, for which the solver holds one average. The sub-cell profile must be reconstructed
from information outside the cell.

**What the model knows at the top of a column, every step:** (a) the top cell average, (b) the
next cell's average, (c) the heat flux entering the top face, q = h(T_g − T_surf) − losses —
exact in the model's own terms. A two-parameter shape (exponential, surface rise A and decay
length ℓ — exact for a steadily receding front) needs two of these. Today: (a) + (b); the ratio
gives ℓ, then A.

**Why (b) is a poor datum.** The second cell is 2–4 mm down and is warm or cold for reasons
unrelated to the surface: in a cold start it has not warmed (ratio huge ⇒ layer read too thin ⇒
surface over-read ⇒ premature firing); beside the hole wall it is warm from the SIDE (ratio ≈ 1
⇒ ℓ 7–77 mm ⇒ the flake rule asks for a slab — the whole wall-band problem). The information is
at fault, not the formula.

**The proposal: use (a) + (c).** Fourier: dT/dz|₀ = −q/k — the surface gradient, exactly known.
For the exponential the surface slope is A/ℓ, so
    A/ℓ = q/k            (how steeply the heat is concentrated at the surface)
    A·ℓ(1−e^{−dz/ℓ})/dz = T_top − T0   (how much heat the top cell holds)
Solve jointly (q depends on T_surf through the Robin BC ⇒ a 1-D Newton per column, as the face
form already does). Much heat + gentle slope = thick warm layer; little heat + steep slope = thin
hot skin.

**On the three cases that matter.** Steady front: identical to today. Cold start: on a constant-
flux erfc at the h500 condition, 2 mm mesh, the two-cell fit over-reads A by 25 %, the flux fit
under-reads by 7 % (0.5 mm: 2 % vs 1 %); h150: 2.6 % vs 1.0 %. Side-heated wall column: small
top flux ⇒ small surface slope ⇒ long ℓ for the RIGHT reason ⇒ small stress jump across the
flake ⇒ no firing — the physics stops it, no cap needed. ⚠ Evidence the present anchor has the
cold-start error: h500 fires at 1.44 s (2 mm) vs 1.81 s (0.5 mm); exact reconstruction would
coincide. Score the anchor on firing TIME across meshes, not on the estimator's own superheat.

**Connection to the gradient criterion.** Stress jump across the flake
σ = Eβ·(q/k)·ℓ(1−e^{−h_f/ℓ})/(1−ν) ≈ Eβ·(q/k)·h_f/(1−ν) for thin flakes: gradient × flake
thickness × thermoelastic modulus. On a steady front h_f = c_f·κ/v and q = ρcvΔT̄ ⇒ q·h_f =
c_f·k·ΔT̄ ⇒ σ = Eβ·c_f·ΔT̄/(1−ν), flux-independent — Kant's firing temperature on a normal front
is unchanged.

**Free bonus.** Three data, two unknowns ⇒ one residual. If (a), (b), (c) cannot be fitted by one
exponential the column is not a receding front — a clean per-column flag for "heated from
somewhere other than its surface", which caps and radial bins were approximating.

**Limits.** Still an assumed two-parameter shape; side heat landing IN the top cell inflates
T_top ⇒ longer ℓ ⇒ less drive (safe direction); does not carry to MMW (surface gradient set by
losses, not heating — needs the resolved stress profile from the mechanics solve).

**No-reconstruction alternatives.** Per-column 1-D sub-grid over the top few mm (≈ 20 cells of
0.25 mm coupled to the coarse grid; resolves the flake; the K_I(z) scan then yields h_f and c_f
becomes an output), or the AMR surface band (parked). Both are more plumbing than the estimator.

**Recommendation.** Flux-anchored estimator inside P1 with the flake-scale reference; cell below
kept only as the consistency residual; Kant anchor rescored on firing time; sub-grid decision
only if the wall band is still ambiguous afterwards.

## 12. Proposed fix for the P1 stall: the flake-differential criterion on EVERY exposed face

**Rule.** For each face of a surface cell that touches void (top and sides): (1) two-cell
exponential fit along the inward normal (cell + next cell inward) ⇒ face temperature and
decay length normal to the face; (2) stress jump across one flake thickness inward from that
face (eq. 4.3 along the normal); (3) fire if K_I ≥ K_Ic(T_face). A cell fires if any face
fires. Top-face firing = today's sub-cell flake + remap. Side-face firing = whole-cell void via
the existing per-cell removal path, sink = the cell's own enthalpy (accurate because a
side-fired cell is hot through). One-wide pillar (void both sides): declare unsupported, remove
when above the firing temperature, log the count.
**Why it fixes the stall without reopening the wall.** Side flux already differs by location:
inside 40 mm a side face gets the column's floor coefficient (~1 MW/m²), beyond the wall the
exhaust coefficient (~0.1 MW/m²). Drive ∝ q·h_f ⇒ pillar sides fire, wall faces do not, by the
same physics that silenced r > 50 mm in P1. ⚠ The 1-D alternative (scan each column for the
depth of maximum jump so a pillar fires at its cold root) is wrong: it fires the hole wall at
its root too — lateral support is visible only face by face.
**Exists already:** per-cell removal path enumerates exposed faces (side faces on the cell
average), Step 20 surface-normal kernel (off on all Meier runs), side-face flux per face.
**Unit tests before Meier:** hot pillar on cold floor under floor-level side flux ⇒ fires;
hot wall backed by cold rock under exhaust flux ⇒ does not; hot floor skin ⇒ unchanged (column
spread, Kant anchor); uniform slab ⇒ zero.
**Pre-registered Meier:** 20–30 mm roughness 128 → ≲ 10 mm, no collision; volume per mm of
advance 3.1 → toward 7 mL/mm; mouth ~95–100 mm; r > 50 silent; 40–50 mm still fires (map).
**General route later:** elastic equilibrium on the void geometry (stress_mf) + the same
per-face differential criterion — captures pillar constraint relief directly; MMW needs it.

## §24 Best next step: lateral drive AND lateral removal together (23:05)

Drive-only is incoherent on the wall. A wall column at r = 42 mm has its top at ~100 mm depth and an exposed riser running down to the floor at ~390 mm; the hot part of that face is near the floor, where the gas is. A lateral drive evaluated there that then removes the column's **top** cell takes rock 300 mm away from the heat and does not widen the hole where it is heated. On the floor staircase (1-cell steps) drive-only happens to be right because the face cell is the top cell; on the wall it is not. So the combined package is not a scope choice, it is what makes the lateral mode well-posed.

**P1f (combined), three parts, two keys, one Meier leg:**

## §26 P1f rev 2: the two-sided (apex) cell and the exponential — what still needs pinning (00:20)

**The exponential on a two-sided apex cell — one part is exact, one part is not.**
1. **Slope: exact at any angle.** For a planar exponential profile, shifting the integration square by one cell in x multiplies the cell average by the same factor e^{−dx|n_x|/ℓ} whatever the angle, so x_lat = ln((T_c−T0)/(T_b−T0)) and ℓ_lat = dx|n_x|/x_lat are exact for cell averages. The vertical formula inherits this. Keep.
2. **Face temperature: NOT exact, and ambiguous by up to half a cell.** The vertical path extrapolates T_s = T0 + (T̄−T0)·x/(1−e^{−x}), which assumes the surface sits at the cell's outer face. On a 45° staircase the gas-wetted surface is a zigzag: the apex cell's centre is 0.707 dx from the plane through the outer corners, 0.354 dx from the zigzag's mid-plane, and 0 from the plane through the inner corners. The extrapolated superheat differs between these choices by e^{x/2}, i.e. **≈ 40 % at ℓ_lat = dx** and 18 % at ℓ_lat = 2dx. ⇒ **Do not extrapolate T_f from the exponential laterally.** Take T_f from the side-face Robin's own face Newton (the packet already says "anchor on `lat_Ts`"; make it the rule in deliverable 1: *T_f := the Robin face temperature; the exponential supplies only ℓ_lat and T(h_f) = T0 + (T_f−T0)e^{−h_f/ℓ_lat}*). Then the only staircase-position choice lives in the face Newton's half-cell distance (dx|n_x|/2, already specified) and the flux and the criterion read the same temperature — P0b's rule, kept.
3. **Two faces, one cell.** An apex cell yields two (cell, face) candidates with the same flaw and near-identical drive; fire one, void the cell once (the packet's "largest K_I/K_Ic" does this). Add: **on a column where the vertical criterion also fires this step, vertical wins** — otherwise the floor mode is touched on wall-top cells, against P5.
4. **Far field behind a soaked wall is not 293 K.** The two-cell form then gives T_b ≈ T_c, x → 0, ℓ_lat large, h_f capped, drive → 0: no thermal shock, no spall. Physically right. ⚠ But it means **`flake_h_max` is the wall's flux threshold**: drive_frac = 1 − e^{−1.5 mm/ℓ_lat} with ℓ_lat = k(T_f−T0)/q.
