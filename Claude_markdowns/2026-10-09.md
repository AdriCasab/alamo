## §1 — The flaw volume anchor: `weibull.V0` cannot do two jobs

## §2 — Literature grounding

**Dey & Kranz (1985) are the direct precedent**, via Walsh/Lomov/Roberts GRC 2011: they "estimated
spall rates by **relating heat penetration depths to an assumed Weibull distribution** of
fracture-nucleation sites." Their four assumptions read as a checklist against ours: compressive
failure by the uniaxial-splitting mechanism (⇒ the `UCS/sigma` band); heat diffusing a short distance
so the stress state ignores hole shape; **plate buckling** for separation (⇒ P2-2e, named in the
founding paper); Weibull at the **spall-thickness scale**.

**The same problem has a name in another field.** The Beremin local approach to cleavage fracture
states both things we derived independently: the reference volume "only scales the microcrack size
distribution but does not change the distribution shape" (⇒ it and the scale parameter are **one
calibration**), and taking it as the mesh size is acceptable only "for low stress gradients, [where]
the mesh size is not significant." A 2 mm skin across 500 K is the opposite case.

**⚠⚠ THE DECISIVE NUMBER, already on disk** in
`tests/MMWSpalling/validation/spall_thickness/REFERENCES.md`: Wilkinson & Tester 1993 report
**m = 20 MECHANICAL** vs **8 (Barre) / 13 (Westerly) back-fitted from SPALLATION**. The
spallation-inferred modulus is roughly **half** the mechanical one — published in 1993. So a low fit
is the documented behaviour of this measurement, not a defect. Shen 2018 (280 spalls) recommends
**10-17** and aspect ratio **6.91**; Walsh 2014 Fig 4 gives aspect ratio 1.5:1 at 200 um rising to
~6:1 at 1 mm.
⚠⚠ **LOAD-BEARING AND SECOND-HAND:** `REFERENCES.md` states three of four primary sources are
paywalled and were never fetched. Independent search corroborates 10-17 / 15-20 / 15-25 but **not**
the mechanical-vs-spallation split. **Obtain Wilkinson & Tester 1993 before writing this up.**

**The convention, resolved.** `m_strength = 2*m_flaw`, and under that substitution the spread in
firing stress is **identical to every digit** — so P2-2c stays retired and the reviewer **withdrew**
their claim that a strength variate buys scatter relief (they had compared a flaw-length spread
against a strength spread). ⚠ **But the convention is not cosmetic:** published granite moduli are
*strength* moduli, so it decides whether our number is comparable at all. At `n` = 3 the chain gave
`m_flaw` 3.40-5.05 / `m_strength` **6.80-10.10** — the latter brackets Barre's **8**.

**Geometry.** Generalising `V ~ ell_q^n` gives `e = n/(2m+n)`, so `m = (n/2)(1-e)/e` is **linear in
`n`**: a 2x error in the geometry power is a 2x error in the modulus. `n = 3` assumes geometric
similarity; real spalls are thin disks with a size-dependent aspect ratio ⇒ `n > 3`, which moves
`m_strength` from Barre's 8 (n=3) through Westerly's 13 (n=4) to Shen's band (n=5). ⚠ **Report `n`,
do not fit it:** a power law from two anchors predicts 1.21x thickness growth where `REFERENCES.md`
records 1.36x.

**Two confirmations.** Literature puts granite spallation at **550-600 degC = 823-873 K**, exactly
Kant's two numbers, so the 850 K target is mid-range of the *literature*, not just of Kant. And
Wilkinson & Tester found flame-jet heating gives "local overheating and possibly **stress relief**
[leading] to higher temperatures than predicted using room-temperature Weibull parameters" — which
names stress relief as real and predicts the direction of the ~93 K `T_s` rise under option A.
⚠ Their own warning, worth keeping while chasing a 2 % residual: agreement "could simply be the
result of fortuitous cancellation of errors."
