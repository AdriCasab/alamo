## 0. What S1 established (decision record)

1. **ROP under a prescribed flux is converged at 2 mm, by construction.** Every
   removed cell leaves at ρ·Cp·ΔT_fire whatever dz, so ROP = q/(ρ·Cp·ΔT_fire).
   F1: 9.40 m/h at 2 mm vs 9.37 at 0.125 mm. This carries to 2D/3D.
2. **The criterion is a cell-average temperature threshold** in self-heated
   ablation. Firing T = 656 K for a = 20 µm (S1), ≈ 812 K for the production
   Weibull draw (a_ig ≈ 4.4 µm, Meier/Kant). Same criterion, different flaw.
3. **h_col is a removal increment, not a flake.** The depth scan
   (`Removal.H` ~506–560, ~745–800) returns the depth to which a flaw of length
   a_f is still critical. At marginal firing that depth is zero in the
   continuum limit; the code returns h ≈ dz/2 − a_f only because T is flat
   across the top cell (0.49 dz at 2 mm → 0.22 dz at 0.0625 mm, as measured).
   Two firings on consecutive steps empty one cell. Harmless for energy and
   ROP. The Rossi "775 µm floor" is dz/2 at 1.5625 mm. **Flake/PSD claims are
   retired for the drilling regime**; the scan stays because in prescribed-T
   tests (Hu, Kant onset) it computes a well-defined isotherm depth.
4. **Robin sources are not converged at 2 mm** and the two discretisations
   bracket the answer (cell +42 %, face −82 % at 2 mm). The converged 1-D answer
   is a closed form: **q = h·(T_gas − T_fire) − εσ(T_fire⁴ − T_a⁴)**, i.e. the
   surface is pinned at the firing temperature while the column spalls.
   Check: T_fire = 660 K, ΔT_rem = 367 K → 3.39 MW/m², 15.3 m/h; S1's
   R-face extrapolation 3.5 MW/m², 15–16 m/h.
5. **Melt is unreachable for physics reasons, not resolution** (P1 stands):
   steady ablation gives a monotone profile (Pe = v/(ακ) ≈ 275 at α = 100/m,
   15 MW/m²), and K_Ic(T) falling to 0.22 MPa√m by 850 °C means spall always
   wins the race to the surface temperature. MMW melt needs missing physics
   (loss of elastic driving stress at high T, or a heated-layer-thickness
   dependence in the criterion). Parked until the MMW anchor step.
6. **Time-step rule for removal runs** (binding, not the conduction limit):
   overshoot per step ΔT_step = q·dt/(ρ·Cp·dz). For ≤ 5 K:
   dt ≤ 11 ms (2 mm, 2 MW/m²), 1.4 ms (2 mm, 15 MW/m²), 0.7 ms (0.125 mm,
   2 MW/m²). S1's M-cases at fine dz overshoot 50–110 K per step; that is
   the p10–p90 spread in their firing T, not physics. Write into the S1 README.
7. **Bugs found:** (a) `weibull.enabled = 0` + removal segfaults —
   `flaw_a_mf` is registered only under `weibull_enabled`
   (`MMWSpalling.H:443-445`) but `Removal.H:472/512` dereference it
   unconditionally; the criterion path guards it (`MMWSpalling.H:2660`), the
   removal path does not. (b) `h_col_events_csv` logs only the first firing
   per new top cell → sums undercount recession ≈ 2×.

**Energy accounting (Meier pilot, from the reference packet):**

Reading of this table:

- **Volume removal rate is already close.** The low end of Meier's own bracket
  gives 3.3–3.4 cm³/s against 2.47 measured (+35 %). The thermostat physics,
  ΔT_fire in the Kant band, and the energy machinery are not what is off.
- **ROP is off by ~4× because the model puts all the removal into the
  impingement footprint.** The experiment drills an 85 mm hole (2.9× the
  footprint area) at 1.5 m/h; the model drills a footprint-sized hole at
  ~6 m/h. Same volume rate, different distribution.
- **The upper half of the bracket is excluded by energy**, not by the model:
  (30 k, 1700) demands more than the flame delivers on the floor alone, and
  (10 k, 1400) demands 30 % of the flame on 2 % of the hole surface. The
  rock-side balance says 13–15 % in total. Any Robin sweep must be capped by
  ∫q·dA ≤ (share of 38 kW) and reported against that cap.

**What the thesis (Ch. 8, checked 2026-09-15 evening against pp. 193–226)
adds — this supersedes the "wall took the energy" picture written earlier
today:**

- **How the burner advanced.** The burner rests on feet on the rock; the
  feet fix SOD = 7 D (p. 212). The crane was operator-driven and remote
  controlled (p. 214); the drawworks applied a constant axial push "to keep
  the drill free" with "intermittent and rough axial displacement"
  (pp. 219–220). ROP was read from marks on the coil tubing every 10 cm; the
  crane scale showed whether the burner hung or lay on the rock
  (pp. 214–215, 221). The burner can drop only once the rock under its feet
  is gone: **ROP = recession rate of the face's outer ring at the burner
  radius, where the jet flux is lowest.** The operator either keeps up or
  holds back; holding still widens the hole (p. 223). "The diameter of the
  drill controls the smallest hole diameter" (p. 223).
- **Removal happens at the advancing face, not on the side wall.** The jet
  spreads radially over the face; the outer ring of the face spalls, which
  is why the hole is at least burner-sized. The side wall conducts heat into
  the shell but does not do the removal. The "30–50 mm footprint" is the
  wiki's guess, not Meier's.
- **The 4× is the area ratio and nothing else.** 2.47 cm³/s ÷ 56.7 cm²
  (Ø 85) = 1.57 m/h. A model that matches the volume rate but spreads it
  over Ø 50 mm is 2.9× fast, over Ø 40 mm 4.5× fast. Face-averaged flux
  forced by the data: ≈ 0.5 MW/m² ≈ 2.8 kW ≈ 7 % of 38 kW.
- **Data self-consistency:** 1.5 m/h × 1383 s = 58 cm but the block is
  50 cm; 3.42 L over 50 cm is Ø 93 mm, not 85. The consistent triple is
  (2.47 cm³/s, Ø 93, 1.3 m/h) or (2.47, Ø 85, 1.57). **Target ROP 1.3–1.6
  m/h, tolerance ±20 %.** 1383 s is the two drilling phases only (Fig. 8.7);
  the break is already excluded.
- **Other corrections:** cyclone collected 82 % of the mass (60 % is the
  share sized, 82 % × 72 %; pp. 224, 226). Combustion is completed inside an
  oversized chamber (p. 215); chamber thermocouple 1163 °C uncorrected; the
  cooling-water mantle is an unquantified loss before the nozzle, so the
  rock-side share should be quoted against gas enthalpy at the nozzle, which
  is < 38 kW. Vacuum added between phases changed the exhaust path with no
  ROP change → debris shielding unlikely to set the rate. Meier's Ch. 7 model
  never removes rock (Ø 40 mm patch, ≤ 10 s, lab core, p. 193) and he never
  modelled the pilot (p. 224): nothing to compare against. No wall
  thermocouple exists; the only rock TC is under the block. Fig. 8.8 shows a
  thermally altered band beside the hole (rough check from the photo only).

**Are we close?** In the mechanics, yes: with no tuning, the low end of the
literature bracket at the stagnation point gives the measured volume rate
within ~35 %, and ΔT_fire sits in the Kant band. In ROP the gap is the face
area, and ROP becomes a genuine prediction only with the feet constraint
**and** a jet model that makes the flux depend on radius and local
stand-off. Without stand-off dependence a feet rule gives either an
unbounded central pit (peaked flux) or the trivial face-averaged answer
(uniform flux). With it, the centre digs until its growing stand-off lowers
its flux to the ring's, which is what really happens. Impinging-jet
correlations (Nu falling ~3–4× by r ≈ 6 D; temperature excess ∝ D/s beyond
the potential core) are literature inputs, not knobs; a stagnation flux of
≈ 1.9 MW/m² with a normal radial decay averages to ≈ 0.5 MW/m² over Ø 85.

**Unknowns vs observables:** knobs = (h, T_gas) at the stagnation point,
inside the brackets; jet radial/axial decay from correlations; burner
outer/foot diameter (drawings: thierrymeier.ch/documents/drillBabydrill_VT5_R4.pdf;
Fig. 8.3 suggests 70–80 mm). Observables = ROP 1.3–1.6 m/h, hole Ø 85–93 mm
and ≥ burner Ø, volume 3.42 L, flat ROP in time, no axial fractures, width
of the altered band (rough). That is a validation; a single efficiency
number is not.

**Implementation consequence:** because removal is at the face, D2 does NOT
need a Robin flux on side-wall faces through the surface normal. The
follow-mask column-top patch is enough, given a spatial profile keyed to the
burner position and local stand-off, plus the burner descent rule.

## 4. Packet C — pinned-surface Robin closure

- New `surface_patch.robin_form = pinned` (or a sub-option of `face`):
  before a column's first firing use the face form; after it, evaluate the
  flame flux **and the radiation/convective losses** at T_s = T_fire(col),
  where T_fire(col) is the top-cell temperature recorded at that column's
  last firing (store per column; reset if the column stops spalling for
  longer than one heating cycle ρ·Cp·dz·ΔT_fire/q and fall back to face).
- Rationale: in the spalling regime the physical surface is pinned at
  T_fire; the 2 mm cell average is 100–300 K low for most of each heating
  cycle, so cell and face forms both misread the driving force. The closure
  is also what α(T), R(T) and re-radiation should read later.
- Validity: assumes flakes thinner than δ = κ/v. At Meier's 1.5 m/h
  δ = 1.7 mm — fine. At 15 m/h δ = 0.17 mm — weaker; report the (pinned,
  cell) pair as the band where it matters.
- **A/B:** S1 column, R-face at 2 mm with `pinned` vs the existing 0.0625 mm
  R-face run: expect 15.3 m/h and 3.4 MW/m² within ~5 %; R-face-1400 likewise
  vs its 0.125 mm run (≈ 26 m/h, further from converged, so ±15 %).
- Energy ledger must still close to ≤ 1e-9 with the closure on.
- Default-off; witnesses identical.

**D2. Impinging-jet face source + burner-on-feet descent → emergent ROP and

- **Source:** Robin at the face through the existing follow-mask column-top
  patch with the pinned closure (Packet C), but with (h, T_gas) varying in
  space: h(r, s) and T_gas(r, s), where r is radius from the burner axis and
  s(r) = z_burner − z_face(r) is the local stand-off. Radial and axial
  shapes from impinging-jet correlations (Nu(r) falling ~3–4× by r ≈ 6 D;
  temperature excess ∝ D/s beyond the potential core, flat inside it);

  optional operator feed. Face cells inside the ring may run ahead (central
  pit); the axial jet decay is what limits the pit. The old Sp-gated bit
  (Step 19) is the crude ancestor of this rule and can be reused as the
  descent bookkeeping.
- **Expected behaviour to state in advance:** a steady bowl-shaped face
  with the centre at a stand-off where its flux equals the ring's; ROP set
  by the ring; hole Ø slightly above burner Ø; face-averaged flux
  ≈ 0.5 MW/m².
- **Acceptance (this is what "match ROP" means):** with (h₀, T_gas,0)
  inside the brackets and the jet shapes from literature, ROP 1.3–1.6 m/h

  ROP within 5 % between 2 and 1 mm. Report the sensitivity of ROP to h₀,
  T_gas,0 and burner Ø (%/unit). Wall temperature is NOT a target (nothing

## 6. What not to do

- No delivery-efficiency knob, and no ROP target in any packet before D2.
- No flake-size closure on the depth scan; no Rossi PSD work.
- No P4 debris shielding, no subcritical growth.
- No sub-cell temperature reconstruction from the 2 mm cell average for the
  Robin flux (gives ~3000 K; see S1 "implied T_s" column). The closure is the
  pinned surface, Packet C.
- Do not change removal cadence (two firings per cell) or the E1 path.
- MMW anchor (Woskov/Oglesby known-power case) waits until D2 is done, per
  the user's decision; but keep §0.5 in ROADMAP as the known MMW gap.

## 7. Open risks, stated now

- D2 is still its own step (spatial (h, T_gas) profile keyed to burner
  position and local stand-off, plus the descent rule), but smaller than the
  withdrawn wall-face version. Do not fold into D1.
- The jet correlations are for free jets on flat plates; inside a hole the
  spent gas recirculates. Treat the radial/axial shapes as literature
  defaults with one stated sensitivity each, not as tuned knobs.
- If the only (h₀, T_gas,0) that fits sits outside the brackets, the honest
  outcome is "Meier constrains the face-averaged flux to ≈ 0.5 MW/m² and
  the stagnation flux to ≈ … given the jet shape", not a fitted ROP.
- The pinned closure on the outer ring: ring cells spall intermittently, so
  the fall-back to face form (Packet C) must work there; test on the 2D run.
- 2D slab vs 3D: the face-area ratio is the whole effect, so no 2D ROP is
  quotable; 2D is for machinery only.
- Data tolerance is ±20 % at best (58 vs 50 cm, Ø 93 vs 85 mm in Meier's own
  numbers). Do not promise tighter.
- Burner/foot diameters are not in the thesis text; without the drawings the
  hole-Ø check is "≥ 70–80 mm", which is weak.
