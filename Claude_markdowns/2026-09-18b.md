## 2. Diagnosis

### 2.1 Time step is clean
R7b rerun at 16 ms and 8 ms (another session's `dt_cap.py`, new binary): burner ROP
1.483 / — / 1.486 m/h over 150–300 s against 4 ms; 0–50 s blocks 1.255 / 1.259 / 1.247.
T_rec and gas reach identical. The 4 ms cap can be relaxed for cost, but it is not the mesh
problem.

### 2.2 The gap opens while the gas is identical
Burner ROP by 50 s block, 1 mm / 2 mm (m/h):
1.31/1.25 (+5 %), 1.76/1.56 (+13 %), 1.89/1.50 (+26 %), 2.00/1.46 (+37 %), 2.13/1.49 (+43 %),
2.22/1.51 (+47 %).
Through 150 s: ring gas temperatures agree to 2 K (T_in at 30/40 mm at 100 s: 1673/1629 vs
1674/1631 K), firing temperature 821 K in both, T_rec *lower* at 1 mm (1534 vs 1546 K at 150 s),
reach 168–169 mm in both until the nozzle plane crosses the surface. **Bin radii, s_c and the
pinned-share criterion are not the seed.**

### 2.3 The seed: the pad rim under the 0.9-quantile feet rule + whole-cell clearance
Reconstructing `FeetDescent` (pads, q = 0.9, nearest rank, mean of 3 sectors) from the removal
log reproduces `foot_z` to 0.3 mm at every checked time, so the following is exact:
- The pad height H is **always the 38–40 mm band** (86–100 % of that band sits at H; no
  column of any inner band does). All regime-4 (mechanical) events are in that band.
- Rim recession = spall + clip. 2 mm: 14 + 11 = 25 mm/min, constant over 300 s.
  1 mm: 12→17 spall + 14→19 clip = 26→37 mm/min.
- Pad shape: 2 mm is a cone (28→40 mm: 37→25 mm/min, persists; the interior slows to 32 as its
  stand-off grows under the n = 1 law). 1 mm flattens to 37 mm/min everywhere by 300 s.
- Mechanism: the clip (`foot_body_clearance = 1`, Removal.H cut to `foot_clear_k`) removes a
  whole cell that was part-way to firing; the fresh top cell restarts from its preheat. The
  thermal layer under the face is ≈ κ/v ≈ 3 mm, so a 2 mm clip discards most of it and a 1 mm
  clip a third. The rim therefore restarts warmer at 1 mm: +5 % at 0–50 s, +11 % at 50–100 s
  with identical gas. Earlier mesh checks (S1, D2b trimmed) measured the ring-average front;
  the quantile rule selects the slow tail, which was never tested.

### 2.4 The amplifier: flank exclusion → hotter recirculation
Faster burner → nozzle plane deeper (110 vs 74 mm at 300 s) → columns with face above the
plane get no flux (`s <= 0` in BuildFlameColumns) → the flank outside the pad leaves the march
(`jet_r_reach` 63→40 mm at 1 mm vs 71→53 mm at 2 mm over 150–300 s; 40–60 mm band removal
1.37→0.04 cm³/s vs 1.88→1.22) → less enthalpy extracted (`jet_P_face` 1012 vs 1309 W at
300 s) → hotter exhaust → hotter recirculation (`jet_T_ent_mode = exhaust`: T_rec 1686 vs
1623 K) → hotter, leaner stagnation (T_stag 1796 vs 1745 K; s_c 133 vs 152 mm so φ is larger
and m = ṁ/φ smaller) → faster ring and centre → deeper nozzle. At 300 s the 1 mm run absorbs
less jet power but spends all of it inside r < 40 mm; the 2 mm run spends a third of its power
widening the flank. The 1 mm hole below the funnel is Ø 80 = the burner.
The domain-height finding (+4 % at 0.40 vs 0.70 m) is the same loop with a smaller seed.

### 2.5 Consequences for the D2d reading
- The 2 mm ROP PASS is a property of the 2 mm rim cycle, not of the physics.
- R1's steady window exists because the 2 mm cone regulates the interior (larger s → lower h);
  a converged pad may not have that regulator.
- The T_nozzle inference in §7(d) inherits the mesh error and should not be quoted.
- The mouth/funnel result (§7(b)) is *not* affected by the seed: the funnel is made in the
  first ~120 s while the nozzle is above the surface, with the same flank removal in both
  meshes (40–60 mm band 1.3–1.9 cm³/s). It is a gas-reach problem, see §4.

## 3. Proposed D3 core: three controlled mesh pairs before any physics

All pairs: 2 mm and 1 mm, 0.40 m full domain, same keys as R7b/R7 except the one switch,
stop 300 s, matched window 150–300 s, `score.py --mesh`. Cost: 2 mm ≈ 0.7 h, 1 mm ≈ 4.5 h at
the campaign rate (less with the 09-18a bit-exact speed-up, 1.4×; and dt could go to 7–8 ms at
1 mm per 2.1 since 4 ms was the harness cap, not the overshoot rule).

| pair | switch | isolates | pre-registered expectation |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-a | `surface_patch.foot_body_clearance = 0` (D2b rule) | the seed (clip cycle) | 0–100 s gap shrinks from 5–11 % to ≤ 3 %; rim spall alone sets the burner (slower, ~14–20 mm/min) |
| P-b | `jet_T_ent_mode = fixed`, `jet_T_ent` = 1550 K (both runs' T_rec at 150 s) | the amplifier | gap stays near its 100 s value (≈ 10 %) instead of growing to 43 % |
| P-c | `surface_patch.foot_rule = mean` | slow-tail selection | burner follows the pad mean (37→32 mm/min at 2 mm); mesh gap small; clearance volume rises |

Decision rule (state before running): the mesh problem is *understood* if P-a removes the
early gap and P-b caps the late growth. If P-a does not shrink the early gap, look at the pin
idle rule (`t_cell ∝ dz` in `PinRule`) and the half-cell removal increment on rim columns next.
Report per pair: burner ROP blocks, rim spall/clip split by band (script in this note's
reconstruction: cumulative `h_applied` per column, `k_top` staircase, sector quantile),
`jet_r_reach`, `T_rec`, pad shape (v per 2 mm band).

Do not treat P-a/P-b/P-c as candidate scored configurations. They are diagnostics.

## 4. The physics that removes both problems: the tube wall

Drawings (memory `meier-burner-drawings`, ROADMAP notes): tube OD 80 / ID 56, 50 mm below the
nozzle flange, three slots at the bolt positions, rim = the feet on r 28–40 mm. Two effects the
model lacks, both raised by the user on 09-18:

1. **Shielding.** Rock under the 12 mm rim is not under the jet. In the model it receives
   full h(r) and T_gas and recedes at 25–37 mm/min, and the burner rate is its clip cycle. In
   reality the burner descends when rim rock is undercut from the bore side or eaten through
   the slots: a lateral problem, and naturally a hole a few mm wider than the tube (Meier 85 vs
   80 mm). This is item 4 "pad shielding" in D2d §7(h), ranked last there.
2. **Confinement.** Inside the bore the stagnation zone and wall jet are contained within
   r < 28 mm; the exhaust leaves through three slots. The flat surface outside r = 40 mm sees
   hot gas only in three diluted streaks. That removes the first-120 s flank spalling that
   makes the whole funnel (R1 still 164 mm at the surface; Meier 96 → 85), and removes the
   flank "switch" that amplifies the mesh seed. Free-surface dilution (D2c) is a weak proxy
   for it.

Why it belongs in D3 rather than later: it acts on the same element (the rim) that the mesh
result identifies as the model's weak point, it is the only candidate that addresses ROP
convergence and the mouth together, and it makes the recirculation a physical parameter
(inside a closed bore the jet entrains its own exhaust; the slot flow is what leaves).

Minimal implementation sketch (planner to specify; input-only where possible):
- A collar radius `r_collar` (28 mm bore) and rim `r_tube` (40 mm). For r in [r_collar, r_tube]:
  flux to the rock scaled by the slot open fraction f_slot (3 slots × width / circumference,
  from the drawing) or zero for the rim proper; for r > r_tube while the rim is below the
  original surface: march mass reduced to f_slot·m and free-surface dilution as now. The
  enthalpy that does not reach rock leaves with the slot flow, i.e. it is *not* recirculated:
  T_rec then mixes only the in-bore exhaust.
- Burner descent under shielding: the rim cannot spall from above, so the feet rule needs the
  lateral mechanism (surface-normal kernel, spall.surface_normal, was inert under the pinned
  closure in D2b — check whether it is inert because the rim received top flux; under
  shielding the bore-side wall of the rim is the heated face). Alternatively a stated
  undercut rule with its own unit test. Either way, the rim rate must be shown mesh-converged
  by the same pair method.
- Scoring unchanged (D2c definitions). Pre-register mouth Ø at 25/50 mm and steady ROP from
  the hand model with confinement before running.
