## Finding 1 — OPEN BUG: beam energy leak of 30–60 % in every removal + beam run

Closed the energy budget on the existing plotfiles. Removed cells keep their
enthalpy frozen (`Removal.H:1265`), so E_in vs (stored + flake) closes exactly.

2D harness (`input_2d_dev`, 11.4 kW, r = 42 mm, α = 2000 /m, dz = 2 mm):

| t (s) | E_in (kJ) | stored | in flakes | closure | instantaneous |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8  | 11.1 | 1.8 | 5.9  | 0.70 | — |
| 24 | 33.2 | 2.3 | 19.5 | 0.66 | 0.64 |
| 40 | 55.3 | 2.7 | 31.2 | 0.61 | 0.51 |
| 56 | 77.4 | 3.4 | 40.2 | 0.56 | 0.42 |

3D no-bit prediction: identical closure (0.70 → 0.56). Surface losses < 1 %.
Removed cells all leave at ⟨ΔT⟩ = 519 K (exact thermostat at Sp = 1).

**Mechanism (verified to ±0.02 against plotfiles):**
- `Removal.H:1233-1240` flags a cell `removed` when `phi` crosses the cell
  **centre** (`phi_old >= 0 && phi_new < 0`) — its lower half is still solid per
  the level set.
- `MMWSpalling.H:1224-1233` hands the next solid cell's `phi` ∈ [dz/2, 3dz/2)
  to `BeamSource` as the optical path → `s_top = phi − dz/2` ∈ (0, dz). The
  slice between the level-set surface and that cell's top face is "deposited" in
  a void cell (Q = 0) → lost. Per-column loss = 1 − exp(−α·s_top), up to 86 %.
- Predicted deposition fraction from `phi` of top solid cells: 0.69, 0.61, 0.55,
  0.49, 0.45, 0.41 — matches the measured interval closure 0.63, 0.64, 0.58,
  0.51, 0.46, 0.42.
- This IS the "unexplained within-run deceleration 3.6 → 2.3 m/h" (Meier README §3c).

**Consequences:**
- Model drills *slower* than it should. Fix ⇒ no-bit Meier ROP rises ~1.6–1.8×
  (2.7 → ~4.5–5 m/h). Gap to Meier becomes ~3×, not 1.9×.
- N1 resolution study is confounded (leak fraction depends on α·dz). Redo after fix.
- Kant / Rossi / Hu unaffected (prescribed_T or no beam). Every `spall.enabled`
  + beam run is affected (Meier, wellbore extension, regime tests).
- Second energy-conservation bug in the same deposition path in 3 months
  (first: pointwise Beer-Lambert sampling, fixed Jun 10). Need a standing
  energy-closure regression.

**Fix candidates:** (a) void a cell only when `phi` passes its bottom face;
(b) measure the beam path from the top face of the first solid cell
(mask-consistent, clamp s_top = 0); (c) partial-cell volume fractions.

**Reproduce:** `Claude_markdowns/scripts/energy_audit.py <outdir> P0 r x0 y0`

## Finding 2 — the "1.9×" is an energy-partition ambiguity, not a spall-physics gap

In the thermostat regime, ROP = q_rock·(1 − f_leak)/(ρ·Cp·ΔT_spall). ΔT_spall is
already pinned by Kant (~520–560 K). So with a fixed-flux BC, **ROP is linear in
the assumed delivered power and nothing else.**

- **P4 debris shielding η is exactly degenerate with delivery efficiency**
  (0.30·η ≡ 0.17, or post-fix ≡ ~0.09). Meier cannot calibrate Σ₀ or confirm
  shielding exists. The ACTIVE_STEP acceptance ("ROP moves toward 1.5 m/h with
  a plausible Σ₀") passes for any power-removing mechanism. → Do not build P4
  against Meier.
- **The "20–50 % plausible efficiency" band is unsourced and is the weak
  link.** Rock-side balance: flakes at ~820 K carry 2.47 cm³/s × 1.2 J/mm³ ≈
  3 kW; the ~30 mm conductive shell over 1383 s absorbs ≤ ~2–3 kW. Rock can take
  ~13–15 % of 38 kW, ~7–8 % leaves as cuttings. Meier's own Ch. 7 Robin BC
  (h = 10 kW/m²K, T_ref = 1000 K, 50 mm spot) gives ~4 kW ≈ 10 %. Model's
  post-fix implied ~9 % is *consistent* with the experiment's own energy balance.

Other angles noted:
- Use the Robin BC for flames (`surface_patch.mode = convective_flame` exists but
  is pinned to the fixed top face) — makes flux self-consistent with the
  thermostat; two literature knobs (h, T_ref) instead of one free efficiency.
- 85 mm hole vs 50 mm footprint is exhaust-gas wall heating; the 42 mm uniform
  disk smears that in by hand.
- Thermostat fires on the 2 mm cell average; physical boundary layer κ/v ≈ 0.9 mm.
  True surface hotter, flake thinner than a cell — root of N1 non-monotonicity
  and the Rossi 775 µm floor.
- For MMW none of this ambiguity exists (gyrotron power + reflection known).

## Finding 3 — we are not (yet) a grain-scale fracture model

What is actually on in every validated spall/drilling case:

| Ingredient | Report claims | Validated path |
|---|---|---|
| Stress | 3D elastic solve / closed form | `local_thermoelastic` only, `el.type = disable`, MLMG shelved |
| Microstructure | Voronoi grains | Meier & Rossi `nphases = 1`; wellbore ext. 3 phases but still per-cell closed form |
| GB cohesion | CZM | diagnostics only, traction feedback deferred since Step 9 |
| Damage | Drucker–Prager | `damage.enabled = 0` (parser conflict with `sp_weibull`) |
| Fracture | grain-scale | per-column 1-D K_I(z) scan, no lateral mechanical coupling |
| Mesh | — | dx = 2 mm = grain size |

Honest description: **column-wise thermoelastic LEFM ablation model with Weibull
flaw statistics and a level-set moving boundary** — a numerical Kant /
Rauenzahn–Tester model with a proper receding surface. Useful and publishable;
not grain-scale. Competitor class is Liu 2024 / Rauenzahn–Tester with a better
boundary, not Walsh GEODYN / Vogler MOOSE. Literature characterisations in the
report are fair; our own "cannot do" column undersells (no lateral interaction,
no stress concentration, no crack propagation).

Vs. `main.tex` objectives: (1) two-mode removal implemented, vapor regime never
reached (melt unreachable without α(T)); (2) granite/basalt/limestone — not
started; (3) onset-time scaling law — not done; (4) depth/EOS regime transition —
EOS dropped, only Kant pressure sweep. Only MMW validation in the ladder is
Zhang/Oglesby (thermal only). ~6 months went to a flame-jet case.
