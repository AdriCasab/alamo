## §9 The 1.5 mm flake: where it came from, and a better approach than scanning it (21:00)

Agree with the other agent's facts: `flake_h_max` = 1.5 mm is 0.75 of a 2 mm cell, cited to a
Wilkinson band that tops out at 1 mm, never derived; it binds on 99.9 % of lateral firings and ~60 %
of removed volume (wall band), never on the floor interior. `c_f` = 0.4 is declared empirical
(Wilkinson/Rauenzahn at ℓ = 0.8–1.6 mm). One correction: the floor flake is **not**
resolution-limited and not capped — floor ℓ reads 1.3–1.4 mm, and α/v at Meier's 1.5–2.4 m/h is
1.0–1.7 mm (α = 6.9e-7 m²/s), so the two-cell read is physical there; the thickness question is a
wall question (ℓ 8–12 mm because the wall barely recedes).

**What the Meier reference itself says the thickness should be**
(`meier-2017-pilot-660kg-validation-reference.md:235`): a continuum model "can predict avg spall
thickness from **λ = α/(m v) ≈ 50–100 µm at ROP = 1.5 m/h** (Walsh-2014 length scale)". `m` is the
Weibull modulus — **20 in our own input** (`input_drilling:124`). α/v at 1.5 m/h = 1.66 mm ⇒ λ = **83 µm**,
inside Meier's measured band (average < 100 µm, p90 < 300 µm — fragments, so a lower bound on flake
thickness, but the right order). The physics: with σ(z) = σ_s e^{−z/ℓ} and Weibull failure probability
∝ ∫(σ/σ₀)^m dz, the depth over which failure is probable is ℓ/m — the weakest-link depth. So the flake
thickness fraction is **c_f = 1/m ≈ 0.05, not 0.4**, and it is set by a parameter the model already
carries, not by a new constant.

**Better approach (no cap, no scan):**
1. **h_f = ℓ/m.** Floor: 83 µm. Wall (ℓ 5–12 mm): 0.25–0.6 mm — below the old cap everywhere, so
   the cap becomes unnecessary rather than retired. Sub-cell recession makes 80 µm flakes representable
   (≈ 25 firings per cell; bookkeeping, not cost). P1g-1/2 showed recession over 60 s is nearly
   independent of per-event thickness, so the rate does not ride on this number; shape, roughness
   and the flake-enthalpy fraction do.
2. **Bound ℓ by the flux, not the mesh.** For an exponential skin the surface flux is q = kΔT/ℓ, so
   **ℓ_q = k(T_s − T0)/q_in** — both already computed at every face and column, no neighbour cell, same
   formula floor and wall, finite wherever the face is heated. Floor at q ≈ 1 MW/m²: 0.7 mm; wall at
   h = 169, T_g 1600: ≈ 5 mm (matches the measured lateral 5 mm). Use it as the ℓ for h_f, or at least
   as the ceiling the two-cell read may not exceed; the flat-column "3 km" vanishes.
3. **Offline first, zero compute:** from Leg B's logs compute ℓ_q per firing and ℓ_q/20; expect ~80 µm
   on the floor and 0.3–0.6 mm on the wall; compare the floor figure to Meier's PSD as a consistency
   check, not a target. Then the mesh test — which with thin flakes tests the pinned-surface,
   energy-limited limit, the classical thermal-spallation steady state.

**The uncomfortable implication, which is the user's decision.** Under the **differential** form the
drive is (1 − e^{−h_f/ℓ})·ΔT_s = (1 − e^{−1/m}) ΔT_s ≈ **5 %** of the superheat with Weibull-thin
flakes; keeping the threshold at ~756 K would need a0 ≈ 45× larger (≈ 3.6 mm) — absurd. The
differential form needs thick flakes (h_f ~ ℓ) to have any drive, which is why P1 adopted c_f = 0.4
and then had to cancel it with the 80 µm refit. The **absolute** form — surface stress at the flaw,
thickness from the weakest-link depth — is the classical Rauenzahn/Walsh model, consistent with thin
flakes, and is what the control already runs (8 µm, 756 K). ⇒ Rather than re-deriving a0 for the
differential form (P1g-4), reconsider whether the differential form is the right physics at all. The
P1 freeze (soaked columns inert under differential) was the form's own behaviour; with absolute +
thin flakes, soaked columns fire at T_s like everything else, and whether a soaked wall should stop
spalling becomes a question for the flake-stability physics (buckling of a thin flake over a flaw —
h vs a — Rauenzahn), not for the drive fraction.

Order: offline ℓ_q / ℓ/m check (an hour) → user decision on the form → mesh test with the chosen
thickness law → P1g-3 is then "h_f = ℓ/m with ℓ_q", not a scan of 1.0/1.5/2.0.

### §9a Correction to §9 (4 Oct, 00:40) — the Weibull thickness is 2.7·ℓ/m, not ℓ/m; the existing 0.4 is the m ≈ 7 end of the literature, not an outlier

Read Rauenzahn (OCR §4.4.4, Eq. 4.96–4.100) and Walsh 2014 Eq. (1)–(5) directly. The spall
thickness distribution is G(w) = 1 − exp[−0.693(1 − e^{−w}(1 + w + w²/2))] with **w = m·x/ℓ**
(cone-shaped stressed volume, constant aspect ratio). Its median is **w = 2.67**, so the median flake is
**h = 2.67·ℓ/m**: m = 20 → 0.13ℓ; m = 13 → 0.21ℓ; m = 8 → 0.33ℓ. Rauenzahn's own statement — "the
backside of a newly formed spall is at ~80 % of the surface rise for m = 10–20" — is x = 0.22ℓ; Wilkinson's
representative spall is 0.4 mm at 6e-4 m/s and α = 1e-6, i.e. x/ℓ = 0.24, a 21 % drop across the spall.
⇒ §9's "c_f = 1/m ≈ 0.05" used the **scale** λ = α/(mv), which the Meier reference quotes (50–100 µm),
not the **median** (2.67λ ≈ 130–270 µm at m = 20 — against Meier's p90 < 300 µm, consistent). The
source's c_f = 0.4 corresponds to m ≈ 7, at the m = 8 (Barre, Wilkinson's spallation fit) end; the
literature's central value is ≈ 0.22 (m ≈ 12). **Withdrawn from §9:** "the differential form is
incompatible with thin flakes" — with c_f = 0.13–0.33 the drive fraction 1 − e^{−c_f} is 0.12–0.28, not
5 %; the a0 ⇄ drive-fraction coupling stands exactly as P1 found it, and the choice of form remains the
user's physics decision on its own merits (does a soaked slab spall), not a consequence of thickness.
**Kept from §9:** the cap is undived and wall-only; the flux length ℓ_q = k(T_s − T0)/q_in as the
mesh-free bound; the thickness is a by-product of the same Weibull integral that sets the spall
temperature, so m is one parameter for both.

## §10 Flake thickness — external references, the other agent's plan, and what else to test (4 Oct, 00:50)

**On the plan.** Right that Rossi 2018 is the only on-file measurement of a depth, and right that
Step 16b failed for mesh reasons (dz = 1.56 mm against a 150 µm feature) and that a one-length
prediction needs no resolving. But Rossi measures **thermal micro-crack density under a 5 K/s ramp
with no spallation and no receding surface**, GB/IG ratio 5–6 — grain-scale expansion-mismatch
cracking (quartz α–β at 573 °C in range), a population our single-phase, grain-free continuum has no
mechanism for. The ramp form of the law exists (Rauenzahn Eq. 1.5–1.7 with the ierfc field; the
gradient length for a linear ramp is 0.886·√(αt), 7–8 mm at 90–130 s ⇒ /m = 0.4–0.6 mm, near Rossi's
520 µm falloff, not its 100–200 µm peak) — so Rossi would most likely "refute" the law for a reason

it drive the thickness. Agree: m differs between inputs (15 vs 20) and both are unsourced; the GB/IG
row is unscorable on grain-free rock.

**The references that actually measure spall thickness with a receding surface are on disk and were
not in the plan:**
1. **Rauenzahn 1986** (MIT, flame-jet on Barre granite): §4.4.4 gives the thickness distribution
   G(w), w = m·u·x/α, median 2.67; Ch. 4 has measured spall size distributions vs drilling rate.

3. **Walsh 2014** Fig. 4: measured average spall thickness vs spall size (aspect ratio 6:1 → 1:1

4. **Meier PSD**: average < 100 µm, p90 < 300 µm — fragments, a lower bound; the model's median at m = 20

5. **Kant/von Rohr 21 onset legs (Grimsel)**: onset temperature vs flux is exactly what the Weibull
