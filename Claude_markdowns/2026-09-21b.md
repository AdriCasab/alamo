# Design plan (2026-09-21): a continuous ablation front for the MMWSpalling surface

Written for the planner who turns this into the next packet(s). Decisions taken with the user
today: **scope = recession law only** (gas closures, onset criterion and flux-on-horizontal-area
unchanged); **form = slope-aware (normal) continuous front**; **onset = the K_I/Weibull criterion,
as a one-time switch per column**. Everything below follows from those three.

## 0. What we are trying to fix, and what we are not

The last four steps showed that the surface model — a per-column *event* (fire when the cell
meets the criterion, remove dz/2 − a_f) under a flux closure that imposes h(T_gas − T_pin) —
has hard thresholds whose outcome depends on cell size wherever the surface meets a lateral
hot/cold edge: the whole-cell clearance clip (D2e), the idle clock t_cell ∝ dz (D2h), and the
binary fire/no-fire of edge columns sitting a few K under T_fire (D2i). Descent is also
quantised (dz/3 steps at 2 mm, D2f), which is why 50 s blocks were noisy.

**The front fixes the event artefacts:** no half-cell increments, no clocks, no binary stall of
a marginal column (it recedes slowly instead), no descent quantisation, and the surface really
sits at the firing temperature instead of being emulated by a pin.

**The front does not fix under-resolved lateral conduction.** The D2i edge margin was set by
lateral loss into cold rock at k ΔT/dx; a continuous front recedes at the rate the net heat
allows, but if that net heat is mesh-dependent because the lateral gradient is unresolved at
1–2 mm (thermal length κ/v ≈ 3 mm), the rate is still mesh-dependent, only smoothly so. And it
does nothing to the 80° cone (flux per horizontal area) or the cold wall above the nozzle
plane (s ≤ 0). Those are the next packets, deliberately separated. The acceptance tests below
are designed to tell these apart *before* the Meier pair is run.

## 1. Formulation

Per column (i, j), host-side state like `pin_T` today:
- `abl_on[col]` ∈ {0, 1}: 0 until the first step the existing K_I ≥ K_Ic scan fires for the
  column; then 1 forever (no reversion; a column whose gas goes cold simply stops receding).
- `T_abl[col]`: the top-cell temperature at that first firing (today's `rev_T_top` → `pin_T`),
  i.e. the temperature at which the column's own flaw draw meets K_Ic. Fixed thereafter.
  With V₀ = V_cell and m = 20 it is 821 K ± 7 K across columns, as now.

**Surface condition for an ablating column.** The face is at `T_abl`. The gas flux into the
face is the existing pinned expression, q_in = h(T_gas − T_abl) − losses(T_abl), unchanged and
still per horizontal cell area (decision: scope). The pinned closure's branch logic
(`SurfaceCellFlux`: face Newton first, T_s = min(T_f, T_pin), `qneg` when the gas is colder)
stays as it is; `pinned_idle_cycles` is irrelevant in this mode (no clock; the state rule
remains). In other words the pin becomes what it was always emulating: a Dirichlet-like
surface at T_fire.

**Recession by sub-cell isotherm (the enthalpy-method equivalent, zero latent heat).** After
the thermal advance of a step, for every ablating column:
1. Reconstruct T along the column near the face from the top live cell (fill f = phi_top/dz,
   temperature T_0) and the cell below (T_1) — linear, using cell-centre positions that account
   for the partial fill (the top centre sits at f·dz/2 below the face).
2. Find the depth z_iso ≥ 0 below the current face at which T = T_abl (0 if T_0 ≤ T_abl).
3. Remove the thickness δh = z_iso (vertical, capped at δh_max = dz/2 per step; a cap hit is
   logged, not silently accepted). Removal goes through the existing PASS 3 column phi shift
   (`phi −= δh`, cells flip when phi < 0), so masks, `removed`, `regime`, `RoP`, plotfiles and
   the E1 beam path all keep working. New regime code (5, "ablation") in `regime` / `regime_field`.
4. Energy: the removed slab leaves with its enthalpy (T_abl plus whatever superheat was above
   the isotherm); the ledger's removal term counts it exactly as today's events do. Nothing new
   is created or lost; the ledger must still close to round-off.

In the moving steady state this gives v = q_net/(ρ c_p (T_abl − T_∞)) with the surface at
T_abl — the S1 result, now without events. On a column losing heat sideways, v shrinks
continuously with the net heat instead of dropping to zero at a threshold.

**Slope-aware (normal) variant — the decided form.** `ColumnSurfaceCos` (Step 20) already gives
cos θ per column from the gathered column tops, decomposition-independent. With it:
- the isotherm is sought along the normal: depth along the column s maps to normal depth
  s·cos θ, so the reconstruction uses the normal gradient (∂T/∂n ≈ cos θ ∂T/∂z); the removed
  normal thickness δh_n converts to the vertical column shift δh = δh_n / cos θ (this is the
  `icth` factor the Step 20 kernel applies today);
- the cap becomes δh ≤ dz/2 on the vertical shift (so steep walls cannot jump);
- cos θ is clamped as in Step 20 (`SP_SN_COS_MIN`).
With the gas flux still per horizontal area, a steady slope receives q cos θ per unit of true
face, so δh_n = q cos θ/(ρc_pΔT) and the vertical rate is q/(ρc_pΔT): **the same vertical
recession as the column front**. What the normal form adds is regularisation: needles and
steps see a thinner normal skin and heal, and the coherence cap is not needed (it stays off).
Implementation milestone order: cos θ = 1 first (identical to the vertical front), then
enable the slope factor — the second is a one-line switch once the first is verified.

**What is retired in this mode:** the dz/2 − a_f increment, `spall_fires_col` as a removal
driver (it remains the onset test), the idle clock, the coherence cap, the K_I bisection for
h_col (the scan still runs to *decide onset*; its depth output is ignored). `removal_events.csv`
would receive a row per ablating column per step (millions); replace with (a) a per-column
cumulative-recession field in the plotfile and (b) a thermo line: number of ablating columns,
mean/max δh per step, cap hits, rate-of-onset. `rim.py`-style analyses read the cumulative
field instead of events.

## 2. Where it goes in the code (pointers, not a diff)

- `src/Integrator/MMWSpalling/Removal.H`: PASS 2 decides per-column `h_chosen_col` /
  `regime_col`. Add: if `spall.removal = isotherm` and `abl_on[idx]`, set
  `h_chosen_col = δh` (from the isotherm step above) and `regime_col = 5`, skipping the
  dz/2 − a_f branch; if `!abl_on` and the K_I scan fires this step, set `abl_on = 1`,
  `T_abl = rev_T_top`, and apply this step's δh (which may be 0). PASS 3 unchanged.
- `src/Integrator/MMWSpalling.H`: parse `spall.removal` (`events` default → byte-identical
  everywhere; `isotherm`); registration of the cumulative-recession field and the thermo
  counters; `SurfaceCellFlux` untouched; `PinRule`: under `isotherm`, return `T_abl` (never
  idle). Reuse `ColumnSurfaceCos` for cos θ. Host vectors (`abl_on`, `T_abl`) are not
  checkpointed — same caveat as `pin_T`; document it.
- Time step: the front moves ≤ dz/2 per step by the cap; the existing overshoot rule
  (q dt/(ρ c_p dz) ≤ 5–6 K) already keeps δh ≪ dz per step at the Meier fluxes. Verify with the
  dt ladder in test (a).
- Partial top cell with small f: the isotherm reconstruction must not divide by f; when
  f < 0.1 use the top two cells' combined enthalpy for T_0 (the S1 phi-window logic exists
  for the beam path; do not reuse `floor(phi/dz)`, see the E1 note at MMWSpalling.H ~2450).

## 3. Acceptance tests, in the order they should be run

All default-off: the 2639-file reference set must be byte-identical with `spall.removal`
unset. Then:

**(a) 1-D ablation column (mirrors `unit/stefan` and S1).** One column, uniform imposed flux
q (Robin with large h, or beam), Neumann-0 sides, `spall.removal = isotherm`, onset by K_I as
in S1. Expected after the transient: v = q/(ρc_p(T_abl − T_0)) to < 2 % at dz = 4, 2, 1,
0.5 mm; the surface temperature = T_abl ± 2 K at every dz; ledger round-off. Pre-register the
convergence order (expect first order in dz from the linear isotherm). Cost: seconds.

**(b) Hot-disk edge test (new; the arbiter).** Flat block, no jet, no feet: a Robin patch of
radius 30 mm at a fixed T_gas (1600 K) and h (700 W/m²K) on a cold surface, ε 0.8, h_c 10,
0.10 × 0.10 × 0.15 m quarter domain, dx = dz = 4, 2, 1 mm. Measure at 100, 200, 300 s: centre
recession rate, the radius at which the recession rate has fallen to half the centre value
(the edge-zone width), and the fraction of patch columns receding at < 10 % of the centre
rate. **Run it first with today's event model** to reproduce D2i's edge stall cheaply
(minutes per run; this is the mechanism in isolation), then with the isotherm front, then with
the slope factor on. Pre-register: centre rate converged at all dx in both models; edge-zone
width converges with the front (changes by < 20 % from 2 → 1 mm) and does not with events. If
the edge width still shifts by more than that with the front, the remaining sensitivity is
under-resolved lateral conduction, and the Meier pair will show the same — say so before
running it, and decide whether the answer is finer cells near the edge (AMR is available in
the code; the Meier configuration has been single-level) or the cone physics.

**(c) The Meier pair.** D2i keys plus `spall.removal = isotherm` (slope factor on), 2 mm / 1 mm
to 450 s, criterion as D2i (matched window and 100 s blocks within 5 %), plotfiles every 50 s.
Pre-register from the hand model and D2i: burner rate 150–350 s within 5 % of D2i's 1.50 m/h
(the physics is unchanged; only the discretisation of removal changed); no silent pad columns
at either mesh; the 80° cone and the mouth unchanged (they are gas-side); the residual mesh
gap at 350–450 s no larger than test (b) predicts for the edge. Any of these failing is
information about the front, not about Meier.

**(d) Regression of the accepted physics with the front on:** S1 fixed-flame column (rate and
T_fire), `robin_pinned`, `sp_lefm` onset temperature unchanged (onset is untouched), Kant and
Hu onset cases unchanged (they never fire or fire once).

## 4. Pre-registered expectations, stated now

1. (a) converges; the front is energy-exact.
2. (b) with events reproduces an edge stall whose width scales with dx; with the front the
   edge becomes a smooth rate profile. Whether its width converges at 1–2 mm is the open
   question; my expectation is *partial* convergence (the thermal length is 3 mm), which would
   make the Meier long-time rate converge to within ~5–8 % rather than 2 %.
3. (c) 150–350 s reproduces D2i within 5 %; the late gap shrinks from −16/−36 % to whatever (b)
   predicts. The hole shape is unchanged.
4. The support rule's descent becomes smooth (no dz/3 steps), so 50 s blocks become usable.

## 5. What this does not do, so nobody expects it

No change to: h(r, s), the march, recirculation, free-surface dilution, the feet rule, flux
per horizontal area, the s ≤ 0 wall rule, T_nozzle. The cone, the funnel and the cold wall are
the following packets (true-area flux first, then wall/exhaust heating and the wellhead
confinement), each with the pair method and test (b)-style isolation. The nozzle-temperature
sweep is redone only once the front is accepted.

## 6. Packet split and cost

- **D2j-a (code + tests a, b, d):** one implementation packet. Source: Removal.H PASS 2 branch,
  parse + registration in MMWSpalling.H, thermo counters, a `unit/ablation_column` test and a
  `unit/hot_disk` test (both fast). Reference-set identity with the key off. Estimated at the
  size of D2c.
- **D2j-b (test c):** the Meier pair, ≈ 2.5 h at 1 mm plus 20 min at 2 mm, plus the analysis
  reusing d2g/d2i `analyze.py` with the cumulative-recession field in place of events.
- Do not merge these; (b)'s result decides whether (c) is worth running as designed.

## 7. Open points for the planner to settle in the packet text

- The isotherm reconstruction stencil (two cells, linear) vs a three-cell quadratic: start
  linear, note the order.
- The per-step cap (dz/2) and what to do on a cap hit (log; never abort).
- Whether `T_abl` is re-evaluated when K_Ic(T) would change with damage/melt: no, in this
  packet.
- The diagnostics contract for downstream scripts (`rim.py` reads events today): provide the
  cumulative field and a converter, or keep a decimated event log (one row per column per
  plot interval). The former is cleaner.
- Naming: `spall.removal = events | isotherm`; `spall.isotherm_cap` (default 0.5 dz);
  `spall.isotherm_normal = 0 | 1` (default 1 under the decision, 0 for the milestone check).

## 8. Amendments after the planner's review (2026-09-21, reviewer agrees)

1. **Slope-aware variant withdrawn.** §1's gradient relation was inverted: for T = T(n) near a
   face inclined at θ, a vertical step d is a normal step d·cos θ, so ∂T/∂n = (∂T/∂z)/cos θ.
   Implemented as written it would remove z_iso/cos²θ (2× at 45°, 4× at 60°). Done correctly it
   is an identity: the vertical distance to the isotherm along each column *is* the shift that
   puts the face set on the isosurface, whatever the slope. The normal form is a no-op for an
   isotherm front; Step 20's kernel mattered for the event increment, which this packet
   retires; needles heal through lateral conduction. `spall.isotherm_normal` is dropped from
   the design. The slope correction that matters is in the flux (true vs horizontal area) and
   stays deferred by decision.
2. **Test (b) runs first, with today's model, as its own packet (D2j-0), before any code.**
   Zero source changes; minutes per run. It decides whether the redesign is aimed at the right
   target. Add dx = 0.5 mm (three meshes give an order) on a reduced domain
   (0.06 × 0.06 × 0.08 m), and a smoothly tapered h(r) beside the sharp patch to separate BC
   discontinuity from mesh. Use the pinned closure with the idle clock off, as D2i.
3. **Cap is a gate quantity:** pre-register max(δh/dz) and cap hits per block; a cap that binds
   at one mesh and not the other voids the run.
4. **(c) interpretation pre-registered:** a fail consistent with (b)'s measured edge sensitivity
   reads "lateral resolution", answered by surface-refined AMR or true-area flux, never by
   re-tuning. Note: `robin_form = pinned`, `jet_closure = enthalpy` and `nozzle_descent = feet`
   abort above level 0 today, so surface AMR is a real packet, not a switch.
5. Linear stencil closed (error scales with the per-step increment, ~0.1 % in rate).
6. Conditional thermo/field registration behind the key (2639-file identity on headers).
7. Hard abort on checkpoint restart under `isotherm` (host state unsaved).
8. Diagnostics parity: the events→cumulative converter must reproduce D2i's rim numbers before
   (c). If (c)'s residual exceeds (b)'s prediction, the difference is the stand-off loop; the
   check is the D2h open-loop pair (collision radius 0.028, fixed in run.py, never rerun).
9. Packet split: D2j-0 (test b, no code) → D2j-a (vertical front, test a, identity,
   regressions) → D2j-b (b with the front) → D2j-c (Meier pair).
