# 2026-10-06a — A reduced wall-jet model for the hole-wall heat-transfer coefficient (reviewer, 15:30)

Purpose: replace the hand-set `side_face_h = 169.3` and the P2-0 duct estimate (≈50 W/m²K) with a
coefficient derived from the jet's own momentum, with every constant sourced and every step checkable.
No CFD. Companion to 2026-10-06 §8.

## 1. Flow picture, and why each element is there

1. **Free jet, nozzle → floor.** Momentum J = 19.15 N (D2c `nozzle.py`, isentropic Laval state) is the
   invariant. In hot hole gas (≈1550 K, ρ ≈ 0.24 kg/m³) the momentum-equivalent diameter is
   D_e = √(4J/(πρu_e²)) = **7.9 mm** with u_e = 1277 m/s, so the product U_o·d_o that scales every wall-jet
   law below is **u_e·D_e = 10.1 m²/s**. The jet entrains hole gas; the model's own closure gives
   ṁ_jet/ṁ_e = 1/φ = 2.97 at impingement (ANNULUS_H (a1), three ways). A free jet would entrain more
   (Ricou–Spalding 0.32·x/D_e ⇒ 7–9× at x/D_e = 22–27); the model's cap is a confinement choice already
   accepted in D2a2/D2c and is not revisited here. Either way **the 2ṁ_e of excess mass at the floor came
   from inside the hole and goes back into the jet** — that is the recirculation, and it is a consequence
   of entrainment, not a hypothesis (Fitzgerald & Garimella 1998 mapped the toroidal cell for confined
   impinging jets).
2. **Impingement region** r < ~0.22 H (Beltaos & Rajaratnam 1974): pressure-driven turning, the radial
   velocity rises from 0 to its maximum. At the model's standoff (H ≈ 200 mm) the hole wall at
   R = 43–51 mm sits at r/H = 0.21–0.26 — **right at the edge of the impingement zone**; at Meier's
   H ≤ 100 mm it is at r/H ≥ 0.43, well inside the developed wall jet. This is the geometric reason
   the model's wall and Meier's wall are not the same flow.
3. **Radial wall jet** along the floor, r > 0.22 H. Self-similar; laws in §2.
4. **The corner**: the radial wall jet meets the hole wall and turns 90° upward. Some momentum is lost in
   the corner eddy; parameterised by κ_c (§2e).
5. **Rising plane wall jet** on the hole wall, confined by the shield (Ø67) on its outer side. It grows at
   the plane-wall-jet rate until its thickness equals the gap, at z_fill; above that the annulus is full
   and a duct description takes over. z_fill = **177 mm** at Ø102 (gap 17.5 mm) and **71 mm** at Ø85
   (gap 9 mm). The nozzle plane sits at H above the floor; faces above it receive zero in every current
   run (`side_face_factor_above = 0`), so the live wall is 0 < z < H.
6. **Return flow** down the shield side closes the cell; the net 1ṁ_e leaves up the annulus above the nozzle.

## 2. The model

Gas at the wall-jet temperature T_g ≈ 1550 K: ρ = 0.240 kg/m³, μ (Sutherland) = 5.4e-5 Pa·s,
ν = 2.2e-4 m²/s, k = 0.100 W/mK (Incropera A.4 air; products +10–15 %), c_p = 1250 J/kgK (model), Pr = 0.67.

**(a) Radial wall-jet maximum velocity** (Poreh, Tsuei & Cermak 1967; Beltaos & Rajaratnam 1974; the
Colebrook-for-radial-wall-jets re-analysis, OSTI 2587227, Eq. 24 and Fig. 3):
  v_m(r) = h_r · U_o d_o / r,  h_r = 1.0–1.1 (normal impingement; Beltaos Eq. 38 gives 1.1(1+cos φ)/sin φ).
  Measured exponent −1.1 to −1.12 over r/d = 10–60 (Poreh); the 1/r form with h_r = 1.05 is used.
  With U_o d_o → u_e D_e: **v_m(R) = 207 m/s at R = 51 mm, 249 m/s at R = 42.5 mm**, independent of H
  to first order (Poreh's (r/H)^−0.1 is folded into the uncertainty).
**(b) Wall-jet thickness** (half-velocity): δ_r = β_r r, β_r = 0.077 (Beltaos) – 0.098(r/H)^−0.1 (Poreh) –
  0.09 ± 0.005 (Launder & Rodi); β_r = 0.09 used ⇒ **δ_R = 4.6 mm (Ø102), 3.8 mm (Ø85)**. Velocity
  maximum at z/δ_r = 0.237 ± 0.064 (OSTI re-analysis of five data sets).
**(c) Skin friction**, Fanning form τ_w = ½ c_f ρ v_m²:
  - Bradshaw & Gee 1960 / Launder & Rodi 1981: c_f = 0.0315 Re_m^−0.182, Re_m = v_m δ/ν  (central);
  - Poreh et al. 1967: c_f = 0.06 Re_m^−0.3 (r/H)^−0.16  (low);
  - Beltaos 1974 Eq. 26: c_f = 0.098 Re_o^−1/5 with nozzle Re_o = 31,000 ⇒ 0.0124  (high).
  The three span 2.4×; the OSTI paper's point is exactly that this spread is unresolved for radial wall
  jets. Re_δ at the wall radius = v_m δ_R/ν = **4,300** (same at both bores: v ∝ 1/R, δ ∝ R).
**(d) Heat transfer by the Chilton–Colburn analogy**: St = (c_f/2) Pr^−2/3, h = ρ c_p v_m St.
  Justification: in a wall jet the heat flux is carried by the inner (boundary-layer) half of the profile,
  where momentum and heat are transported by the same eddies; Myers, Schauer & Eustis 1963 solved the
  plane-wall-jet thermal layer with a modified Reynolds analogy and matched data. Expect ±20 %.
  ⇒ at Re_δ = 4,300 the three c_f give St = 0.0036 / 0.0042 / 0.0079, i.e. **h = 1.03–1.25–2.4 × v_m**
  [W/m²K per m/s].
**(e) Corner turning**: momentum per unit circumference M' = I₂ ρ v_m² δ (I₂ = ∫f² dη ≈ 0.65 for the
  Glauert profile) is carried round the corner with retention κ_c. No clean source for a radial wall
  jet meeting a concave cylindrical wall; sharp-corner turning flows lose 10–50 % of momentum to the
  corner eddy. κ_c = 0.7 central, 0.5–0.9 band. Because h ∝ v_m^0.82 and v_m ∝ √κ_c, the whole band
  moves h by only **×0.79–1.11** — the corner is not where the uncertainty lives.
**(f) Rising plane wall jet**: momentum conservation I₂ ρ v_m² b = κ_c I₂ ρ v_m,R² δ_R with
  b(z) = δ_R + 0.073 z (Launder & Rodi, dy_½/dx = 0.073 ± 0.002) ⇒
  v_m(z) = v_m,R √(κ_c δ_R / b(z)),  Re_b = v_m b/ν,  c_f(Re_b),  h(z) = ρ c_p v_m(z) (c_f/2) Pr^−2/3.
  Closed form of the shape: h ∝ v_m c_f ∝ b^−½ · (b^½)^−0.182 ⇒ **h(z) = h_c · (δ_R / (δ_R + 0.073 z))^0.59**.
  Caveats, both pushing h UP: the hole wall is concave on the gas side (Görtler-unstable ⇒ more mixing),
  and the counter-flowing return stream on the outer edge shears the jet (faster decay of v_m but higher
  turbulence). A spalled wall is rough (k_s ~ 0.2–1 mm against δ ~ 5 mm ⇒ transitionally rough ⇒ c_f up).
**(g) Filled annulus, z > z_fill**: the floor of h is the duct value with the recirculating mass over the
  annulus — Gnielinski at Re 6,300–7,000 ⇒ **51 W/m²K at Ø102, 110 at Ø85** (P2-0's numbers reproduce).
  It applies only above z_fill, which at the model's bore is above the nozzle plane, i.e. in the dead zone.
**(h) Gas temperature seen by the wall**: the wall jet is the impinging gas after the floor pickup:
  T_corner ≈ T_stag − P_floor/(ṁ_jet c_p) = 1645 − 840/(0.0113·1250) ≈ **1585 K** (quarter numbers),
  relaxing toward T_exhaust = 1514 K at the nozzle plane as the return flow mixes in. The current
  `side_T_eff` = 1514 K is 70 K low at the corner (~10 % of the gas–rock ΔT). P2-0's `cooled` march
  (−156 K up the wall from the exhaust value) moves it the wrong way where the faces are.

## 4. What this says

1. **169.3 is approximately the right MEAN over the live wall at today's bore and standoff (model mean
   ≈150, band 125–280) but the wrong SHAPE**: ~1.7× too low within 25 mm of the floor, ~1.4× too high
   at 150–200 mm. The P2-0 premise "~3× too high" is the duct limit applied where the flow is a wall jet.
2. **The "missing brake" is mostly an artefact of the duct form.** Duct: h ∝ gap^−1.x ⇒ 4× from Ø122 to
   Ø84. Wall jet: v_m ∝ 1/R and Re_δ constant ⇒ **h_corner ∝ 1/R ⇒ only ×0.83 from Ø85 to Ø102**; the
   duct's steep feedback does not exist below z_fill. ⇒ wall heating cannot be the main width-setting
   feedback. (P5 "the funnel flattens 4×" would not survive.)
3. **Where does the width come from, then?** From the floor: v_m ∝ 1/r and T_g(r) falling with
   entrainment put the wall-jet flux below the spall-sustaining level at some radius, and the rock
   beyond is warmed but not spalled — the flame-jet testers' empirical **R_h ≈ 0.45 H** (tester-theses
   memory) is this statement. In the model that radius is pinned by the hand-drawn cos² taper at
   40–56 mm (D2v) plus the flat side coefficient. D2ad already named "the map's radial fall-off across
   28–40 mm" as the binding term; this analysis says the same from the gas side.
4. **Measured today, the flat coefficient removes wall rock in the wrong place.** On `MEIER_CTRL2`,
   lateral voids by height above the floor at the time of removal (t > 100 s, n = 27,270):
   **0 % within 25 mm, 9.6 % at 25–50, 50.3 % at 50–100, 32.6 % at 100–150, 7.5 % at 150–200;
   median 88 mm**. The wall-jet model has its maximum in the band the model never touches and half its
   corner value where the model does most of its cutting. A wall cut 90 mm behind the floor keeps
   widening long after the floor has passed, which is how a +4 mm per-side excess accumulates at every
   depth and why the bore does not stabilise into Meier's cylinder (84 at 300 vs 87 at 100 mm).

## 5. What to implement (P2-0's structure, different content)

`surface_patch.side_face_h = walljet` (default: the scalar), per column c at radius R_c with its
face at height z_c above the local floor (the floor depth under the burner is already tracked for the
feet rule):
  h_c = C_h · h_floor(R_c, s) · (δ_R / (δ_R + 0.073 z_c))^0.59,   δ_R = 0.09 R_c,
  floored at h_duct(R_c) and multiplied by 0 above the nozzle plane as today,
where h_floor(R_c, s) is the model's **existing Martin map evaluated at the wall radius without the
cos² taper** (continuity across the corner — the same h on both sides of the step), and
C_h = κ_c^0.41 ≈ 0.86 (κ_c 0.7). Two new keys only: `side_face_kappa_c` (0.7) and the growth rate (0.073);
no gas-property inputs, no Nu correlation, nothing to tune. The duct value stays as the z > z_fill floor.
T_side: T_corner from (h) at z = 0 relaxing linearly to T_exhaust at the nozzle plane, instead of the
flat exhaust value or the downward `cooled` march.

Pre-registered: (i) wall power roughly unchanged in total (±25 %) but **shifted to z < 50 mm** —
lateral-void median height falls from 88 mm to < 50 mm; (ii) the bore at a given depth **stops
growing** once the floor is ~50 mm past it (today it grows until the nozzle passes, ~200 mm); (iii) the
mouth collar changes little (formed in the first seconds); (iv) net diameter change **small and of
uncertain sign** — this is a shape law, not a width lever; the width lever remains the floor map's
radial fall-off (D2ad) and T_g(r); (v) the mean-h band 125–280 translates into ±30 % on wall power, to be
reported, not tuned.

## 7. Corrections after the other agent's review and my own re-derivation (17:10)

1. **Gas density was inverted.** §2 used ρ = (353/T)·(29/27.5) = 0.240 kg/m³; the molar-mass ratio goes the
   other way (products M ≈ 27.6 are LIGHTER than air): ρ = pM/(R T) = 1.013e5·0.0276/(8.314·1550) =
   **0.217 kg/m³**. Propagation: h ∝ ρ·v·c_f(Re): ρ ×0.90, D_e ∝ ρ^−½ ⇒ v ×1.05, ν ×1.11 ⇒ Re ×0.95 ⇒
   c_f ×1.01 ⇒ **h ×0.96**. Corrected central table (W/m²K): Ø102 — corner 268, z = 0/25/50/100/150/200:
   232/190/164/132/113/100, live-wall mean ≈ 145; Ø85 — corner 322, 278/221/187/148/125/110. Nothing in §4
   changes.
2. **The "never cuts near the floor" reading conflated two things** (the other agent's correction, verified
   against their bore-vs-height table): within 25 mm of the floor the bore radius is 25–37 mm, i.e. inside
   the 40 mm gate, so no face there is in the lateral scope at all. The zero at 0–25 mm is SCOPE, and the
   90 mm median is ~80 mm of geometry (the hole takes that depth to pass r = 40) plus ~10 mm of thermal
   lag. ⇒ §5's expectation (i) "median height falls below 50 mm" is **wrong as written**: the wall-jet law
   changes nothing at the corner unless the gate is also lifted, and lifting it puts the law onto the
   pad-ring risers inside 40 mm, which feed the floor rate (D2w/D2x/D2o-0). That is a decision about rate
   containment, not a side effect. §4 item 4 stands as a statement about WHERE the flat h cuts (50–150 mm
   above the floor, where the wall jet is 1.4–2× weaker than at the corner); it does not stand as "the
   coefficient is why nothing fires at the corner".
3. **Mass carried by the wall-jet layer.** With Glauert's full profile (I₁ ≈ 1.2 over δ) the layer at the
   corner carries ρ v_m δ I₁ 2πR = **0.084 kg/s**, against the model's capped entrainment 0.045 and a free
   jet's 0.10–0.14 (Ricou–Spalding at x/D_e 22–27). The other agent's "0.039 vs 0.045, 15 %" takes the
   mean velocity over the half-width only. The honest statement: the wall jet's mass sits between the
   model's capped jet and a free jet, which is consistent with the model under-entraining; v_m itself is
   set by MOMENTUM (conserved), so the h estimate does not depend on which mass figure is right.
4. **Definition to verify from the original**: the Bradshaw & Gee / Launder & Rodi correlation is taken as
   c_f = τ_w/(½ρU_m²) = 0.0315 Re^−0.182 with Re = U_m y_½/ν (as used by Eriksson, Karlsson & Persson
   1998). If the original defines c_f on ρU_m² (no ½), h doubles and lands on the Beltaos end of the band.
   This single definition is the largest lever in the whole estimate and should be read from Launder &
   Rodi 1981 before any number is carried into a packet.
