# Electromagnetic absorption of granite and its minerals under microwave / millimetre-wave heating, and thermal runaway

Written 2026-11-02. Scope: pre-registered questions Q7 (permittivity, absorption coefficient, penetration depth of granite and its minerals vs temperature at ~2.45 GHz, ~10 GHz and 90–140 GHz) and Q8 (thermal runaway in microwave-heated rock: documented? onset temperature? mechanism? temperatures, heating rates, spalling / cracking / melting observed?).

Labels used throughout: **MEASURED** = a laboratory measurement reported by the cited source; **MODELLED** = a value assumed or fitted inside a simulation by the cited source; **COMPUTED HERE** = a number I derived from a cited measurement with the formula stated; **INFERRED** = an interpretation.

Conventions. Complex relative permittivity ε = ε′ − jε″, tan δ = ε″/ε′. For a plane wave the **field** attenuation constant is
α_f = (2πf/c)·sqrt{ (ε′/2)·[ sqrt(1 + tan²δ) − 1 ] } ≈ (2πf/c)·sqrt(ε′)·tan δ / 2 (low loss),
and the **power** attenuation (the Beer–Lambert coefficient a code uses for volumetric deposition, P(z) = P₀ e^{−α z}) is α = 2α_f ≈ (2πf/c)·sqrt(ε′)·tan δ. Penetration depth d_p = 1/α (power falls to 1/e). "Half-power depth" (Hartlieb's files) HPD = ln2/α; I verified this on the raw file (22 °C, 2986 MHz: ε′ = 5.451, ε″ = 0.03586 → α = 0.961 m⁻¹ → ln2/α = 72.1 cm; the file lists 72.12 cm). **Woskov quotes field attenuation in nepers**: 0.14 Np/cm is α_f; the power coefficient is 0.28 cm⁻¹ = 28 m⁻¹ and the power 1/e depth is 3.6 cm, not the 7.1 cm he quotes for the field.

## Q7. Measured ε′, ε″/tan δ and derived absorption coefficient / penetration depth of granite and its minerals vs temperature at (a) ~2.45 GHz, (b) ~10 GHz, (c) 90–140 GHz

### Takeaway
The only temperature-resolved granite permittivity dataset I could obtain is Hartlieb et al./Microwave Properties North (0.4–3 GHz, RT→1000 °C): tan δ of granite is ~0.007 at room temperature, is flat to ~300 °C, then rises 5.6× by 700 °C (thermally activated loss), dips 750–900 °C (an irreversible mineral transformation), and reaches ~9× the RT value at 1000 °C; **there is no signature of the 573 °C quartz transition in either ε′ or ε″**. Direct mm-wave data for granite exist only at 28 GHz and room temperature (Woskov, Barre granite: n = 2.24, α_f = 0.14 Np/cm → power penetration depth 3.6 cm, tan δ ≈ 0.021); no measured 90–140 GHz permittivity of granite, hot or cold, was found anywhere — the 137 GHz figures in the MIT/Quaise literature are emissivities of the melt, not solid-rock absorption coefficients. The model's α = 300 m⁻¹ at 137 GHz corresponds to tan δ ≈ 0.045, roughly twice Woskov's room-temperature 28 GHz value, i.e. it is a plausible warm-rock (≈500 °C by the Hartlieb ratio) or mildly frequency-steepened value, but it is not a documented measurement.

### Inferences
1. Granite's microwave loss in the 0.4–3 GHz band is thermally activated and conduction-like (ε″ ∝ f^−0.41, Arrhenius-type rise above ~400 °C, Olhoeft's framework; feldspar alkali-ion conduction with ~1 eV activation energy is the natural carrier). The ×5.6 rise in tan δ between RT and 700 °C at 2.45 GHz is the quantitative basis for any "hot skin absorbs more" argument, and it is **real but modest**: d_p at 2.45 GHz only shrinks from 1.2 m to 0.2 m, so at 2.45 GHz granite remains volumetrically heated through centimetre-to-decimetre samples all the way to 1000 °C.
2. The 573 °C quartz transition is **not** a dielectric event in granite at microwave frequencies (Hartlieb/MPN explicit statement; MPN quartz data; low-frequency anomaly only). The hypothesis that the quartz α-β transition steepens absorption should be dropped; the loss rise is carried by feldspar/mica conduction and, above ~675 °C, by an irreversible transformation (likely dehydroxylation/oxidation of biotite/chlorite or breakdown of a hydrous phase — the rod bleached from grey to white, and ε″ fell back after 750 °C).
3. Frequency scaling of the temperature effect: a conduction loss contributes ε″_cond = σ/(2πf ε₀), i.e. 1/f. For σ = 10⁻³ S/m, ε″_cond = 0.0073 at 2.45 GHz, 0.0018 at 10 GHz, 0.00013 at 137 GHz (COMPUTED HERE). ## Q8. Is thermal runaway in microwave-heated rock documented, at what temperature does it set in, what is the mechanism, and what temperatures, heating rates, spalling, cracking and melting were observed?

### Takeaway
Thermal runaway is documented experimentally and theoretically for **basalt** (Jerby group, 2.45 GHz, ≤1 kW, hotspot to >1200–1500 K in ~1–2 min, molten core in 70 s in simulation) and is the operating principle of the microwave drill (runaway in the "temperature growth-rate above ~600 °C"); for **granite** no author reports a solid-state runaway — granite heats slowly (~1 °C/s at 0.9–3 kW), its melting in cavity experiments at 1.4–6 kW is local to biotite-rich spots at 550–800 °C surface temperatures over minutes, and at 28 GHz Woskov's granite remained partly transparent until a melt formed 2–4 mm below the surface, after which absorption jumped to the surface and the heating rate rose 15× (7.4 → 110 °C/s). Spalling under microwaves is reported only for basalt (violent fragment expulsion before melting, 2.7 kW at 28 GHz; surface spalling/bursting at 2.45 GHz); granite cracks and bleaches but is not reported to spall.

### Inferences
1. Thermal runaway in rock is a documented, modelled and exploited phenomenon, but **only for materials whose loss rises steeply toward melting (basalt, concrete, glass, alumina)**, and the documented onset is "above ~600 °C" in Jerby's simulations with the explosive phase within ~200 K of the melt. In granite the measured loss rise (×2 at 500 °C, ×5.6 at 700 °C at 2.45 GHz) is too gentle, and the penetration depth too large (0.2–1 m at 2.45 GHz), for a self-focusing skin to form in the solid; every granite "melting" report at 2.45 GHz is a biotite-seeded local melt reached over minutes at 1.4–6 kW with surface temperatures 550–800 °C.
2. The one sharp absorption event common to all granite experiments is the **melt step**: Woskov's transmission cut-off, the 15× jump in heating rate on a glassed surface, the reflectivity change 0.14 → 0.3, and the fact that the melt nucleates 2–4 mm below the surface (where the volumetric deposition peaks under surface cooling) and breaks out. For the user's hypothesis this means the "race" is not skin-sharpening-vs-melting but **volumetric heating until a sub-surface melt nucleates, then an abrupt switch to surface-limited absorption**. A model that encodes this as α(T) with a step (or a steep sigmoid) at the solidus, plus the Hartlieb ratio for the solid, is supported; a smooth solid-state runaway driven by the 573 °C quartz transition is not.
3. Spalling under microwaves is reported for basalt (violent, before melting, at 28 GHz with 0.3–0.9 kW/cm², and "surface spalling, bursting" at 2.45 GHz multimode), never explicitly for granite, which instead cracks, bleaches (the ~30 mm bleached/weakened halo) and loses strength (UCS −80 % by 800 °C). The user's observation of granite spalling at lower ~10 GHz intensity therefore has no direct literature precedent at microwave frequencies; it is closer to flame/laser thermal spallation of granite, where the surface is heated conductively — which would be the expected regime if the 10 GHz penetration depth were much shorter than the ~10 cm inferred for dry Barre granite (e.g. wet rock, higher-loss granite, or the beam's field concentration).
4. Heating-rate scale: granite surfaces reach ~1 °C/s at ~1 kW in a 2.45 GHz cavity, 7 °C/s (volume) and 110 °C/s (glassed surface) at 28 GHz with ~1 kW/cm²; the melt-to-vapour regime is radiation-limited (temperature ∝ power^⅓, "the thermal signal changes would have to be raised to a power of about three to equal the incident power changes").

### Gaps
- Original texts of Jerby, Meir, Faran (AMPERE 2013) and Lu et al. 2017 (Appl. Therm. Eng.) were not accessible; their runaway-onset and per-mineral heating numbers are taken from the Teimoori & Hassani review and search snippets.
- No source gives a runaway-onset temperature for **granite**; the "~600 °C" figure is Jerby's for the microwave-drill materials (alumina/concrete/basalt) and the Zeng et al. 600 °C is a melt-cavity observation, not a dielectric runaway threshold.
- Power densities in the cavity experiments are mostly reported as cavity power (kW), not surface intensity; only Woskov (1–2 kW/cm², 0.3–0.9 kW/cm² in the DOE tests) and the diabase antenna study (22 W/cm²) quote W/cm².
- Satish (2005 McGill thesis), Kingman/Jones comminution papers, Toifl et al. 2017 full text, Peinsitt 2010 full text, Kahraman 2020 full text, Zeng 2019 full text, Zheng et al. 2020 Rock Mech. Rock Eng. and the Springer 2026 granite-mineral papers were not reachable (403/redirect); none of the accessible summaries of them mentions a dielectric runaway in granite.
- No microwave experiment on granite reports surface spalling; whether this is because none occurred or because cavity geometries hide it (debris not collected) cannot be decided from the accessible texts.

**One-line verdict (Q8):** Thermal runaway in microwave-heated rock is documented and modelled, but only for basalt-type (iron-rich, conductivity-rising) rocks with onset ≳600 °C and explosive behaviour within ~200 K of melting; for granite the literature shows slow volumetric heating (~1 °C/s at kW cavity power, 7 °C/s at 28 GHz/1 kW cm⁻²), biotite-seeded local melting at 550–800 °C surface temperature over minutes, cracking and bleaching but **no reported spalling and no solid-state runaway** — the only sharp absorption step is the sub-surface melt breakout (×100 absorption, heating rate ×15), so the user's regime boundary should be sought at the solidus, not at the 573 °C quartz transition.
