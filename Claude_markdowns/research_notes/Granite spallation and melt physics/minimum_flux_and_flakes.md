## Q4 — What physical argument gives a minimum heat flux (or maximum standoff), and does it predict a flat onset with a sharp cut-off or a gradual climb?

### Takeaway
The literature contains exactly one derived minimum-flux argument — Preston's buckling mechanism made quantitative by Dey & Kranz (1985, 1987): a heated layer can only buckle if a flaw spans more than ~10× its thickness, and since the heated-layer thickness grows as k·ΔT_s/q when flux falls, a bounded flaw population implies a bounded layer thickness and hence a minimum flux. It is a bifurcation (geometric) condition on top of a fixed-stress (fixed-ΔT_s) criterion, so it predicts a FLAT onset temperature above q_min and NO spallation below it — not a climb. The Weibull-volume models (Dey 1984 → Rauenzahn & Tester 1989 → Wilkinson & Tester 1993 → Walsh & Lomov 2013) and Kant et al.'s 2017 LEFM criterion contain no minimum flux at all and make the onset slightly EASIER as flux falls. The only measured minima are Kant & Rudolf von Rohr 2016 (0.45–0.61 MW/m² sensor flux, h ≈ 500–700 W/m²K, Gotthard/Bethel granite) and the empirical "~1 MW/m² and ~500 °C" rule of thumb traced to Rauenzahn; a third, heat-loss/finite-body argument (Browning 1965, Meier 2015, Beentjes 2018) is stated qualitatively only.

### Cited Findings

**A. Fixed-stress (Gray) criterion — the baseline every model inherits**
- Gray (1965, Canada Mines Bureau) compared the plane compressive stress in a semi-infinite elastic body confined in the two in-plane directions to the compressive strength σ_c, giving a spalling temperature RISE ΔT_s = (1−ν)σ_c/(β E) [Eq. 1], with E Young's modulus, ν Poisson's ratio, β linear expansion coefficient; "for the 13 samples tested by the Mines Bureau ... ΔT_s is essentially constant" — a pure temperature criterion with no flux dependence and no minimum. MODELLED. — [Rauenzahn & Tester 1989, IJRMMS 26:381, p. 385](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2); on-disk `franck_material/RockFailureMechanism.pdf`.
- Dmitriyev et al. (Russian school, via R&T 1989 Eqs. 2–4) kept Gray's ΔT_s and added a layer thickness that "must support the entire linearized temperature difference": δ_s = k_r ΔT_s / [h (T_f − T_s)] = k_r ΔT_s / q, with h the flame convective coefficient, T_f flame temperature, T_s surface spall temperature; time to spall from 1 − exp(X)·erfc(√X) = ΔT_s/(T_f − T_0), X = h²α_r t/k_r²; drilling speed = δ_s/t_s. Thickness ∝ 1/q, but still no minimum flux: as q → 0 the layer thickens and the time lengthens without bound. MODELLED. — [R&T 1989 p. 385](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2).

**B. Buckling / bifurcation — the one derived minimum**
- Preston (1934, bricks) proposed the accepted mechanism: a pre-existing flaw near the surface under thermal compression extends parallel to the surface; "the high aspect ratio (diameter to thickness) of the resulting plate and the high compressive stress cause buckling". — [Wilkinson & Tester 1993, RMRE 26:29, §4 and Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01019868).
- R&T 1989 Eqs. 15–16 (Preston/Dey buckling): an incipient chip "will spall by a buckling mode of failure from the surface that is confined against movement in the plane of a crack extension when the cracked length d divided by the thickness of the potential spalls exceeds" π/√(6(1−ν²)σ_s/E), which with σ_s = EβΔT_s/(1−ν) becomes C_L = d/δ = π/√(6(1+ν)β_r ΔT_s) ≈ 10 (the paper's stated value; the OCR of the radical is imperfect, treat the exact prefactor as "Euler plate-strip buckling"). MODELLED. — [R&T 1989 p. 390](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2).
- "Building on Preston's mechanism of spall production, Dey and Kranz [1985, 1987] employed plate-buckling theory to BOUND THE HEAT FLUX required to generate spalls from a granite surface. Their analysis estimated spall rates by relating heat penetration depths to an assumed Weibull distribution of fracture-nucleation sites." (Secondary; the Dey & Kranz equation itself could not be opened.) — [Walsh, Lomov & Roberts, GRC Trans. 2011, §1](https://gdr.openei.org/files/174/WalshLomovRoberts_GRC_2011.pdf); Dey & Kranz 1985 = GRC Trans. 9:103–106; Dey & Kranz 1987 = [28th US Symp. Rock Mech., ARMA-87-0413](https://onepetro.org/ARMAUSRMS/proceedings-abstract/ARMA87/All-ARMA87/ARMA-87-0413/129761) ("Rock failure during thermal spallation drilling as well as during the formation of borehole breakouts is caused by high compressive stresses parallel to the rock surface").
- "The traditional buckling model of spall-production is essentially a bifurcation event [14]. Hence, small fluctuations in the stress-state can have a large impact on the onset and extent of spall production." — [Walsh & Lomov 2013, LLNL-TR-632239, §4 "Microporosity"](file:///Users/tzetze20/amr_tools/alamo/franck_material/Walsh_2013_ModelingThermallyInducedFailure_LLNL-TR-632239.pdf).
- Grain-scale GEODYN simulations: "simulations with a flat fluid-rock interface withstand substantially higher heat fluxes before spalling ... suggesting that analyses employing classical beam and plate buckling theory in a semi-infinite half-space may over-estimate the compressive stress required for spall production"; asperities nucleate spalls; fractures start at grain boundaries and run along the thermal front. MODELLED. — [Walsh/Lomov/Roberts GRC 2011 §3](https://gdr.openei.org/files/174/WalshLomovRoberts_GRC_2011.pdf); [SGW 2012, LLNL-CONF-523558](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1035600).
- Germanovich (1997, ICF9 vol. 6 pp. 2771–2782) adopted the thin-layer buckling approach for rock and made spallability a function of rock type AND absorbed heat flux (seen only through a 2021 Acta Geotechnica citation; original not opened). — [Acta Geotech. 2021 summary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11440-020-00927-7).

**C. Weibull-volume models — flux dependence of the WRONG sign for a minimum**
- Dey (1984) / R&T 1989 Eqs. 9–14: median spalling (G = 0.5, I = 0.693) over a cone of disc-shaped spalls of aspect ratio C_L with the quasi-steady "subliming" profile T − T_0 = ΔT_s exp(−u_r x/α_r) and σ = βEΔT/(1−ν): 0.693 = (σ_s/σ_0)^m (πC_L²/4) ∫₀^∞ x² e^{−ωx} dx, ω = m u_r/α_r ⇒ T_s − T_0 = [ (Q/ρc_p)³ ((1−ν)σ_0/(β_r E))^m (2·0.693/(πC_L²)) (m/α_r)³ ]^{1/(m+3)}, i.e. ΔT_s ∝ Q^{3/(m+3)} (exponent 0.13 at m = 20, 0.28 at m = 7.7). "From equation (14), high heat fluxes induce stresses in a thinner skin of rock more quickly. The overall stress level in that region must be higher" — so the steady spalling temperature RISES weakly with flux and FALLS as flux drops; nothing stops spallation at low Q. MODELLED. — [R&T 1989 pp. 390–391](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2); same equation as [W&T 1993 Eq. 9](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01019868) and [Walsh & Lomov 2013 Eqs. 14–19](file:///Users/tzetze20/amr_tools/alamo/franck_material/Walsh_2013_ModelingThermallyInducedFailure_LLNL-TR-632239.pdf).
- Applied at the low-flux extreme: R&T used Eq. 14 on a 2 m gneiss diesel-exhaust passage spalling at ~0.006 m/h under 300 °C gas and computed a spallation temperature of 150 °C (Gray's conduction estimate: 110 °C from 2.5 cm thick slabs) — the model happily spalls at fluxes of order kW/m², which is the clearest statement that the Weibull family has no floor. MODELLED vs field observation. — [R&T 1989 p. 392](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2).
- Walsh & Lomov 2013 replaced the constant C_L by C_L(x) = 1 for x ≤ 100 µm, 5 for x > 100 µm (from field spall measurements): the flux for a given rate rises ~15 % and the predicted surface temperature "is changed significantly ... as the scaled thermal penetration distance ω = mα/v approaches the spall thickness associated with the aspect ratio transition" — a fixed physical length is what injects flux dependence into the Weibull model. MODELLED. — [Walsh & Lomov 2013 §4 Eqs. 15–20](file:///Users/tzetze20/amr_tools/alamo/franck_material/Walsh_2013_ModelingThermallyInducedFailure_LLNL-TR-632239.pdf).
- A full-text search of both MIT theses (Rauenzahn 1986 pp. 17–460, Wilkinson 1989 pp. 11–262) for "minimum heat/flux", "threshold", "cease" found NO minimum flux or wall-spallation cut-off; the modelled wall recedes for any Q > 0; the only cut-off is an UPPER temperature ("Above a rock-specific temperature, spallation ceases to occur ... attributed ... to the rock becoming plastic", W p. 82). — [on-disk `rauenzahn_wilkinson_inhole_heat_notes.md` §5](file:///Users/tzetze20/amr_tools/alamo/tests/MMWSpalling/validation/hoeser/rauenzahn_wilkinson_inhole_heat_notes.md), theses at [hdl 1721.1/14884](https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/14884) and [hdl 1721.1/14031](https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/14031).
- Kant et al. 2017 LEFM criterion Sp = K_I/K_Ic ≥ 1 for a plane-strain edge crack (a = 20 µm, "the largest initially present cracks") under σ_xx = −EαΘ/(1−ν) − pν/(1−ν) with Θ the temperature rise over the INITIAL rock temperature; reduced form Sp_red = 2·1.763·√(a³/π)·Eα/(K_Ic(1−ν)λ)·(T_fl − T_i)·h + 2·1.763·√(a/π)·pν/((1−ν)K_Ic). The onset falls with confining pressure (measured −70 °C over 48 MPa, modelled −45 °C). No minimum flux appears in the criterion although the same group measured one a year earlier. MODELLED. — [Kant et al. 2017, JGR Solid Earth 122:1805](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JB013800); on-disk note `kant_2017_jgr_notes.md`.
- Kant SGW 2017 drilling-velocity model: u solves Θ_SP = λ(h/λ)(V − T_i)/(u/κ + h/λ)·exp(−uΔx/κ) for u < u_m = (κ/Δx)·ln(Θ_m/Θ_SP), u ≈ 0 above (melting cap); parameters Θ_SP = 550 °C, Θ_M = 1100 °C, V = 1200 °C, λ = 2 W/mK, Δx = 200 µm; "after a steep increase of the drilling velocity at rather low heat transfer coefficients, the velocity increase levels off" — again continuous to h → 0, no floor. MODELLED. — [Kant et al. SGW 2017, Eqs. 13–15, Fig. 4](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2017/Kant2.pdf).

**D. Heat-loss / finite-body arguments (qualitative)**
- Browning et al. 1965 (via Meier 2015): "If the heat flux is too low, the induced stress field is not strong enough to fracture the rock. In contrary, if the heat flux is too high, the heat diffuses faster than the spalls are removed, the temperature profile flattens and the stresses are diminished. Therefore, an optimal incident heat flux must exist for every kind of rock." EMPIRICAL. — [Meier, Brkic et al., WGC 2015, §3.4](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/WGC/papers/WGC/2015/21040.pdf).
- Meier 2015 hydrothermal-flame tests (78 kW, 15 mm standoff, 1/10/30 min): the cavity grows from 1 to 10 min but "no obvious difference" between 10 and 30 min; the centreline temperature profile "does not evolve anymore after 10 minutes ... the state of stress in the rock probe starts to decrease as the temperature profile flattens"; once > 10 % of an unconfined sample's surface is heated the planar-confinement criterion fails and "the stresses released". MEASURED (cavity size, thermocouples). — [Meier et al. WGC 2015 §3.3–3.4, Fig. 7](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/WGC/papers/WGC/2015/21040.pdf).
- Beentjes et al. 2018 (Cornell, supercritical-water jets on Barre granite): "If the heat flux at the rock surface is too low, the heat transferred to the rock will dissipate, allowing the rock to expand at a rate that does not force spallation" — a strain-rate/heat-loss phrasing; their fluxes "were also near or below the lowest values measured for flame or laser induced spallation. Therefore ... there appears to be a threshold temperature/heat flux that must be reached to induce true thermal spallation." EMPIRICAL. — [Beentjes et al. SGW 2018, §1 and §5](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2018/Beentjes.pdf).
- Thirumalai 1969/1970 (secondary, via on-disk literature note): "in good spalling rocks a thin heated layer with high thermal gradient forms" (quartzite ~1 mm, > 200 °C/mm); "inelastic behaviour inhibits thermal spalling". — [`literature_minimum_flux_notes.md` §B](file:///Users/tzetze20/amr_tools/alamo/tests/MMWSpalling/validation/hoeser/literature_minimum_flux_notes.md).
- Seagraves ARMA-2019-1799 (abstract only): continuous thermally activated surface recession + fracture-mechanics onset at GRAIN-BOUNDARY flaws; "three characteristic scales ... control the onset temperature for spallation, the dependence of surface temperature and penetration rate on heat flux, and the limiting penetration rate at the onset of surface melting"; matches W&T's Barre/Westerly rate AND T_s(q). Whether it contains a minimum flux is not stated in the abstract. MODELLED. — [OnePetro abstract](https://onepetro.org/ARMAUSRMS/proceedings-abstract/ARMA19/All-ARMA19/ARMA-2019-1799/125016).

### Inferences
- The buckling bound turned into a flux: buckling needs flaw span d ≥ C_L·δ with C_L ≈ 10 (R&T Eq. 16) and δ ≈ k ΔT_s/q (Dmitriyev Eq. 2). With a largest usable in-plane flaw/weakness d_max, q_min ≈ C_L·k·ΔT_s/d_max. For Grimsel-like numbers (k = 3.2 W/mK, ΔT_s ≈ 550 K) this gives q_min ≈ 1.2 MW/m² for d_max = 15 mm, 0.9 for 20 mm, 0.45 for 40 mm — the Kant 2016 range (0.45–0.6 MW/m²) corresponds to d_max of a few centimetres, i.e. the 10–20-grain spall diameters actually observed (R&T, W&T). This is my arithmetic, not Dey & Kranz's published number.
- Shape of the onset curve implied by each family: (i) Gray/Preston/Dey–Kranz buckling: onset flat at ΔT_s = (1−ν)σ_c/(βE) wherever the geometric condition is met, no spall at all below q_min — a step, not a ramp; (ii) Weibull-volume: onset FALLS gently as q falls (ΔT_s ∝ q^{3/(m+3)}), never stops; (iii) Kant LEFM: flat in q (stress is set by the surface rise over the initial temperature), lowered by confinement, never stops; (iv) heat-loss/finite-body: a time-dependent cut-off (spalling stops when the body saturates — Meier's 10 min), not a temperature climb. None of the four predicts the model's climb from 879 K to 1001 K between 1.6 and 0.34 MW/m².
- The model's constraint depth is structurally the Dmitriyev/Dey layer thickness δ = kΔT/q frozen at d = 2 mm: it reproduces q_min ~ kΔT/d (≈ 0.9 MW/m² for ΔT 550 K) but, because the driving ΔT is taken across a fixed 2 mm instead of the stress being set by the surface rise, it converts the geometric cut-off into a temperature penalty. The literature route keeps the stress criterion fixed and lets GEOMETRY (flaw span vs layer thickness) switch spalling off.
- Walsh & Lomov's finding that the aspect-ratio transition length (~100–400 µm, grain scale) changes the predicted surface temperature "significantly" when ω⁻¹ = α/(mv) approaches it supports the general point that any fixed physical length put into a volume criterion produces flux dependence; the sign and size depend on where the length sits relative to the thermal layer.

- Dey & Kranz 1985 (GRC Trans. 9:103) and 1987 (ARMA-87-0413) full texts: the actual buckling-bound equation and the bound's numerical value (in MW/m²) were not obtainable; everything above about them is via Walsh et al. 2011/2013 and R&T 1989.
- Augustine 2009 (MIT, hdl 1721.1/51671): DSpace returned 429 and the sandbox curl returned 0 bytes; any minimum-flux statement there is unverified (the search snippet "heat flux (~1 MW/m²)" matches the Meier 2015 quote of Rauenzahn).
- Kant 2016 does not state the exposure time after which "no spallation" was declared at SOD 140–160 mm (raw-data traces in Fig. 9a run to ≥ 90 s).
- Whether any W&T 1993 laser run at 0.1–0.3 MW/m² FAILED to spall is not stated in the extracted text; the lowest spalled flux (0.23 MW/m² Westerly) is a lower bound on the data, not the threshold.
- Seagraves 2019 full text (paywalled): the closest published analogue to a mechanistic q-dependence; whether it has a floor is unknown.

**Verdict Q4:** the only derived minimum flux in the literature is a buckling/bifurcation bound (flaw span ≥ ~10 × heated-layer thickness, with the layer ∝ kΔT_s/q), which predicts a FLAT onset temperature above q_min and a sharp cut-off below it; the Weibull-volume and LEFM models have no minimum and make onset slightly easier at low flux; no published model predicts an onset that climbs toward q_min, so the model's climb is a construction artefact of referencing the stress to a fixed depth rather than a supported physical feature.

## Q5 — Which datasets resolve onset temperature vs heat flux near the minimum, and what are the exposure times?

### Takeaway
Four independent datasets measure the FIRST-spall (onset) temperature across a flux range and all are flat or slightly falling toward the threshold: Rauenzahn & Tester 1989 (Barre/Westerly, propane torch, 0.68–1.19 MW/m², ΔT_s 423–473 K, "quite small, if any" flux dependence), Wilkinson & Tester 1993 (global onset 400–600 °C over 0.2–2.7 MW/m², "relatively insensitive to the applied heat flux"; Sioux quartzite flat at 331–364 °C over 0.71–2.29 MW/m²), Kant & Rudolf von Rohr 2016 (Gotthard 620 → 570 °C over 0.72 → 0.61 MW/m² sensor flux as SOD 50 → 130 mm; Bethel 630 → 540 °C to SOD 150; means ≈ 600 °C with no SOD trend), and Höser 2016 (Grimsel 430–600 °C at 4.3–7.4 MW/m², "any trend was not recorded"). The one RISING curve in the literature — Wilkinson's 500 → 930 °C on Barre over 0.6 → 2.9 MW/m², and Kant 2016's "linear" Fig. 14 regression built on it — is the STEADY-STATE spalling-surface mean (flakes overheating before ejection), not the onset. Onset occurs within 0.5–5 s of flame contact in every lab study; the no-spall exposure times are poorly reported (Kant: traces ≥ 90 s; Meier hydrothermal: cavity growth stops between 10 and 30 min).

### Cited Findings

**Rauenzahn & Tester 1989 — Barre and Westerly granite, propane–oxygen welding torch (25 mm tip, standoff 15.2/20.3 cm), first spall on 2000 fps video, flux by copper-block calorimetry, T_s from the 1-D constant-flux solution ΔT_s = 2Q√(αt/π)/k**
- Torch flux profile Q = Q_max exp(−r/r₀) with Q_max = 0.68/0.89/0.92/1.19 MW/m², r₀ = 90/79/87/75 mm (Table 3). Spallation temperature RISE 465/473, 435/446, 445/423, 472/455 K (Barre/Westerly) — i.e. T_s ≈ 700–750 K ≈ 430–480 °C, with "quite small, if any" dependence on flux (Fig. 9); all below the 573 °C quartz transition. "Spallation commenced within 2 sec after exposure of the surface for all experiments conducted and was allowed to continue until a region about 8–10 cm in diameter had been damaged." MEASURED (indirect T). — [R&T 1989 pp. 392–395, Table 3, Fig. 9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2); on-disk `rauenzahn_tester_1989_notes.md`.
- The paper's own expectation: "The dependency of predicted surface temperature on applied heat flux is often slight (ΔT_s ∝ Q^{3/(m+3)}) ... for extremely homogeneous samples (m ≫ 1) such as the granites used in these tests, variation with heating rate should be minimal." — [R&T 1989 p. 392](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2).
- Laser (500 W CO₂, 5–15 mm beam): 520–1120 K, inconsistent — "extremely localized heating ... invalidates the 1-D assumption"; single grains lifted. — [R&T 1989 §Experimental](https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-9062(89)90935-2).

## Q6 — Reported flake thicknesses/diameters, their scaling with flux or heated-layer depth, and distribution parameters

### Takeaway
Direct thickness measurements are scarce and old: the Canadian Mines Bureau field average of 0.4 mm ("less than a grain"), the MIT torch/field description "lamellar, 10–20 grains in diameter, one to two grains thick" (aspect ratio 8–15 for Geller's chips, 10 from buckling), and the LLNL shadow-method survey of Potter Drilling field spalls giving a size-dependent aspect ratio (≈ 6:1 above 5 mm, 4:1 at 1 mm, 2:1–1:1 at 200 µm, transition at ~400 µm ≈ grain size) with thicknesses up to ~2 mm; Hu et al. 2018 (280 spalls, granite) confirm size dependence and recommend 6.91. Höser's Grimsel "200–300 µm" is a laser-diffraction FRAGMENT peak, not a thickness. The only thickness THEORY is Dey/Rauenzahn's Weibull spall-size distribution in ξ = m V x/α (median 2.9, mode 2.0, number mean 3.35, volume-weighted 4.85 for C_L = 10), giving x_med ≈ 0.10 mm at 6 m/h and thickness ∝ α/(mV) ∝ 1/q; the Dmitriyev/Gray layer rule δ = kΔT_s/q gives the same 1/q scaling at ~20× larger absolute values. No Grimsel thickness measurement exists against which the model's h = 0.4 × skin depth (cap 1.5 mm) can be checked.

### Inferences
- Scaling comparison (my arithmetic, k = 3.2 W/mK, ΔT_s = 550 K, κ = 1.2e-6 m²/s, ρc_p = 2.7e6 J/m³K): the layer rule δ = kΔT/q gives 3.5 mm at 0.5 MW/m², 1.8 at 1.0, 0.35 at 5.0, 0.24 mm at 7.4 MW/m²; the Weibull median x_med = 2.9 κ/(m V) with V = q/(ρc_p ΔT) gives 0.14 mm at 0.5 MW/m², 0.07 at 1.0, 0.014 at 5 (m = 20) — i.e. the Weibull family predicts flakes 20–25× thinner than the heated layer (x_med ≈ 0.145 ℓ with ℓ = κ/V) and the measured 0.4 mm field average / 1–2-grain lab flakes sit between the two rules, closer to the layer rule at high flux (Höser's 200–300 µm fragment peak at 4.3–7.4 MW/m² ≈ kΔT/q = 0.24–0.41 mm) and to ~0.1–0.25 ℓ at field rates (Browning 15.8 m/h: ℓ ≈ 0.27 mm, measured 0.4 mm).
- Both published rules give thickness ∝ 1/q with no cap; the model's cap of 1.5 mm equals kΔT_s/q at q ≈ 1.2 MW/m² (k 3.2, ΔT 550), so below ~1.2 MW/m² the model's flake is thinner than either rule predicts, which is the mechanism by which a flake cap acts as a hidden minimum-flux rule (as the Kant 2016 note already observed).
- The thermal penetration at onset, √(κt) for t = 0.5–5 s = 0.8–2.4 mm, is 2–10× the typical measured flake thickness (0.2–0.5 mm at ≥ 4 MW/m²; 0.4 mm field), consistent with Preston's picture that the flake is the outer fraction of the heated layer rather than the whole of it; Walsh's aspect-ratio collapse below ~400 µm says the flake thickness has a FLOOR near the grain size, not the ceiling the model imposes.
- No experiment reports flake thickness as a function of heat flux on one rock with one method; the "thickness falls with flux" statement is theory (Rauenzahn) echoed by Kant, supported only by the qualitative "if the heat flux is low, relatively thick" remark and Gray's 2.5 cm slabs at ~kW/m² fluxes.

### Gaps
- No measured Grimsel/Central Aare flake thickness anywhere (Höser measured particle size by sieve and laser diffraction only; Kant 2017 did not collect spalls).
- Hu et al. 2018 body (flux, thickness distribution, trimodal parameters) and Walsh & Lomov 2014 IJRMMS 70:375 journal version not opened; Fig. 12c thickness–width data of Walsh 2013 were not digitised.
- Rauenzahn 1986 thesis spall-size figures (Eq. 4.99, w = 2.67 median) could not be re-read; the 1989 paper's 2.9 is used here.
- Dey & Kranz 1987 "Flake mechanics" — the paper most likely to give a flake-thickness/buckling relation — unavailable.
- Williams/Potter/Dey (Los Alamos, 1988/1996) spall sizes unverified.

**Verdict Q6:** measured flame-jet flakes are 0.2–2 mm thick (0.4 mm field average; 1–2 grains in the lab) with size-dependent aspect ratios of 2–10 (floor near the grain size), the only thickness theories (Weibull x_med = 2.9 κ/(mV); layer rule δ = kΔT/q) both scale as 1/q with no upper cap, and no Grimsel thickness exists — so the model's h = 0.4 × skin depth is within the measured band at ≥ 1 MW/m² but its 1.5 mm cap has no experimental or theoretical basis and acts as an undeclared minimum-flux rule below ~1.2 MW/m².
