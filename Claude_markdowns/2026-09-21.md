Three facts:
1. **The silent 1 mm pad columns are hot, not cold: 815–829 K, i.e. at the firing temperature.**
   They are pinned (pinned share equal across meshes, idle columns 0) and receive the imposed
   flux, yet do not fire. They sit in a thermal equilibrium within a few kelvin of the threshold:
   q_pin (≈ 530–600) ≈ conduction down (≈ 300) + lateral loss (≈ 400 at 1 mm, ≈ 300 at 2 mm).
   Whether a column crosses is decided by tens of kW/m² and by its own flaw draw. A knife-edge.
2. **The lateral term is the mesh-dependent one.** Same imposed flux at both meshes (q_pin agrees
   to 1 %), same T_gas to 6 K; the lateral loss is k ΔT/dx and is 30–45 % larger at 1 mm because
   the finer mesh resolves a steeper gradient into the cold rock one cell away. That takes the
   1 mm edge from "just fires" to "just does not". Once a ring stalls it is the cold neighbour
   of the next ring: the front walks inward (40–44 mm 18 % → 36–40 mm 4 % at 450 s; 80 % / 31 %
   at 550 s).
3. **The cold rock beside the rim is the model's own hole shape.** Face depth falls ≈ 12 mm per
   2 mm of radius from r = 30 to 48 mm at both meshes: the floor around the pit is an ≈ 80°
   cone (2 mm: 238 → 153 mm over 14 mm of radius; 1 mm: 230 → 125). The rock beside the rim's
   top cell is the interior of a flank column whose own face is 10–25 mm shallower: virgin,
   500–680 K, renewed as the floor descends. This is not the `s ≤ 0` wall (that is further out,
   ≥ 44–46 mm) and not the idle clock. It is geometry.

## Why the cone
The pinned/Robin flux acts on the **horizontal** cell area (D2a caveat: "no side-wall Robin"),
and h falls with r and s. A face sloped at 80° has 6× the true area per horizontal area, so per
unit of real surface it receives one sixth of the flux: sloped rock is starved, stays steep and
cold, and drains the pad rim. The same family as D2b's centre-pit runaway and "A3 inert under
pinned". Meier's hole bottom is a "half elongated spheroid" (p. 222), not a needle pit with an
80° skirt.

## Diagnosis in one sentence
The mesh discrepancy is the visible symptom of a hole geometry the model gets wrong: flux per
horizontal area plus a radially falling h produce a steep cold cone around the pit; the pad rim
lives at the edge of that cone in a thermal balance a few kelvin under the firing temperature,
and the cell width decides which side each column falls on. Removing the idle clock removed one
trigger; the knife-edge is still there, and no refinement removes it.

## What would (and would not) fix it
- **Would:** flux per true face area (× 1/cos θ, θ from the column-height gradient, capped),
  i.e. side-wall heating, default-off. Prediction: the 80° cone relaxes, T_nb rises by
  ≥ 100 K, the rim margin grows to > 250 kW/m² at both meshes, no front to 450 s, pair within
  5 %. The hole widens outside the pad (Ø at the feet depth goes up); the hand model should
  pre-register by how much. This is D2d §7(h) item 1, and the surface-normal kernel (Step 20)
  already computes the slope; the missing piece is applying it under the pinned closure.
- **Would also help, later:** heating the wall above the plane (exhaust) — a separate, outer
  cold sink; the wellhead/tube confinement for the mouth.
- **Would not:** `losses.T_amb` as a proxy (losses are 25 kW/m²; the wall cools by conduction,
  not by radiation); any coherence cap (it caps the fast column, freezing cones);
  a longer idle clock (D2i: 20 ≡ off); more mesh pairs on this configuration.
- **Test:** one 2 mm / 1 mm pair to 450 s (≈ 2.5 h at 1 mm) with the normal-area flux on,
  hand prediction of the slope and the feet-depth Ø written first; criterion as D2i.

## Caveats
- The lateral loss is a single-neighbour, cell-centre estimate; downward conduction is not in
  the table (≈ 300 kW/m² from T(−1) ≈ 100–150 K below T_top). The ordering between meshes is
  robust; the absolute margins are not.
- The flaw-draw dependence (which column crosses first) is inferred from the 815–829 K
  silent columns, not from per-column K_I.
- The 2 mm run may also stall in a taller box: its edge margins (≈ 230–280 kW/m²) shrink as
  the cone steepens. Untested.

Every band stops firing **while still 5–50 mm below the plane** (still heated), 11–18 s (2 mm)
or 19–95 s (1 mm) *before* it crosses; and every band stops **9–23 s (1 mm) / 33–59 s (2 mm)

stalls was wrong in the detail and right in the role: the trigger of each stall is lateral loss
into the band outside it; what makes that band a strong sink is that, once above the plane, it
receives no flux at all and cools from ≈ 800 K to ≈ 400–500 K. In the hot disk the stalled
ring keeps its imposed flux, stays hot, and cannot cascade. Cascade speed = plane descent ×
lateral coupling strength; the latter is the ≈ 10 %/halving mesh-dependent edge, which is why
1 mm cascades 3× faster and 2 mm would follow later.

**Mechanism as now stated:** (lateral edge, mesh-dependent, bounded) + (zero flux above the
plane → stalled rock goes cold) + (feet on the rim → burner rate) → inward cooling cascade.

### What the 2 mm set says

1. **C1 reproduces the D2i cascade with no feet and no jet.** Every band inside the first stops
   7–14 s *before* its own face crosses the plane, ≈ 5 mm below it, and 21–36 s after the band
   outside it crossed — D2i's pattern (5–50 mm below, 33–59 s at 2 mm). The exclusion alone,
   applied to a static disk, produces the inward front.
2. **The exposed rock's surface temperature is irrelevant: C1 ≡ C2 ≡ C3 band for band.** A wall
   held by h = 100 at 1000 or 1600 K sits at 430–500 K thirty seconds after exposure (the cold
   bulk under a 1.5 mm ablation layer drains it faster than 50 kW/m² can supply); the cell that
   actually drains the firing neighbour is the wall column's interior at the step depth (10–20 mm
   below the exposed surface), 400–500 K in all three, unreachable by surface heating on this time
   scale. So **"heat the wall above the plane" is not the remedy** — P4 fails already at 2 mm.
3. **C0 cascades too, three times slower** (28–30 stops at 110 s, 26–28 at 216 s): the un-excluded
   stalled ring is *hot* (804 K, held by the imposed pinned flux; the D2i "hot silent pad columns"),
   its interior at the step depth is 550–560 K instead of 380–480 K, and the next band survives
   longer. So the mechanism is one thing in both: **a firing top cell beside a taller, colder
   column loses k(T_top − T_wall)/dx through the step; the pinned budget q_pin − q_down cannot pay
   it once T_wall falls below a threshold; the threshold scales with dx.** The exclusion only makes
   the first stall hard and the wall cold quickly. Wall *surface* heating cannot reach the sink.
4. Under this reading the 1 mm prediction is **amended before the 1 mm runs are read** (they are
   at ≈ 53 s of 250 as this is written, 15:26): the lateral term doubles at 1 mm, so **C0 at 1 mm should
   cascade as well** (not stand as the 150 s D2j-0 window suggested), and C1/C2 should cascade
   faster than at 2 mm, giving P3's growing gap in *both* C0 and C1. If C0 at 1 mm does not
   cascade, the exclusion is necessary after all and point 3 is overstated.

**Conclusions for the planner**
1. The D2i mesh failure is reproduced on a static disk with a plane: **no feet, no jet, no gas
   closure, and the exclusion is not even necessary.** It is the per-column surface itself: a
   firing top cell beside a taller, colder column loses k(T_top − T_wall)/dx through the step

   worse. This holds for any lateral hot/cold edge — pad rim, disk edge, cone wall.
2. **Wall heating above the plane is not a remedy** (C2 = C3 = C1 at both meshes). The sink is
