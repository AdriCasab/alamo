ALAMO · MMWSpalling · Meier flame-jet validation

One Week on the Meier Block

What we set out to reproduce, the sixteen steps we ran between 15 and 22 September 2026, what each one found, and where the model stands against the experiment now.

Written 22 September 2026 for readers who know heat transfer and rock drilling but have not followed the code. Every number is traceable to a results file in tests/MMWSpalling/studies/ or to the archive in docs/project/ARCHIVE_DONE.md.

In short. The goal of the week was to make the spallation model drill Thierry Meier's granite block the way his burner did: at 1.3 to 1.5 m per hour, leaving a hole 85 to 93 mm wide, with no parameter tuned to make it so. By the middle of the week the model did drill at the right speed with every input taken from the thesis, the drawings or a textbook correlation. But the hole was the wrong shape, and, more seriously, the answer changed when the mesh was refined. Five diagnostic steps then traced the mesh dependence through three partial causes to a single structural one: the model heats rock only through the top face of each vertical column, so at any step between a hot column and a taller cold one there is an unheated wall that drains heat from its neighbour at a rate that grows as the mesh is refined. A one-term fix that heats those step faces removed the mesh dependence completely on a controlled test, and it now exposes the next question, which is how strongly an impinging jet heats a vertical wall. The rate agreement with Meier still stands, on a stated window with a stated uncertainty. The hole shape and the long-time behaviour are not yet claims.

1. The Meier experiment

In 2017 Thierry Meier, at ETH Zürich, drilled a half-metre hole into a 660 kg block of Grimsel granite with a methane-air flame jet and nothing else: no bit, no contact, no drilling fluid. It is the cleanest published demonstration of thermal spallation drilling at laboratory scale, and it is the case the model is being validated against. Everything below comes from chapter 8 of his thesis and from the burner drawings he published alongside it.

The block was 0.7 by 0.7 by 0.5 m, loaded from the sides to about 1 MPa so it would not split. A sealed, water-cooled wellhead was glued to the top. The burner hung from a crane on coil tubing, held in tension by a drawworks, so that it advanced under gravity as the rock beneath it was removed. It burned 2.47 kg/h of methane in 52 kg/h of air, 38 kW on the higher heating value, through a converging-diverging nozzle with a 6 mm throat and a 7.5 mm exit. The jet was therefore choked and left the nozzle supersonic.

Three details of the setup turned out to matter more than the headline numbers. First, the burner did not float above the rock. Its tube continued 50 mm below the nozzle and ended in three slotted feet, so the burner rested on the highest rock under those pads and descended only when that rock was removed. Meier writes that the drill's own advance rate set the clearance to the hole wall, and that an operator jolted the tool to keep it free. Second, the hole diameter he reports, 85 to 93 mm, is only 5 to 13 mm wider than the 80 mm tube, and his sawn section (his figure 8.8) shows a near-cylindrical hole from top to bottom with no funnel at the mouth. Third, the gas temperature at the nozzle was never measured. The thermocouple he logged sat upstream of a flame that had lifted off its injector during the ramp, and read 500 to 580 °C for the whole drilling period. Any nozzle temperature the model uses is therefore an estimate, and the adiabatic flame temperature for his air-fuel ratio, about 1900 K, is the natural upper anchor.

The rate of penetration deserves one more sentence, because it drives the whole comparison. Meier quotes 1.5 m/h as the steady advance, but 0.5 m in the 1383 s of drilling is 1.30 m/h. The difference is the operator's pauses. The model has no operator, and its burner sits as low as the rock allows, so it bounds the real advance from above. The honest band is 1.3 to 1.5 m/h, and a model result at the top of that band is agreement, not excess.

2. The model in one page

The simulation lives in ALAMO, a block-structured finite-volume code built on AMReX. Rock is a three-dimensional grid of cubic cells, 2 mm or 1 mm on a side, in a quarter of the block with the jet axis at the corner so the two cut faces act as symmetry planes. Heat conduction is solved in enthalpy form with temperature-dependent properties for Grimsel granite. Mechanics is not solved: the thermal stress at the surface is taken from the one-dimensionally confined thermoelastic expression, which is the same closure Meier used in his own finite-element model.

Spallation is a surface event. Each column's top cell is tested every step: a Weibull-drawn flaw of length a is assumed to lie in the compressed skin, its stress intensity KI is integrated over the local temperature profile, and the column fires when KI reaches the temperature-dependent toughness KIc(T) of Nasseri's granite data. For this granite and the chosen flaw distribution the top cell fires at 821 K, plus or minus 7 K from cell to cell. When it fires, the level set that defines the surface drops by half a cell, and after two firings the cell is empty. It was established early in the week (step S1) that this increment is a removal step, not a flake thickness, and that the drilling rate is set by the energy balance, not by the increment: the flux must heat each new layer from the bulk to the firing temperature, so v = q / (ρ cp ΔTfire). With the flux the model computes at the hole floor, about 0.5 MW/m², and ΔTfire = 528 K, that is 1.5 m/h. The number is not a coincidence, and understanding why it is not was the first result of the week.

The flame is not simulated. It enters as a Robin boundary condition: a heat-transfer coefficient h and a gas temperature Tgas on each column's top face. Both vary with the column's radius from the jet axis and its stand-off from the nozzle. The coefficient comes from Martin's 1977 impinging-jet correlation, anchored at 1449 W/m²K at the stagnation point. The gas temperature is not prescribed: it is marched outward ring by ring from the stagnation point, each ring giving up the enthalpy the rock absorbed, so that the jet cannot deliver more power than it carries. That march is the piece of the model that changed most during the week, and figure 3 shows it.

Finally, the burner. Under the prescribed-feed treatment used before this week, the centre of the hole simply recedes at whatever rate is fed, and the flux adjusts to match; the rate carries no information. Under the feet rule adopted on 16 September, the nozzle sits 50 mm above the 0.9-quantile of the rock height under three pads on the 28 to 40 mm annulus, never rises, and descends as the pads' rock spalls. Nothing sets the rate. That is what makes the comparison with Meier's 1.3 to 1.5 m/h meaningful, and it is also what makes the rim of the pads the most sensitive place in the whole model, as the second half of the week showed.

3. How the work is run

The work is organised as numbered packets, each passing through three separate sessions: a planner writes the packet, an implementer builds and runs it, and a reviewer verifies it against the packet's own text and issues an accepted or rejected verdict. A fourth role, taken by the author of this report, reads the results, checks arithmetic against the raw logs, runs diagnostics when the packets leave a question open, and argues with the planner. Several of the week's decisive results came from those diagnostics rather than from packets, and they are labelled as such below.

Four rules were kept throughout, and they explain some of the odd-looking machinery in what follows.

  • No tuning toward Meier. No parameter was moved toward 1.5 m/h or 85 mm. Where a closure has a free constant, its value was pre-registered and a sensitivity run reported, never fitted.
  • Pre-registration. Before each campaign a criterion file was written, made read-only and hashed, with the pass and fail conditions, the windows and the estimators fixed. Results are scored against that text. When a prediction failed, the file says so.
  • Byte identity. Every code change is default-off. A reference set of 2639 output files from the project's full test suite must come back byte-identical with the new binary before any physics result is read. Only one source change was made all week (D2k), and it passed.
  • Mesh pairs. A result counts only if it agrees between a 2 mm and a 1 mm run, on the same window, within 5 % per 100 s block. This gate is what the second half of the week is about.

4. Where the week started

On the morning of 15 September the model drilled a two-dimensional Meier slab at 6.3 m/h, four times too fast, under a fixed 2 MW/m² heat flux over a 42 mm radius. Four short steps that day and the next set up the rest of the week and are summarised here rather than given their own sections.

E1 · beam energy leak fixed

The microwave beam source was measured from the level set rather than the first solid cell, so 30 to 60 % of the energy in every beam-plus-removal run vanished into voided cells. Fixed by counting from the removal mask; energy closure went from 0.42 to 0.995 and the slab's rate steadied at 6.3 m/h.

S1 · surface resolution accepted

A one-dimensional column study from 2 to 0.06 mm. Found that the firing criterion is a cell-average temperature threshold, that the half-cell removal increment is not a flake, that a fixed-flux rate is converged at 2 mm, and that a Robin flux is not: two discretisations bracketed the rate 14 to 19 m/h.

A1 · firing temperature accepted

Two removal-path bugs fixed and a per-event removal log added. With the Weibull reference volume set to the cell volume the firing temperature holds at 821 K across meshes. That choice switches off the Weibull size effect, which is why no flake-size claim appears in this report.

C1 · pinned closure accepted

The converged Robin form: the surface rides at the column's last firing temperature, so the flux is h (Tgas − Tfire) minus losses, mesh-independent in one dimension to 0.6 % per halving. Every later step uses it.

The remaining factor of four between 6.3 m/h and Meier came from the area, not the physics. Meier's 2.47 cm³/s of rock removed over a hole 85 to 93 mm wide is 1.3 to 1.6 m/h, and a flux of about 0.5 MW/m² over that face gives exactly the observed volume rate with no adjustment. That reading, made on 15 September, set the programme: replace the fixed flux by an impinging-jet source, replace the prescribed feed by the burner on its feet, and see whether the rate and the hole come out.

5. The packets, one by one

Each entry gives the question, what was run, the result, and what it changed. Verdicts are those of the reviewer session. Dates are 2026.

5.1 D2a · A jet that varies in space accepted

16 September · code + study

Question. Replace the uniform flux by an impinging-jet source whose h and Tgas depend on radius and stand-off, with the nozzle descending on a prescribed path, and see what hole forms.

What was built. Parser expressions for h(r, s) and Tgas(s) evaluated once per column per step, the nozzle path, a collision abort, and reason counters for the pinned closure. Martin's correlation was checked against Zuckerman and Lior. Three anchors were carried: Martin's own h at 1500 K, and the 5 and 10 kW/m²K values that the literature borrows from Potter Drilling, at 1200 and 1000 K.

Result. Under a prescribed feed the centre rate carries no information about h or Tgas. The pinned centre must absorb ρ cp ΔTfire times the feed, 0.48 MW/m² at 1.5 m/h, so the face simply finds the stand-off that supplies it. Also found: D2a's stand-off-only gas temperature gave potential-core gas to off-axis rock and deposited 21 to 29 kW into the face, above the 20 kW the jet carries.

What it changed. The energy violation forced D2a2 to be inserted before the feet. The "rate is an identity under prescribed feed" finding is why the burner had to rest on the rock before any rate could be scored.

5.2 D2a2 · An energy-conserving gas temperature accepted

16 September · code + study

Question. Make the gas temperature a consequence of what the rock absorbs, so the face can never take more than the jet carries.

What was built. The ring-by-ring enthalpy march of figure 3, with the free-jet centreline decay at the stagnation point, an entrained-mass option, and in-code invariants that abort the run if face power exceeds the cap or the budget fails to close to one part in 10⁹. Rock above the nozzle plane receives no jet flux, a choice flagged in the packet as biasing the hole narrower and justified independently because the correlation has no meaning there.

Result. The pre-registered prediction table had to be rewritten before the runs: the planner's hand solution used a hole criterion that could not be computed (the gas never cools to the firing temperature, it approaches it exponentially), and its entrainment argument had the sign wrong. With a removal-based criterion the two literature anchors gave 80 to 110 mm holes at 1.3 to 3 m/h and Martin's was the slowest. The march also showed that where the gas is spent and where it drills are different places.

What it changed. Every later run uses this march. The prediction-table episode is the first of several in which a hand model written before the runs was corrected by the reviewer's independent integration, which is the pre-registration system working as intended.

5.3 D2b · The burner on its feet accepted

16 to 17 September · code + study

Question. Let the burner rest on the rock and see whether a rate and a hole diameter emerge that match Meier.

What was built. The feet rule (figure 3), with three pads on the annulus read from the drawings, a nearest-rank quantile of the pad rock height, and exhaust recirculation into the jet. The drawings were fetched and read to unblock this step: tube 80 mm outer and 56 mm inner diameter, feet 50 mm below the nozzle, three slots.

Result. With Martin's h and 1900 K, untuned, the burner drilled at 1.36 m/h, inside Meier's band, with a firing temperature rise of 528 K inside Rauenzahn's measured range and a minimum hole diameter of 85 mm. But the depth-mean diameter was 104 mm against 85 to 93, the volume rate inside 93 mm was 3.27 cm³/s against 2.47, and there was no steady state: the stand-off correlation was clamped at twelve nozzle diameters, so the centre pit deepened without limit. Runs at a fixed 293 K entrainment temperature stalled after a centimetre; a 1436 K nozzle gave 0.67 m/h.

What it changed. The first untuned rate in the band, and the first statement of the two problems that persist to the end of the week: a funnel at the mouth and a needle pit at the centre. Also the first mesh check on the feet rule, trimmed to 60 mm radius, which passed and was later shown to have been too short.

5.4 D2c · Closures for a steady state, pre-registered accepted

17 September · code only

What was built. Five default-off closures for the gas side: a momentum-based decay diameter that grows with the exhaust temperature, a far-field power law beyond twelve diameters instead of the clamp, free-surface dilution of the jet with room air while the mouth is open, an exact exponential bin update, signed rock-to-gas flux, and a mechanical clearance rule that cut any pad rock standing proud of the pad height. A hand model was written and its predictions for the campaign hashed before any run: a steady ring rate of 1.93 m/h, a stand-off of 180 mm, and the statement that a 0.40 m domain would stop before reaching steady state.

What it changed. The Meier reference file gained two errata from a full reread of the thesis: the nozzle bore is 7.5 mm in the drawings, and the thermocouple value must not be quoted as a gas temperature.

5.5 D2d · The scored campaign accepted mesh fail

17 to 18 September · seven runs, 15 h

Question. Score the model against Meier under the pre-registered criteria, sweep the nozzle temperature, isolate the funnel, and check the mesh on the full domain.

Result. The scored 2 mm run found a steady window from 470 to 694 s with a rate of 1.65 m/h (pass), a whole-hole volume rate of 2.59 cm³/s against 2.47 (pass), a firing rise of 528 K, and a settled stand-off of 172 mm inside the predicted 160 to 200 mm band. The mouth was 130 and 114 mm wide at 25 and 50 mm depth against Meier's 92 and 88 (fail), the depth-mean diameter 101 mm (fail), and the minimum diameter down to the feet 77.6 mm. Free-surface dilution closed about half the mouth excess. The nozzle sweep gave 0.75, 1.29 and 1.50 m/h at 1600, 1750 and 1900 K. Eleven of sixteen pre-registered predictions held.

And then the mesh. The 1 mm run drilled 43 % faster over the matched window, a gap that opened from 5 % in the first 50 s and grew every block. Every 2 mm number above became conditional on understanding that.

What it changed. This is where the week turned. A diagnosis written the same night from the logs identified a seed and an amplifier for the mesh gap (next section), and the planner's ranking of open physics was set aside until the mesh question was closed.

5.6 D2e · The first mesh seed: a clearance clip accepted

18 September · perf + three controlled pairs

Question. Which closure reads cell-scale geometry? Three controlled 2 mm/1 mm pairs, each switching one thing, with predictions hashed first.

What was found. The gas side was identical between meshes to 150 s (ring temperatures within 2 K), so bin radii, stand-off and the pinned-share criterion were not the seed. Reconstructing the feet rule from the removal log showed that the pad height was always set by the outermost 2 mm band of the pads, and that the burner's rate was that band's spall rate plus the mechanical clip from D2c, which removed a whole cell that was part-way to firing. A 2 mm clip discards most of a 3 mm thermal layer; a 1 mm clip discards a third. So the rim restarted warmer at 1 mm and ran faster, and a feedback through the recirculating exhaust amplified it. The pair with the clip switched off closed the gap to 0.1 % over the matched window.

Also in this step. A profile of the 1 mm run found that 45 % of the time went into the conduction kernel and a fifth into ghost-cell fills; five bit-exact code fixes made the campaign binary 2.8 times faster, verified on the 2639-file reference set.

What it changed. The user stopped the remaining pairs and ruled, from a reread of chapter 8, that the burner rests on the highest rock under its feet and that the clip and the quantile were workarounds. Every D2b to D2d Meier number was declared superseded once the rule changed.

5.7 D2f · The support rule as Meier ran it accepted

18 to 19 September · six runs to 300 s

Question. With the clip retired, does the burner rate converge, and how much does it depend on the quantile that defines "the highest rock"?

Result. Barely at all: 1.535, 1.491 and 1.448 m/h at quantiles 0.8, 0.9 and 1.0, a ±3 % uncertainty to carry. The 0.9-quantile pair was converged to 300 s (window −2.1 %, blocks within 4.3 %) but trending, with an unexplained late slowdown of the 1 mm rim that was opposite in sign to D2e's amplifier. The hole at 300 s was unchanged by the support rule: the funnel is a gas-side problem.

What it changed. The reviewer required a mesh check beyond 300 s before any scored run, which became D2g-1. The 1 mm runs of this step were paused overnight with a stop signal rather than killed, a practice kept for the rest of the week.

5.8 D2g-1 · The long gate accepted gate failed

19 to 20 September · one pair to the bottom of the domain

Result. Over 150 to 573 s the 2 mm burner drilled at 1.493 m/h and the 1 mm burner at 1.067 m/h, a −28.5 % gap that grew by 22 points per 100 s. The centre kept pace between meshes; what failed was the ring under the pads, whose spall rate at 1 mm fell from 24 to 8 mm/min while at 2 mm it held. Everything scored in D2b to D2d had been read at times where the pair was 40 to 65 % apart.

What it changed. No Meier number could be quoted, and the planned rescoring (D2g Stage 2) was blocked. Reading the end-of-run fields showed that the silence at 1 mm began in the bands nearest the nozzle plane and walked inward, and that the pinned closure's idle clock, which drops a column back to the plain face balance if it has not fired for two cell-heating times, was mesh-scaled. That became the next diagnostic.

5.9 D2h · Three key-only pairs reviewer diagnostic

20 September · six runs to 350 s, no packet

What was run. The D2f rule with, in turn, the idle clock switched off, the exhaust recirculation replaced by a fixed 1600 K, and the burner path prescribed. Criterion hashed before launch.

Result. With the clock off, the rate gap over 250 to 350 s fell from −12.5 % to −4.0 % and the silent pad columns at 1 mm went from 9 % to none: the clock was a real mesh mechanism. The fixed recirculation changed nothing, which answered D2e's untested amplifier question. The prescribed-path pair aborted on a collision guard whose default was wrong for that mode; it was fixed but not rerun, because by then the question had moved on.

What it changed. The idle clock is a threshold rather than a dial, and was set per input rather than changed in the source. The gate was rerun with it off.

5.10 D2i · The gate again, clock off accepted gate failed

20 to 21 September · three runs, 8 h

Result. Matched window 150 to 572 s: −13.9 %, with 100 s blocks at −2.1, −4.0, −16.3 and −36.1 %. The divergence was delayed by about 150 s and its slope halved, but it still ran away, with zero idle columns at either mesh in every block. So a second, slower mechanism with the same signature sat underneath: the ring stalls at 1 mm while the centre keeps pace.

The reading at the time. The silent pad columns at 1 mm were hot, 815 to 829 K, sitting a few kelvin below their own firing temperature. The pinned flux of about 550 kW/m² was balanced by downward conduction of about 300 and a lateral loss into the cold cone beside the rim of about 300 at 2 mm and 400 at 1 mm, proportional to one over the cell width. A per-column threshold at a lateral hot-cold edge sits on a knife-edge that the mesh decides. That reading was correct as far as it went; what it missed was that the same edge exists everywhere, not only at the pad rim.

What it changed. The gate loop was stopped. A comparison statement was written for what could be claimed (section 6), and a design for a continuous ablation front was drafted, then parked, pending a test of whether the edge alone was the cause.

5.11 D2j-0 · A hot disk on flat rock accepted verdict later withdrawn

21 September · six runs, 150 s

Question. Strip everything away. A fixed Robin disk (h = 700 W/m²K, 1600 K, 30 mm radius) on flat rock, no jet, no feet, no descent, at 4, 2 and 1 mm, with a sharp and a smoothly tapered edge. Does a per-column threshold at a lateral edge produce a growing, mesh-dependent stall front by itself?

Result as scored. A stalled ring forms at the edge at every mesh, but its width fraction does not grow with refinement (7.0, 6.7, 7.4 %), and the transition width converges (5.7, 12.5, 14.4 mm). Sharp and tapered edges agree to a fraction of a percent, so the edge is set by lateral conduction, not by the sharpness of h. The disk-mean rate did fall by about 10 % per halving, which the results called a standing offset. Verdict: the edge is bounded, do not redesign the front, test the burner feedback next.

Why the verdict did not survive the evening. A free check on the D2i logs showed that each ring band had stopped firing 5 to 50 mm below the nozzle plane, and 9 to 60 s after the band outside it had risen above the plane. Bands were not being switched off by the plane; they were being starved by their newly exposed neighbour. That is a cascade, and a 150 s static disk had not been given time to show one.

5.12 D2j-0b · The disk under a descending plane reviewer diagnostic

21 September, afternoon · eleven runs in four hours

Question. Same disk, in a deeper box, run to 250 s, with a prescribed plane 20 mm above the surface descending at the centre rate. Vary only what happens to rock that rises above the plane: nothing (C0), the exclusion rule as coded (C1), a warm wall at 100 W/m²K and 1000 K (C2), an exhaust-like wall at 1600 K (C3). Predictions hashed before launch: C1 would cascade like D2i, C0 would not, and C2 would not, which would justify heating the wall above the plane as the fix.

Result. C1 reproduced the D2i cascade exactly, with no feet and no jet: every band stopped 7 to 15 s before its own face crossed the plane, 5 mm below it, 21 to 36 s after the outer band crossed at 2 mm and 8 to 15 s at 1 mm. The 1 mm to 2 mm gap grew −13.5, −28.7, −45.5 % by block. C2 and C3 were identical to C1 band for band at both meshes. And C0 cascaded too, three times slower, with its gap growing −10.4, −15.3, −20.1 %. Of the four predictions, only "C1 cascades" held.

The mechanism, read from the temperature fields. A firing top cell at about 800 K sits beside a taller column. The cell it conducts into laterally is that column's interior at the step depth, 10 to 20 mm below the exposed surface, and it is at 400 to 560 K in every variant, because no surface heating at 100 W/m²K reaches that deep in thirty seconds. The loss through that face is k ΔT / dx: about 240 kW/m² at 2 mm and 480 at 1 mm against a pinned budget of 520. Once the step is taller than the 1.5 mm thermal layer, the wall is at bulk temperature at any mesh and the loss diverges as one over the cell width. The column stalls, cools, and becomes the next wall. The exclusion rule only makes the first stall hard and cold early; the same cascade runs without it. Wall surface heating cannot reach the sink. This is the continuum re-entrant corner, which in real rock would spall, represented in a model whose vertical faces cannot.

Three more things this diagnostic settled. A pre-launch audit of every kernel on the path found no bug that would bias the mesh comparison. The existing slope-aware spall kernel, switched on as an exploratory pair with "no effect" predicted in writing, was inert to the second. And the planner's suggestion that the half-cell removal increment might be a second mesh mechanism was closed from data already on disk: before any band stalls, the 1 mm to 2 mm rate ratio is 0.996 to 1.007 everywhere the local slope is below 0.7 and falls to 0.53 only where the slope reaches 8, so the whole offset lives in the tall-step regime and none in the flat interior.

What it changed. Three proposals were withdrawn on evidence: wall heating above the plane (the reviewer's own), the open-loop burner pair, and D2j-0's bounded-edge verdict. The design for a continuous front became a fallback rather than the plan. The next packet was written the same evening.

5.13 D2k · Flux on exposed vertical faces arbiter pass Meier gate fail, inconclusive

21 to 22 September · the week's only source change

What was built. Two default-off keys. A solid cell inside the patch with a void lateral neighbour receives, on each exposed face, f times the flux its column's top face would receive, computed by the same routine, so the surface-temperature cap, the exclusion above the nozzle plane and the losses come for free. The side power is drawn from the enthalpy march's budget, not added to it, which required an exact sparse gather across the domain decomposition; the budget was verified to 0.1 W. The factor f corrects the staircase area, which overstates a sloped wall by up to 41 % at 45°; it was pre-registered at 1.0 with 0.7 as a sensitivity and never fitted. The 2639-file reference set came back byte-identical with the key off, and a twelve-check unit test passed with the energy ledger closing to 2 × 10⁻¹⁴.

The arbiter. The D2j-0b C1 cascade with the key on. The 1 mm to 2 mm disk-mean gap went from −13.5, −28.7, −45.5 % to +3.4, +1.5, −3.2 %. No band stalled at either mesh; every band was still firing at 250 s, 22 mm below the plane. The term drew 7 % of the face power and f = 0.7 moved the result by 0.8 %. The mechanism identified the day before was real, and heating the step faces removes it.

The Meier pair. Run because the arbiter passed, under a criterion and a pre-registered direction hashed first: side heating should lower the centre rate a little and widen the hole 5 to 15 mm. Neither held. The burner rate rose 2.7 times, to 4.07 m/h against D2i's 1.49, and both legs drilled out of the 0.40 m domain before the 350 to 550 s window where D2i diverged, so the gate failed by the rule on a block that measured the bottom of the box and was inconclusive on its own question. The rise is not a budget leak: the march's face power equals the ledger's to 0.1 W and the cap was never approached. The jet was area-limited, not enthalpy-limited. In the Meier geometry the pit and the 80° cone under the pads are mostly vertical faces, several times the horizontal area, and the rule gives them the full stagnation-impingement h. Side faces took 39 % of face power at 2 mm and absorbed power doubled; the rate rose more than the power because removing the sink also converts more of what is absorbed into recession. Compared at the same feet height the hole is only 1.4 to 4 mm wider; the side term is a feet-descent accelerator, not a hole-widener.

What it changed. The mesh divergence that blocked the programme for four days has a demonstrated remedy on the controlled test. What remains is a heat-transfer closure question, how an impinging jet heats a near-vertical wall far along its path, and that is a physics decision to derive and pre-register, not a factor to tune.

5.14 AMR on the thermal and removal path side session, not yet reviewed

22 September, overnight

A separate session relaxed the single-level restrictions so that the finest level owns the surface and a refinement band follows each column's top cell, with a repair of the level set after regridding. On the D2j-0b C1 case a whole-domain level-1 run was byte-identical to uniform 1 mm at 60 s, and a 20 mm band run matched uniform 1 mm within 1.4 % at 250 s with 3.3 times fewer cells. It has not yet been run with the feet, the jet or the side-face budget, the energy ledger aborts under it, the reference set has not been rerun on the working tree, and the wall time was the same as the uniform run. It is a correct start and is being treated as its own packet with its own gates before it goes near a scored run.

Two statements follow. On rate: with every input taken from the experiment, the drawings or a literature correlation and nothing tuned, the model drills Meier's rock at Meier's rate to within the mesh and support-rule uncertainty, on a transient window, not a steady state. On shape and volume: the model makes the right diameter only in a band around 100 to 125 mm depth; above it a funnel, below it a needle pit, and the volume excess is that funnel. That is a finding about the gas closures.

The uncertainty budget on the rate is: mesh −4 % (this window), support-rule quantile ±3 %, time step under 1 %, domain height +4 %, and the nozzle temperature, which dominates and has not been re-swept under the current rule; D2d's sweep under the old rule moved the rate by about 0.25 m/h per 100 K, so the a priori 1600 to 1950 K bracket spans roughly 0.8 to 1.6 m/h.

What may be claimed: an untuned, mesh-checked rate on a stated window; a firing temperature in the measured band; exact energy closure; a specific, diagnosed shape disagreement that localises the model error in the gas closures; and a documented convergence limit with its cause now understood and a remedy demonstrated on a controlled test. What may not: a steady state; a converged long-time rate; a mouth pass or fail; any number from D2b to D2d; a nozzle-temperature range; any flake-size or size-effect claim.

7. What the week taught about method

Most of these were learned the hard way and are recorded so the next week does not repeat them.

  • Two meshes over a short window prove nothing. D2b's trimmed pair, D2f's 300 s pair, D2h's 350 s pair and D2j-0's 150 s disk all "converged" and all diverged later. The gate now requires every 100 s block, and any candidate fix will get a third mesh once.
  • Read the fields, not only the rates. The pad-rim clip, the idle clock and finally the step sink were each found by reconstructing per-column histories from the removal log and by looking at temperature slices at the end of runs, not from the scored numbers.
  • Pre-registration earned its keep. The D2a2 prediction table, the D2j-0 verdict, the D2j-0b wall-heating prediction and the D2k direction all failed in writing. Each failure pointed somewhere specific, which is what a prediction is for.
  • Strip the problem down before adding physics. The Meier configuration has the feet, the jet, recirculation, dilution and exclusion all coupled. The cause was found in a day on a disk with none of them, after four days of controlled pairs on the full configuration.
  • Compare shapes at matched geometry, never at matched time. D2k's hole looked 30 mm wider at the same time because it had drilled three times further; at the same feet height it was 4 mm wider.
  • Small operational rules that saved runs: every launch under a no-sleep assertion after a laptop nap lost a night's work; kill by binary path and plot-file name, never by pattern; read progress from the removal log, not the buffered thermo file; keep every earlier criterion, harness and output frozen and import them by path.

8. What comes next

  1. A wall heat-transfer closure for the side faces. The rule as written gives a vertical face deep in the pit the stagnation-point h, though it sits far along the wall-jet path. The parameter-free correction is to evaluate h at the path coordinate, radius plus the face's height above the neighbouring floor, which reduces to the current rule for a one-cell step. Pre-register it, run the D2j-0b arbiter again, then the Meier pair in a 0.70 m domain that can reach the window where D2i diverged.
  2. A third mesh on the fixed configuration, 0.5 mm on the arbiter, once, overnight.
  3. AMR as its own packet with four gates: reference-set identity on the working tree, identity under whole-domain refinement on the Meier configuration, the band gate against the existing 1 mm leg, and an energy ledger that closes on two levels. And a speed target before it is used.
  4. Then the rescoring: the Meier pair under the support rule with the wall closure, a nozzle-temperature sweep on one basis, the one-sided 1.30 m/h reading with the wellhead stated as an uncertainty on the mouth, and the shape comparison at the feet depth.
  5. The continuous ablation front stays the named fallback if the side-face approach does not converge on the full geometry, and only together with the side-face flux, since a cold wall never reaches the ablation isotherm.
