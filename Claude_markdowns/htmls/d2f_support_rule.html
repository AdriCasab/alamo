The burner rests on the highest rock under its feet — and the rate barely depends on how strictly

Outcome (b), as pre-registered: the next scored Meier run (D2g) uses the pad rule at quantile q = 0.9 with no clip. The quantile hardly changes the rate (±2.9 % over q = 0.8–1.0). The strict maximum (q = 1.0) passes the mesh check over the matched window (−3.1 %) but fails one 50 s block (−9.7 %). q = 0.9 converges to 300 s (−2.1 %).

All runs are diagnostics on the 0.40 m domain to 300 s. No rate here is compared with Meier's band, and hole shapes are direction only.

Why this step

D2e found that the whole mesh failure of D2d (1 mm drilling 43 % faster than 2 mm) came from the burner-body clip. The clip cut rock standing inside the feet in whole cells, a numerical workaround with no physical counterpart. Rereading Meier's Ch. 8 settled the physical rule. The burner hangs on tensioned coil tubing, advances by gravity, and its feet keep the nozzle 50 mm off the rock (pp. 209–212). It can only descend as fast as the highest rock touching its feet clears (hook load, p. 221), and the operator's jolts keep it free (pp. 219–220).

The model's form of that rule is the per-pad quantile with no clip. q = 1.0 is the literal "highest rock". A high quantile such as 0.9 allows for the jolts riding over isolated high points. D2f tests two gates, fixed before any run: (i) does q = 1.0 converge between 2 mm and 1 mm, and (ii) does q control the rate?

Without the clip, both quantiles converge to within a few per cent, against a gap of +43 % with the clip on. The pre-registered rule also demanded every 50 s block after 50 s within 5 %, and Q10's last block misses. Two things make up that block:

  1. Step quantisation. The burner descends in steps of dz/3, three pads averaged: 0.67 mm at 2 mm. One extra step in a 50 s block is about 3.3 % of its rate, and Q10 at 2 mm took one in 250–300 s.
  2. A late 1 mm slowdown, at both q. After about 250 s the 1 mm runs descend about 1 mm less per block, their outer wall stops widening sooner, and the recirculated gas runs 12–17 K hotter. This is not D2e's feedback loop, which made 1 mm faster. Whether it grows is unknown at 300 s.

The support rule does not change the shape. q moves the wall by 2–4 mm, and the clip made no difference. The mouth is still a funnel, +38 mm at 25 mm and +26 mm at 50 mm, and the wall above the nozzle plane (about 75 mm at 300 s) is final. The funnel is a gas-side problem. It is also a weaker target than it looked, because Meier's mouth sat inside a sealed, water-cooled wellhead, not in open room air.

What D2g inherits

  1. Support rule: pads, q = 0.9, no clip. This is contact with the highest rock under each foot, within the jolt allowance.
  2. A longer mesh check. The late 1 mm drift must be followed past 300 s (1 mm to 450–600 s, about 3–4 h) before the scored run is trusted.
  3. The rate is one-sided. The rule models the contact limit. Meier's operator advanced at or below it, averaging 0.5 m in 1383 s = 1.30 m/h (the text quotes 1.5).
  4. Open, gas-side: the mouth under the wellhead, the two drilling phases, the outer-flank gas reach and the rim's exhaust slots.
