Meier flame-jet test: where the model stands

The model now drills a hole at Meier's measured rate with no tuning, but the hole is the wrong shape at the top, and the rate changes by 43 % when the mesh is refined. This page explains how we got there and what the numbers mean.

In short

  • Rates pass. The scored run reaches a steady state and descends at 1.65 m/h, inside Meier's band (1.04–1.92). The volume removed, 2.59 cm³/s, and the energy balance are also in band.
  • Shape fails at the mouth. The top of the hole flares to 130 mm, against 92 mm in the sawn block. Deeper down the diameter is close.
  • The mesh check fails. At 1 mm cells the burner drills 43 % faster than at 2 mm. So the rate agreement above is not yet a converged result, and fixing this comes before any new physics.
  • Predictions mostly held. Of the hand-model predictions written and hashed before the campaign, 11 held, 1 was refuted and 4 were not tested.

1. The experiment we are matching

Meier (ETH thesis, 2017, Ch. 8) drilled a 660 kg Grimsel granite block with a flame jet. The burner rests on three feet (an 80 mm tube), so it can only go down as fast as the rock under its feet is removed. The flame leaves a supersonic nozzle (6 mm throat, 7.5 mm exit) 50 mm above the feet.

One input is not known: the gas temperature at the nozzle. Meier's thermocouple sat upstream of the flame, so it does not measure gas temperature. The adiabatic flame temperature, 1900 K, is an upper bound, and the unreported cooling-water loss would lower it. We therefore score at 1900 K and treat lower temperatures as a sweep, never as a fitted input.

2. How the model got here

The model removes rock column by column when the surface cell reaches a firing temperature. The flame heats the rock through a heat-transfer coefficient h from an impinging-jet correlation (Martin), with the gas temperature Tgas falling as the jet gives up heat along the wall. Each step below fixed a specific failure of the one before.

D2b already hit Meier's rate (1.36 m/h), but only in a transient: the centre of the hole kept running away because h was held constant beyond 12 nozzle diameters. D2c replaced that with a decay, h ∝ (12 D / s), where s is the distance from the nozzle. Before running anything, the implementer wrote hand-model predictions for every run and fixed them with a SHA-256 hash, so the campaign could be checked honestly afterwards.

The steady window is real but marginal: the drift in the centre stand-off over the window is 0.0199 mm/s, against a limit of 0.02.

4. The hole shape: a funnel at the mouth

Below about 200 mm the model hole sits within a few millimetres of Meier's. The problem is the top 100 mm, where the hole flares out. Air dilution at the free surface (R1 against R6) narrows the mouth from 167 to 130 mm at 25 mm depth, which closes roughly half the gap to Meier. The rest remains.

The most likely cause is heating of the side wall: the upper wall sees hot exhaust for the whole run, and the model's wall-jet h there is probably too high. That is a hypothesis, not yet tested.

5. Why the steady state exists: the far-field law

The far-field law controls the centre of the hole, not the burner. R2, which keeps the old constant h, never levels off: its centre reaches 0.36 m depth at 384 s, against 566 s for R1. Yet the burner rate barely changes (1.41 against 1.50 m/h over the same early window). The extra heat just goes into a deeper centre pit, so the removed volume rises by about a quarter.

6. Nozzle temperature sweep

Runs at 1600, 1750 and 1900 K give 0.75, 1.29 and 1.50 m/h. Read as an inference, Meier's 1.3–1.6 m/h corresponds to a nozzle temperature from about 1760 K to above 1900 K, given the scored closures and the 2 mm mesh. It is not used as an input. The 1 mm mesh drills faster, so a converged curve would put this range lower.

The 1600 K run is the one refuted prediction. The hand model said 0.91–1.57 m/h. At 1600 K the gas is only 150–200 K hotter than the firing temperature, so the face fires, stalls and fires again (the green trace in the ROP figure). The hand model assumes continuous firing, so it cannot represent that.

7. The mesh check

Both runs use a 0.40 m domain with identical settings except the cell size. Over 150–300 s the 1 mm burner descends at 2.12 m/h against 1.49 m/h, and the gap grows steadily: +13 % by 50–100 s, +26 % by 100–150 s. At 1 mm the rate is already above Meier's band and still climbing when the run stops.

Some part of the jet model reads the cell size. The candidates are the radial bins the gas march uses, the way the centre stand-off is taken from the top solid cell, the clearance band under the feet, and the rule for which surface cells are held at the firing temperature. The campaign does not say which, and finding it is the next task.

The 1 mm run was stopped at 300 s to save about six hours. That still leaves a valid matched window, and the gap is already over 5 % before the window opens.

9. What comes next

  1. Mesh convergence first. Find which closure depends on the cell size and make it converge. Until then every 2 mm number above, including the rate pass, is provisional.
  2. Then the shape residual, ranked by what the results suggest:
    1. side-wall heating;
    2. the recovery temperature of the recirculated gas;
    3. a supersonic correction to Martin's stagnation h;
    4. pad shielding under the feet;
    5. temperature-dependent gas heat capacity, the smallest.
  3. Housekeeping. A speed-up made during the campaign changed the source and the binary. It was checked as byte-identical on short runs, but it should pass the full reference hash check before new runs use it.

Caveats that apply throughout

  • Martin's correlation is outside its validity range for most in-hole positions, and the real jet is supersonic.
  • Flame heat acts on horizontal cell faces only; there is no separate side-wall heating term.
  • The Weibull size effect is switched off (reference volume = cell volume), so the results say nothing about size scaling.
  • Several run states are not checkpointed, so a restarted run is not the same run. No result here comes from a restart.
