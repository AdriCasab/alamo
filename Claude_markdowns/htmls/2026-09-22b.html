The Undercut Problem

Why the model drills a cone when Meier drilled a cylinder — and why fixing it meant changing how the code stores a hole before touching any physics.

We are validating a thermal-spallation drilling model against Meier's 2017 Grimsel pilot: a flame burner resting on tripod feet, melting and spalling its way down a granite borehole. The model gets the drilling rate roughly right. It gets the shape badly wrong, and shape is the only thing we can currently score without circularity.

This page explains the chain of investigation that followed, and what was actually changed in the code today.

To widen the hole, rock at the wall has to be removed. Everything below is the story of why it could not be.

D2j-0b found the cause

A hot cell beside a cold column bleeds heat sideways

The wall of a drilled hole is a staircase of cells. When one column fires and recedes, its new top cell sits directly beside the buried interior of the taller column next door — rock that is still at bulk temperature. Heat runs laterally across that face, and the model never heats it back.

The flux is k(T_hot − T_cold)/dx. Because the neighbour stays at bulk temperature at any resolution once the step is taller than the thermal length, the temperature difference is pinned and the flux density scales as 1/dx — so refining the mesh makes it worse, not better.

The drain

A firing top cell (orange) loses heat into the cold buried interior of the taller column (blue). That face is never heated back.

The removal that cannot be expressed

The wall column is heated at its base, by that same drain. Removing the cell that got the heat would leave solid sitting above void — an undercut.

Every sharp corner generates the same cascade

We tried prescribing a floor/wall split in the heat-transfer coefficient directly. A corner placed at the stand-off froze the burner's load-bearing feet and jammed the tool. Moving the corner to a radius cured the freeze — and then the corner itself became the drain.

The temperature step was mesh-independent while the sink it drove doubled from 2 mm to 1 mm — the 1/dx signature again. The general finding: no sharp corner in the heating law works, radial or stand-off.

D2p-0 the blocker

The drain is not a loss — it is heat we cannot spend

This was the reframing that changed the target. The heat running into the wall is being delivered exactly where the wall ought to spall. The problem is not that we lose it; it is that the representation has nowhere to put the result.

Three facts in the code closed the packet at design time, before anything was run:

  • There is no per-cell firing criterion — the spall field is written only at each column's top cell.
  • Void was derived from a per-column level set, linear down the column. Solid above void was arithmetically impossible.
  • A side face is heated with its own column's coefficient.

Every lateral rule we tried failed at removal, not at triggering. "Fire on the lateral face" is a no-op: the criterion already fires there. The rule had nowhere to write its answer.

D2p-1 refuted

The cheap way around it does not exist

Rather than fix the representation, we tried regularising the lateral gradient over a physical length instead of over the cell width. It was abandoned before launch for two independent reasons, both provable from numbers already on disk.

1 · The length was wrong by a factor of two

The project had been quoting κ/v ≈ 3.2 mm. Recomputed from the run's own inputs (κ = 1.5, ρc = 2.1725×10⁶, α = 6.9045×10⁻⁷ m²/s) it is 1.604 mm. The old figure came from textbook granite. At 1.604 mm the correction is inert at a 2 mm mesh — it inverts the meaning of the test.

2 · Two acceptance criteria could not both be met

The gate asked for mesh convergence and a ceiling on removal. But the cascade suppresses the fine-mesh rate, so closing the gap requires raising it by +74 % to +93 % — while the ceiling allowed +15 %. The windows do not overlap.

D2q-1 shipped

What was actually changed today

With three routes closed, one remained: make the representation able to hold an undercut. The removal mask removed_mf already existed as a real field; it was simply not authoritative. Six edits across two integrator files make it so.

The level set survives, demoted: it still carries the sub-cell recession remainder that decides when the next cell flips, which is why it could not simply be deleted. But no decision path asks it whether a cell is void.

The gate

2639 / 2639 output files byte-identical, 11/11 runs clean, 7/7 named regressions passing, energy ledger at round-off (≤ 5.3×10⁻¹⁵). Run in three bisectable batches — which paid for itself when one batch failed and isolated to a single site in one sweep instead of leaving the whole refactor under suspicion.

Byte-identity was chosen as the only acceptance criterion deliberately. Most of the risk in per-cell void lives in plumbing — regrid, beam path, depth scan, ledger — and plumbing has a perfect oracle. A mistake shows up as a broken hash in twelve minutes rather than as a wrong physics result diagnosed over a multi-day campaign.

What this does not do

It changes nothing observable. That was the point — 2639 files identical. It creates no interior void, spalls no wall, and widens no hole. It removes a representational blocker so that the next step can be attempted at all.

Two things were escalated, not forced

  • The surface mask stays level-set-derived. Converting it changed 24 files and moved the trajectory. My pre-check said it was safe; the gate refuted it. The counter I checked is blind in the direction that actually bites.
  • The new unit test is unsatisfiable as specified. It needs an interior void, and the only two void-producing sites in the codebase are both column-based and top-down — which is the very premise being fixed.

The known trap ahead

The next step adds the per-cell criterion and lateral removal. The danger is obvious from the numbers: the model already removes 1.9× too much rock, and Meier's wall needs only +2.5 to 7.5 mm per side. A fix that converges by drilling faster is not a fix — and the ceiling must be measured against the artefact-free control, never against a cascade-suppressed run. That error is what killed the previous attempt.
