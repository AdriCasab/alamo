# 2026-09-18c: How Meier ran the pilot test, checked against the model

Planner note. Source: Meier (2017) PhD thesis, Ch. 8, read in full, with the
Fig. 8.1 wellhead view rendered. The file is `~/Downloads/ThierryMeier_PhD.pdf`
(PDF page = printed page + 27). This note checks the D2f support rule and
lists where the model differs from the experiment. Nothing here was run. The
D2f packet is unchanged.

## 2. What the D2f packet says that is inference, not Meier

- **"Jolts."** This is our word for the "constant axial acceleration" and
  "intermittent and rough axial displacement". The claim that the motion
  clears isolated high rock under the feet, which justifies a per-pad
  quantile, is our interpretation.
- **"Gas reached under and around the rim."** This is inferred from the hole
  being wider than the Ø 80 tube. Meier does not say it.
- **"Wider than the tube at every depth."** This is overstated.
  - Tab. 8.2 gives 85 mm, and the text says the drill controls the smallest
    hole diameter.
  - The digitised Fig. 8.8 visible width tapers to 80 mm at 450 mm depth and
    78 mm at the exit.
  - These are lower bounds (the saw cut need not pass through the axis), so
    they neither contradict nor support "every depth".

The D2f gate (mesh convergence of q = 1.0, and q-insensitivity) is an internal
model check and is unaffected. The packet wording should mark these as
interpretation.

## 3. Discrepancies between the model and the experiment

1. **The mouth was inside a sealed, water-cooled wellhead (largest).**
   - Fig. 8.1: concentric cylinders on a baseplate, glued to the rock, with
     the burner starting inside and exhaust leaving by a side port to the
     cyclone.
   - The inner bore looks about twice the burner diameter. That is a visual
     estimate; no dimension is given, and the burner drawings do not include
     the wellhead. Most of the modelled top surface (quarter domain to
     r = 0.12 m) sat under it.
   - In phase 1 the gas over the rock surface was **confined exhaust cooled
     by steel walls**, not 293 K room air.
   - Two consequences:
     - D2c's free-surface dilution assumes ambient entrainment over an open
       surface, and D2d credited it with about half the mouth fix.
     - The wellhead also caps how wide any funnel can grow, which may be
       part of why Fig. 8.8 shows none.
   - Affects: the funnel diagnosis, the mouth score, and the D2c scored gas
     configuration.
2. **Two phases, the second under suction.**
   - Phase 2 restarted in a cooled, part-drilled hole, at about 0.25 m if
     phase 1 advanced at 1.3–1.5 m/h. The thesis does not give the depth.
   - Phase 2 ran with the hole in rough vacuum and room air drawn in through
     the burner–wellhead gap. Phase 1 had no such suction, and exhaust left
     by the wellhead port and the gap.
   - Our model is one continuous run at atmospheric pressure, so the
     entrainment near the top matches neither phase.
3. **The thesis's ROP figures disagree with each other.**
   - 0.5 m in 647 + 736 = 1383 s is **1.30 m/h**, not 1.5.
   - 38 kW × 1383 s / 3.42 L = 15.4 J/mm³, the stated TSE, which confirms
     1383 s was the drilling time.
   - The 1.5 m/h may be the slope of position vs time during steady advance.
   - The scoring band 1.3–1.6 covers both, but results should quote 1.30 m/h
     as the time average and state the inconsistency.
4. **The measured ROP is an operator choice at or below the contact limit.**
   - The operator lowered the burner by crane and read the hook load, and a
     slower advance gave more clearance.
   - The model's contact rule keeps the burner as low as the rock allows, so
     it is an **upper bound** on the rate Meier could have run.
   - Consistent outcomes: a model ROP at or somewhat above 1.3–1.5 m/h, with
     a hole at or somewhat narrower than 85–93 mm near the bottom.
   - A model ROP **below** the measurement is a real miss. The ROP score
     should be read one-sided.
5. **The cooling-water ΔT was instrumented but not reported.** The request to
   Meier is for data he probably recorded. It is the one number that would
   bound the nozzle temperature from an energy balance (his §4.2 method).
6. **Already known, now more relevant:**
   - Above the feet, exhaust flows up the gap between the Ø 67 mantle and the
     Ø 85–93 wall. That is side-wall heating the model lacks, and it
     plausibly sets the clearance above the feet.
   - Nozzle bore 7.1 mm (text) vs 7.5 mm (drawing sheet 14).
   - Burner length and weight 850 mm / 15 kg (text) vs 797 mm / ≈ 14 kg
     (drawing).
   - The 1 MPa lateral confinement is not modelled.

## 4. What this means for the next packets

- **D2f:** the gate is unchanged. Only its wording needs correcting (§2).
- **D2g must decide, before scoring:**
  - **ROP:** a one-sided reading (the model is a contact limit). Quote
    1.30 m/h as the time-averaged rate and state the thesis's 1.5.
  - **Gas over the surface:** keep D2c's ambient free-surface dilution, or
    model a confined, cooled wellhead chamber. The chamber option needs the
    wellhead bore, either estimated from Fig. 8.1 or asked of Meier.
  - **The mouth target:** Fig. 8.8's top region was shaped under the
    wellhead and across two phases with opposite top-flow conditions. So a
    mouth pass or fail is weaker evidence than it looked.
- **Questions for Meier (one message):**
  - the cooling-water ΔT or outlet temperature (160 L/h);
  - the wellhead inner bore and height;
  - the depth at the end of phase 1;
  - whether 1.5 m/h is a within-phase slope or includes the break.
