## 0. The decision this brief asks the planner to make

The Meier programme has run two inputs-only studies (D2ac, D2ad) that established WHERE the
model disagrees with the experiment and that the disagreement cannot be closed by scaling or
reshaping the heat input. The user's stated goal is now explicit: **a physically correct model
that reproduces the Meier test**, not a calibrated pass. This brief specifies the source work
that follows from that goal, in the order the physics requires, with a validation case per
item and a pre-registered Meier prediction at the end.

The order is not negotiable in one respect: **P0 (surface temperature) must land before any
flux-dependent flaking temperature is added**, because the present criterion already carries a
numerical flux dependence through the cell size (§2.1). Adding physics on top of it anchors the
new law to a mesh-specific number.

Estimated span: P0–P3 are source packets of 2–5 days each; P4–P5 are runs. Total 3–4 weeks of
agent time. The SGW abstract (due 8 Oct) does not wait for this; its validation sentence uses
the D2ac geometry result and states the rate residual honestly (§8).

## 2. What the flaking model actually computes today (Removal.H, Meier configuration)

Read on 09-30: `src/Integrator/MMWSpalling/Removal.H` :340–470 (setup), :600–1010 (criterion
and scan), :1300–1500 (removal); `src/Numeric/SpCriterion.H` (Tada K_I :60, Nasseri K_Ic :91);
`src/Integrator/MMWSpalling.H` :6480–6620 (flaw draw), :2050–2070 (per-column flame_h).

Configuration in `validation/meier/sp_meier_pilot/input_drilling`: `spallation.model =
sp_weibull`, `spall.stress_source = local_thermoelastic`, `weibull.a0_ig 4e-6`, `weibull.a0_gb
20e-6`, `weibull.m 20`, `weibull.V0 8e-9` (= V_cell at 2 mm), `kic nasseri_table scale 1.0`,
`spall.surface_normal 0`, whole-cell removal (D2z-1), E 30 GPa, ν 0.3, ρ 2750, Cp 790, κ 1.5.

Step by step per column:
1. Column temperature profile from cell centres (gathered, box-independent). **Surface
   temperature = top-cell average** (`T_face = T_top` under local_thermoelastic, Removal.H
   :846–856; the top half-cell is isothermal).
2. σ_xx(z) = E β (T(z) − T_ref)/(1 − ν), compression taken positive (Kant 2017 eq 5–6 sign
   convention), Removal.H :872–891.
3. K_I = Tada edge-crack integral of σ_xx over a flaw of FIXED length a_f (4 µm intragranular,
   20 µm grain boundary, per-cell Weibull draw with m = 20 and (V_cell/V0)^{1/m} = 1),
   SpCriterion.H :60, MMWSpalling.H :6522, :6589–6614.
4. Fire when K_I ≥ K_Ic(T) (Nasseri Westerly table, clamped), Removal.H :931.
5. Scan the translated flaw downward; h_col = deepest depth still passing (an isotherm depth,
   Removal.H :912–990). **Then whole-cell removal discards h_col and voids one cell**
   (Removal.H :1475, D2z-1). Recession is vertical per column.

Net behaviour: an isotherm-ablation model. The isotherm is "top-cell average ≈ 823 K", the
flake is the mesh cell, the energy spent per volume is the removed cell's enthalpy
(≈ 1.35 J/mm³), and the removal rate is set by conduction feeding that isotherm.

### 2.1 The defect that fixes the order of work
With a 2 mm cell and a thermal decay length κ/v ≈ 1.6 mm at the feed rate, the true surface
temperature when the cell average reads 823 K is ≈ 1220 K; at the full-heat axis rate (2.2 m/h,
decay length 1.1 mm) ≈ 1440 K. **The firing surface temperature is mesh- and rate-dependent.**
Evidence: D2d found +43 % ROP from 2 mm to 1 mm cells (`mmwspalling-d2d-mesh-diagnosis`).
Kant's onset validation (`validation/kant/sp_kant_onset/input_model`) runs at **1 mm cells with a
20 µm scalar flaw**; Meier runs at 2 mm with 4/20 µm Weibull flaws. The validation does not
cover the configuration in use, and 823 K is a mesh-specific anchor.

### 2.2 What is physically right and stays
K_Ic(T) from data; thermoelastic stress magnitude; GB/IG flaw split with grain-facet hashing;
cluster detach gate; melt skeleton factor; flake enthalpy leaves with the flake; the
surface-normal frame (Step 20) exists and multiplies by exactly 1.0 when off.

### 2.3 What is missing, ranked
1. Sub-cell surface temperature in the criterion (§2.1).
2. Any layer-thickness / gradient dependence of the flaking temperature (Kant's normal edge
   crack under sign-reversed compression is a calibrated proxy with none).
3. Gas–solid coupling: no spall blowing; constant gas cp in the enthalpy march (`jet_cp`);
   adiabatic nozzle temperature.
4. Vertical column removal (slope-aware kernel off).
5. Flake thickness = cell.

### 2.4 Energy balance of the isotherm model — what P0 will and will not change
For steady recession the model obeys, per column,

```
v = h · (T_g − T_surf,fire) / ( ρ c · (T̄_removed − T0) )
```

numerator: the convective flux AT THE SURFACE TEMPERATURE at which the column fires;
denominator: the enthalpy that leaves with the removed material. Today T̄_removed ≈ 823 K (the
criterion is on the cell average) while T_surf,fire ≈ 1220 K at 2 mm and ≈ 1000 K at 1 mm
(§2.1). The mesh dependence is therefore in the NUMERATOR: (1684 − 1000)/(1684 − 1220) = 1.47,
which is D2d's +43 %. Three consequences the planner must build the packets around:

1. **A correct surface temperature alone makes the model FASTER, not slower.** Physical
   flakes are thin (T̄_removed ≈ T_surf,fire ≈ T_s(q) ≈ 950–1000 K at the axis flux), so the
   physical rate relative to today's 2 mm run is (1684−970)/(970−293) ÷ (1684−1220)/(823−293)
   ≈ 1.2. The rock-side packets P0–P1 are for correctness and mesh-independence; **they do not
   close the level, and they move it ~20 % the wrong way.** This corrects the estimate given in
   chat on 09-30 ("T_s(q) ≈ 1.3× of the 2×"), which compared against a fixed 823 K surface that
   the model does not actually have.
2. **The level must therefore close on the gas side**, where the literature says the deficit
   is: spall blowing (Wilkinson −35 %, film theory up to −50 % at Meier's feed), gas heat
   capacity (−10 % for an air flame, §5), and the unmeasured gas temperature (cooling water
   1.9–5.6 kW ⇒ −100…−300 K ⇒ another 0.6–0.9). Rauenzahn's copper-vs-rate factor of 3–4 says
   there is room. The product of these lies between 0.3 and 0.7 of the present rate, i.e. an
   axis at 0.8–1.6 m/h against a feed of 1.5. That is the honest prior for P4: a pass is
   plausible, not assured, and the residual will be attributed to the gas temperature band.
3. **With a surface-temperature criterion and whole-cell removal, the denominator becomes
   mesh-dependent** (a thicker cell has a cooler mean). P0 must therefore also make the removal
   energy-consistent (P0b below) or the rate will diverge with dz in the other direction.
   Acceptance is the analytic steady state on a 1-D column under constant flux:
   v = q/(ρc(T_s − T0)) to ±5 % at 2 / 1 / 0.5 mm.

## 3. Packet P0 — surface temperature and a mesh-independent anchor (source, ~3 days)

**Change.** In the T-derived σ_xx path (Removal.H :846–870), replace `T_face = T_top` under
local_thermoelastic by a reconstructed surface temperature. Use the column's own steady
exponential profile, which the model reproduces once the floor moves at a steady rate:

```
T_surf = T0 + (T_top − T0) · (dz/ℓ) / (1 − exp(−dz/ℓ)),     ℓ = κ_th / v_col
```

with v_col the column's recent recession rate (exponential moving average, τ ≈ 5 s; fall back
to the linear form `T_surf = T_top + q_top·dz/(2k)` when v_col = 0, i.e. before first firing,
where q_top is the column's applied flux from `flame_h·(T_g − T_top)`). Interpolate T(z) inside
the top cell between T_surf at z = 0 and T_top at z = dz/2 (the code already lerps; only the
z = 0 endpoint changes). Both forms are algebraic on data the column already holds
(`flame_h`, `flame_Tg`, the events' `rop_s`).

**Re-anchor.** With the surface temperature corrected, the 4 µm intragranular flaw will fire far
too early. Re-fit `weibull.a0_ig` (keep a0_gb/a0_ig = 5 and m = 20 for P0) so that Kant's onset
case (Central Aare granite, h 500–1000 W/m²K, T_fl 1300–1600 K) still fires at ΔT 461 K
(390–560 band), **using the Meier flaw settings and the Meier cell size (2 mm)**; then repeat
at 1 mm and 0.5 mm.

**Acceptance.**
* Firing SURFACE temperature (new event-log column `T_surf`) mesh-independent to ±3 % across
  2 / 1 / 0.5 mm on the Kant onset case and on a 1-D uniform-flux column at 0.55 MW/m².
* Kant onset inside 390–560 K rise at all three meshes; Hu LRST onset (773 K / 37 s granite,
  893 K / 89 s sandstone) within its existing tolerance.
* Identity: with `spall.surface_T = cell_average` (new key, default = the new reconstruction is
  OPT-IN for this packet) the 2639-file tree gate is byte-identical.
* Report, do not score: the Meier FH leg rerun at 2 mm with the new anchor — the energy per
  removed volume and the axis rate will shift; record both. This is the "how much closes on its
  own" measurement (§8 of `2026-09-30.md`).

**P0b — energy-consistent removal (same packet).** When a column fires, the physical flake is
h_col thick (the scan's isotherm depth, or the P1 layer δ once available) at ≈ T_surf; the rest
of the voided cell is rock that physically stays. Keep whole-cell geometry (D2z-1's rim cure
depends on it) but conserve the rock's enthalpy: at the void flip (Removal.H :1475 block),
transfer H_remainder = ρ c (dz − h_col) (T̄_cell,below-flake − T0)·A_cell into the new top cell
(H_mf of k_top − 1), leaving only the flake's enthalpy to exit. New key
`spall.removal_energy = cell | flake` (default cell ⇒ identity). The flake enthalpy leaving per
unit removed volume then tends to ρc(T_s − T0) independent of dz, and the 1-D steady-state test

(`mmwspalling-amr-surface-band`, works on the thermal+removal path) makes the cell ≈ the flake

go-ahead (standing instruction).

**Where the per-column rate comes from (used by P0, P0b, P2).** `rop_s_cand_col` is per step
(Removal.H :1001); keep a persistent per-column vector in the integrator (alongside `flame_h,
flame_Tg, flame_s`, MMWSpalling.H :6707; reduced like `k_top_col`, so every rank holds the same

**K_Ic at the crack tip, not the cell.** `kic_pa_at_cell` (Removal.H ~:770) evaluates the
toughness at the cell-average temperature of the tip cell; under P0 evaluate it at the

## 4. Packet P1 — flaking temperature that rises with flux (source, ~5 days)

**Physics.** Weakest-link statistics on the volume that is actually stressed at failure, which
is the heated layer, not the cell. Rauenzahn 1986 eq 4.91 / Dey & Kranz 1985: with the spall
aspect ratio fixed (≈15), the stressed volume scales as δ³, and for a semi-infinite solid under
constant flux δ ∝ ΔT_s/q, giving

```
ΔT_s ∝ q^{ 3 / (2·m_a + 3) }        (m_a = Weibull modulus on FLAW SIZE; strength modulus = 2·m_a)
```

Wilkinson's measured slope q^{≈0.27} on Barre ⇒ m_a ≈ 4 (his strength m = 8). Westerly m = 13
⇒ m_a ≈ 6.

**Change.** Keep the per-cell random quantile drawn at start-up (MMWSpalling.H :6589–6614) but
apply the volume factor at EVALUATION time, where a_f is read (Removal.H :634, :669):

```
a_eff = a_cell · (V_s / V_ref)^{1/m_a},    V_s = δ³,
δ = depth below the surface at which (T − T0) has fallen to (T_surf − T0)/e   [per column, from the gathered profile]
```

`V_ref = δ_ref³` with δ_ref the layer thickness at the anchor flux (0.55 MW/m², ≈1.3 mm for
Grimsel properties) so that the P0 anchor is preserved at Kant's flux. New keys: `weibull.m_a`
(default = current m ⇒ identity), `weibull.V_ref` (default = V0 ⇒ identity), `weibull.volume =
cell | heated_layer` (default cell ⇒ identity). The per-cell scatter widens with m_a = 4 to
about ±20 % in ΔT_s; this is Wilkinson's 90 % band and is physical — **retire the "firing window
≤ 30 K" guard** from the D2ad packet; it was a lever-cleanliness check, not physics.

**Validation case (new): `validation/wilkinson/sp_wilkinson_Ts_vs_q`.** 1-D column, uniform
prescribed flux q ∈ {0.3, 0.6, 1.2, 2.0, 2.9} MW/m², Barre properties (Wilkinson Table 4.5 —
transcribe from the thesis, DSpace 1721.1/14031, p. 132), record T_surf at first firing.
Targets (Fig. 1.8): ≈480, 560, 680, 830, 930 °C, pass inside his 90 % band (roughly ±100 °C
at 1 MW/m²). Also run Westerly (Fig. 1.9: 450 → 600 at 1.2 → 740 at 2.9 °C) with m_a = 6.

**Operational details for P1.**
* δ per column: walk the gathered profile `T_at_didx(d)` from the surface; δ is the linear
  interpolation of the first crossing of (T − T0) = (T_surf − T0)/e. Before first firing the
  profile is erfc-shaped and δ is well defined; clamp δ ≥ dz/4 so the cube never underflows.
* V_ref: measure δ at first firing in the anchor case at 0.55 MW/m² (h_conv 500, T_flame such
  that q_0 = 0.55) and set V_ref = δ_ref³; report it. Expect δ_ref ≈ 1.3 mm for Grimsel
  properties (κ 6.9e-7, k 1.5).
* Apply the same factor to grain-boundary flaws (they are facet-hashed draws; the volume factor
  multiplies after the draw, MMWSpalling.H :6589).
* The Wilkinson case is a LASER experiment, i.e. a surface-absorbed uniform flux from a cold
  start. Build it with the beam source, not the flame: `beam.omega0` ≥ 10× the domain width
  (flat to 2 %), `beam.alpha_expr` ≥ 1e4 /m (absorption depth ≤ 0.1 mm), `beam.R 0`,
  `beam.schedule.P` chosen so 2P/(π ω0²) = q; domain 20 mm cube at 0.5 mm cells; record T_surf
  at first firing per q. The Zhang case (`validation/zhang/*/input*`) shows the key set.
* Barre / Westerly properties: transcribe Wilkinson Table 4.5 (printed p. 132; scan in the
  09-30 scratchpad `wocr/p-132.txt`, re-OCR if the scratchpad is gone). Literature placeholders
  until then — Barre: ρ 2650, k 2.7, cp 820, α 8e-6, E 50 GPa, ν 0.25; Westerly: ρ 2650, k 2.6,
  cp 820, α 8e-6, E 55 GPa, ν 0.25. K_Ic table stays Nasseri (Westerly) for both.
* Stop rule: if matching Wilkinson's slope needs m_a < 3, the stressed-volume form is
  overreaching; stop and switch to the buckling form (§4, alternative) rather than force it.

**Acceptance.** Wilkinson case passes at 1 mm and 0.5 mm; Kant onset and Hu LRST still pass;
identity with defaults. Report the Meier FH rerun again (not scored).

**Alternative form to keep in view (not this packet).** A delamination-buckling criterion
(Hutchinson & Suo 1991): surface-parallel crack of length L = c·δ under σ = Eα ΔT/(1−ν),
σ_cr = (π²/12)·k·E/(1−ν²)·(δ/L)². It carries the same thickness dependence through the
mechanism itself and is the right form for the MMW regime gate (a thick volumetrically heated
layer does not buckle). P1's stressed-volume form is chosen first because it is one parameter,
Rauenzahn's validated law, and testable against Wilkinson's data directly.

## 5. Packet P2 — heat transfer to a shedding surface (source, ~2 days)

**Physics.** Film-theory transpiration correction (Kays & Crawford), which Wilkinson applied to
the convective terms only and found −35 %:

```
h_eff = h · B / (exp(B) − 1),     B = φ · ṁ″_spall · c_p,gas / h,     ṁ″_spall = ρ_rock · v_col
```

At Meier's feed B ≈ 1.2 ⇒ factor 0.52 (φ = 1) or 0.72 (φ = 0.5, Wilkinson's outer-layer
argument, his Strouhal estimate Sr ≈ 1e-4 says the near-wall layer is unaffected).

**Change.** Apply in `BuildFlameColumns` at the point where `flame_h[col]` is set
(MMWSpalling.H :2059–2067), using the same v_col moving average as P0, so the kernel, the
ledger and the enthalpy march all see one value and the power identity (D2ad criterion (f))
holds by construction. Keys: `surface_patch.blowing = 0|1` (default 0 ⇒ identity),
`surface_patch.blowing_phi` (0.5 default), `surface_patch.blowing_tau` (5 s). Use `jet_cp` for
c_p,gas.

**Also in this packet, one line:** the enthalpy march's constant `jet_cp` (1250) — add an
optional cp(T) for the CO₂/H₂O/N₂ product mix (Wilkinson eq 6.2 for the CO₂/H₂O part; N₂ from
any table) behind `surface_patch.jet_cp_law = constant | products`. Wilkinson found −30 % on
wall flux from this alone. Default constant ⇒ identity.

**Operational details for P2.**
* B uses the same persistent per-column rate as P0. With v in m/s: ṁ″ = 2750·v kg/m²s; at the
  feed ṁ″ = 1.15, B = φ·1.15·1250/h ≈ 1.2 (φ = 1, h 1200). Apply the factor to the convective h
  only, not to the radiative term, and only on floor faces (s > 0); the annulus pair
  (`side_face_h` at r ≥ 40 mm) is a derived wall coefficient and stays.
* Feedback: less absorbed floor power ⇒ the enthalpy march returns a hotter exhaust ⇒ T_g rises
  a few tens of K. That is physical; report T_g on every leg as D2ad did.
* cp(T) for METHANE–AIR products at λ = 1.2 (not Wilkinson's oxygen flame): mole fractions
  ≈ CO₂ 0.08, H₂O 0.16, O₂ 0.035, N₂ 0.72; mixture cp ≈ 1.15 kJ/kgK at 800 K, 1.27 at 1300 K,
  1.33 at 1700 K, 1.36 at 1900 K. Wall/jet ratio ≈ 0.88, so expect −10 to −12 % on the wall
  flux, not Wilkinson's −30 %. Implement behind `surface_patch.jet_cp_law = products` with the
  four-point table above (interpolate), applied where `mcp` is formed (MMWSpalling.H :2866,
  :3022, :3055, :3069). Optional; the bigger lever is blowing.

**Validation.** None direct. Bracket φ ∈ {0.5, 1.0} in P4. Report Rauenzahn's copper-vs-rate
factor (3–4) as the bound the two effects must stay inside.

## 7. Packet P4 — the Meier run, pre-registered (runs, ~1 day compute)

`spall.surface_T = reconstructed`, `weibull.volume = heated_layer`, `weibull.m_a 4`, `blowing 1`,

## 15. P1 correction — reference the stress jump across the FLAKE, not a fixed depth (1 Oct, user-agreed)

The P1 packet (30 Sep 23:21) is the gradient-aware criterion the user asked for, and its
structure stands: `spall.driving_stress = absolute | differential`, both firing sites, the
uniform-slab unit test, the `max(0,·)` clamp, the refit against Kant only, the quarter-domain ×4.
**One line must change, and one guardrail must be reversed.**

**15.1 The physics has two gradient routes with opposite signs.** (i) DRIVE: no stress jump
across the flake ⇒ no delamination (flat preheated slabs do not spall). (ii) STATISTICS: a
steeper gradient ⇒ thinner stressed layer ⇒ fewer flaws ⇒ HIGHER spall temperature (Wilkinson
1989 Figs 1.8/1.9, T_s rising with flux, m ≈ 8–13; Kant 2017's validated local form is
flux-blind). A FIXED `d_ref` implements (i) with the anti-(ii) sign: the drive fraction on an
exponential front is 1 − e^{−d_ref/ℓ}, so a sharper (faster) front gets MORE drive and fires
COOLER — opposite to the data, and a positive feedback at the axis.

**15.2 Pre-registered failure of criterion (c) as written.** Kant anchor profiles are erfc with
√(κt) = 3.7 mm (h150, onset ≈ 20 s) and 1.0 mm (h500, ≈ 1.5 s); the drop over 1 mm is 0.22 vs
0.64 of the superheat. Refit the flaw at h500 and h150 fires at ≈ 635 K superheat (665 / 598 K
for d_ref = 0.5 / 2 mm) — outside 390–560 K at every d_ref in the (g) sweep. The packet's own
two-flux anchor falsifies the fixed-depth form.

**15.3 The fix: `z_ref = h_f`** (the shipped `c_f·ell_eff`, capped by `flake_h_max`), i.e. the
stress jump between the candidate flake and the rock directly beneath it — the buckling /
energy-release drive of Evans–Hutchinson. The packet forbids this as "exactly inert" because the
fraction is 1 − e^{−c_f} = 0.33 on every exponential front. That constancy IS Kant's
flux-blindness, reproduced with no refit (the erfc anchor legs give 0.32 / 0.29). The
discrimination needed is against NON-exponential profiles, and it is delivered: on rev-7 wall
columns (T_surf − T_top ≈ 30 K, flat beneath) the drop across 1.5 mm is ≈ 0.075 of the superheat
vs 0.33 on the floor — 4.4× less drive; the uniform-slab test passes identically (zero jump).
Wilkinson's T_s(q) rise is route (ii) and is a SEPARATE additive step (per-grain / stressed-
volume flaw draws), not a competitor.

* Key: `spall.driving_ref = flake | fixed` under `differential`; `fixed` keeps `driving_ref_depth`
