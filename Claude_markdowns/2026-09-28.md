## Literature check on the δ-closure (user: "the mechanism seems weird")

Question: how do others handle a receding surface on a fixed grid, and is the timing-based sub-cell step height an established idea or an ad hoc one?

**Families found**
1. *Front-fixing / moving grid, 1-D ablation codes* — CMA (Aerotherm), FIAT, CHAR (NASA), ITRAC (Ewing, Laker, Walker 2013, JTHT "Numerical Modeling of Ablation Heat Transfer"): Landau coordinate or a contracting grid with node dropping; surface always a node; no staircase because the mesh follows the front. 3-D shape change needs mesh deformation (CHAR 2021). Not applicable to a hole with a cone floor + walls.
2. *Fixed-grid enthalpy methods* — Voller & Cross 1981 (IJHMT 24:545), Voller 1990 review: front implicit in the liquid fraction; the KNOWN artefact is "waviness" — the front position and melt rate oscillate as the front crosses each control volume, because the interface cell is held at T_m for the whole crossing. Remedies: implicit stepping; locating the front from node-crossing TIMES (interpolate the time each node changes phase) — this is the direct ancestor of our phase φ = (t − t_last)/dt_i. Groot 2018 (arXiv 1804.06107) goes further: stores the continuous front position s(t) on a regular grid, merges the cut cell with its neighbour (Udaykumar), quadratic one-sided stencils at the front ⇒ second order with 5 grid points.
3. *Level set* — Osher–Sethian; Adalsteinsson & Sethian 1995 (JCP 120, 122) for etching/deposition; Hwang, Govindan, Meyyappan 1998 (NASA NTRS): adopted precisely because cell-removal simulators facet/staircase and string methods loop at corners. ⚠ Zhang, Millmore, Nikiforakis 2022 (arXiv 2211.00681, MMW ablation, AMReX+AMR) use a signed-distance φ only to record depth below the surface and set the interface AT THE CELL BOUNDARY between rock and gas cells — i.e. whole-cell removal; same staircase class as ours.
4. *Embedded-boundary / cut-cell conduction* — Johansen & Colella 1998; Limare, Popinet, Josserand et al. 2022 (arXiv 2202.08300, Basilisk hybrid LS/EB for Stefan + dendrites): the level set cuts cells into volume fractions V_i and face apertures α; fluxes scale by α, the interface gradient comes from a two-point one-sided stencil, small cells by flux redistribution, emerging cells initialised. **Our g(δ) and area law are a hand-built face aperture; the EB literature derives both from a stored interface.** AMReX has EB support natively.
5. *Cellular automata for etching* — Gosálvez et al. continuous CA: each cell carries a continuous occupancy and a **predicted removal time**; constant-time stepping gives wrong etch rates/fronts, the exact reformulation removes atoms at their predicted times. Closest analogue to "our removal is event-based, use the timing".
6. *Spallation-specific* — Rauenzahn & Tester 1991 (IJHMT 34:795): steady frame moving with the front, 1-D energy balance + jet model ⇒ rate and steady hole shape, no grid recession at all. Saksala, Kouhia, Mardoukhi, Hokka 2021 (Phil Trans A 379:20200128): FEM damage–viscoplasticity, spall = tensile damage > 0.9, 0.2 s only, no long-run recession. Walsh, Lomov, Roberts 2011/2012 (LLNL GEODYN): spall VOLUME FRACTION in an Eulerian hydrocode (VOF-like), not whole-cell. **Nobody in the spallation literature has run long-duration recession on a fixed 3-D grid with per-cell removal; our artefact class is the enthalpy-method waviness / CA faceting, and the literature's generic cure is a stored continuous front + cut-cell apertures.**

**Reading**: the closure is not exotic — its two ingredients each have a lineage (crossing-time front location; face aperture scaling) — but it is the *estimated* version of a *stored* front. The generic replacement the literature points to is D2j's continuous recession: advance the per-column φ by the energy-set rate every step, void the cell when φ crosses, take δ from φ differences, scale the face by the aperture. Same ingredients, no estimator, no fallback, independent of the heating path (works for the MMW beam line).
