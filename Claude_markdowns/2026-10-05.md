### 1. The central objection: this step cannot decide the form, because the forms differ only where the cap binds
Under `differential`, drive = σ(T_hi) − σ(T_lo), both read from ONE exponential (`:6522-6528`), so
drive_frac = 1 − e^{−h_f/ℓ}. With h_f = c_f·ℓ that is the constant 0.3297 (the code says so at
`:3556-3560`) — i.e. **differential ≡ absolute × 0.33, and the a0 refit (×2.46) undoes it.** The forms
separate ONLY where h_f ≠ c_f·ℓ — today, exactly where `flake_h_max` = 1.5 mm caps h_f (ℓ > 3.75 mm:
the wall band). So (c1) is not "is Rule B usable", it is "is Rule B usable WITH the stopgap cap that
P1g-4 is about to remove". Under P1g-4's law h_f = 2.67ℓ/m the fraction is again constant
(1 − e^{−2.67/m} = 0.125 at m 20) and the two forms are once more one calibration apart. ⇒ **the
ordering is backwards**: the thickness law makes the form question nearly moot on surface-heated rock;
running the form test first measures the cap.

### 2. The MMW argument is reversed by the code the packet cites
Packet: Rule A is "local at one point" so it cannot represent an interior peak. But BOTH forms read the
two-cell reconstruction. An MMW profile with its peak below the surface has T_top < T_1 ⇒ `RawSurfaceX`
returns "flat" (`:9726`) ⇒ T_surf = cell average and the exponential is flat ⇒ under `differential`
T_hi − T_lo ≡ 0 ⇒ **Rule B never fires on an interior-peak profile; Rule A fires on the cell average.**
If anything the MMW case indicts the differential form. The real MMW defect is the RECONSTRUCTION
(exponential-decay assumption), shared by both — and that is a P-step of its own, not a form choice.
(a3) is still worth running as a profile measurement; it should be paired with a zero-cost offline
evaluation of both drive formulas on the measured profile, not with a conclusion about Rule A.
