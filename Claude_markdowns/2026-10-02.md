## Implementation takeaways (P1f)

1. ⚠⚠ **The criterion could not go where the packet's ledger pointed.**
   `MMWSpalling.H:2070-2084` enumerates the right (cell, face) pairs but sits inside
   `amrex::ParallelFor(bx, [=] …)` — a device-capable lambda, where
   `SpCriterion::K_I`'s callable and per-column vectors cannot go. `Removal.H`'s
   MFIter is a plain host loop, which is why the vertical scan captures by
   reference. The lateral pass is therefore its own host pass there, which also
   makes the `detach_gate` trap structurally impossible rather than patched.
2. ⚠⚠ **A new regime's ledger term must be added UNCONDITIONALLY, and the test must
   run the branch that exposes it.** The term first went inside the existing
   `if (remap_on)` block. The unit test runs `removal_energy = cell` ⇒ `remap_on`

   **passed (e)** and been silently wrong on every cell-energy run. A regime that can

### ⚠⚠ The saturation claim does not survive its own test

Takeaway 1 of rev 6 argues the 2 mm legs' identical `ell` (6.2988 vs 6.2992 mm) is **not** isotropy but
the instrument saturating — "the coarse log-ratio samples both geometries the same way" — and concludes
the residual is really ~20 %, visible only once the mesh resolves it.

That hypothesis makes a sharp prediction. `ell = d_n / ln(T_face/T_behind)`, and `d_n` differs between
the two geometries by exactly sqrt(2) (2.000 vs 1.4142 mm). If the instrument were reading the *same*
field twice, the measured **ratios** would match and the recovered `ell` would then differ by **1.4142**.

Measured on the contained 2 mm pair:

| | axis | 45 deg | quotient |
|---|---|---|---|
| `d_n` [mm] | 2.0000 | 1.4142 | 1.4142 |
| `ln(T_face/T_behind)` | 0.3175 | 0.2245 | **1.4143** |
| raw two-cell ratio | **1.3737** | **1.2517** | — |
| recovered `ell` [mm] | 6.2988 | 6.2992 | **1.0001** |

The raw ratios are **not** equal — 1.3737 against 1.2517 — and their logs differ by **1.4143**, i.e. the
geometric sqrt(2) to four digits. So the instrument resolved two genuinely different temperature fields
and the `d_n` divisor brought them into agreement to **1 part in 10,000**. That is the correction
working, and it is the exact opposite of saturation, which would have shown quotients of 1.000 and
1.4142 the other way round. Sample sizes are not the issue either: summed over 1197 records the legs
carry **19,152** and **17,955** firings.
It is also the answer theory predicts: for a long 45 deg wall the isotherms run parallel to the wall, so
the along-x decay is `ell/0.7071` and the ln-ratio quotient is exactly sqrt(2).

### So why does 1 mm read 19.6 %? Not resolved — and (c3) cannot resolve it as built

At 1 mm the ln-ratio quotient is **1.6918**, not 1.4142, so the field is *not* the parallel-isotherm
one. The confound is sufficient to explain it: (c3) seeds the riser by **cell count**, so the step went
**20 mm -> 10 mm** when `dz` halved. At `ell ~ 5 mm` that is 3.3 decay lengths of wall versus **1.7**,
where the top and bottom ends dominate and the isotherms are nowhere near parallel. The axis leg alone
moved 6.30 -> 4.71 mm at the same 60 s soak, which a mesh refinement cannot do to a physical length.
⇒ One clean measurement (0.0057 %) and one confounded one (19.6 %). The disagreement is **open**, and it
is **not** settled in favour of 20 %. The de-confounded run — same 20 mm riser, 20 cells at 1 mm, soak
shortened to hold `ell/dz` fixed — is seconds of compute and is the cheapest decisive test in the step.

## §6 Leg B is VOID — the lateral criterion compares Pa to MPa (12:40)

Leg B ran, (e) and (m2) went red, and the implementer stopped correctly. The cause is upstream of both:
**the lateral criterion compares a stress intensity in Pa·sqrt(m) against a toughness in MPa·sqrt(m).**

### The bug, one line

`KIcNasseri` returns the Nasseri table directly (1.43 ... 0.22, **MPa·sqrt(m)**). `sigma_lat` returns
`C_th * jump` with `E = 3.0e10` **Pa**, so `K_I` is in **Pa·sqrt(m)**. The vertical path converts:

    Removal.H:1333, :1357   K_Ic_Pa = MPa_sqrtm_to_Pa_sqrtm(K_Ic_MPa);   // x1e6

The lateral path does not:

    Removal.H:622-628       K_Ic = KIcNasseri(T_tip, kic_scale_loc);
                            const Set::Scalar score = K_I / K_Ic;

⇒ **the lateral score is inflated by exactly 1e6.** Swept every `KIcNasseri`/`KIcLinear` call site in
`src/`: **:624 is the only one that never converts**, and it hits **both** lateral branches, since the
vertical path feeds `kic_K_Ic0_loc` into `KIcLinear` and *then* converts — so the fallback is MPa too.

### Verified from the event log

224,359 regime-6 firings; **70.7 %** below 400 K; median `T_face_lat` **362.1 K**; 20,264 in the first
20 s at median radius **97.7 mm**; max radius **166.9 mm** (the domain corner). At 362 K the superheat is
69 K, so sigma ~ 7.8 MPa and **K_I ~ 0.16 MPa·sqrt(m) against K_Ic = 1.41** — a factor of 9 short.
Firing needs about **893 K**; the population's p95 is 534 K. Signature: `lat_fires / lat_cand` has median
**0.667**, p95 **1.000** — essentially every column with a candidate fires, which is what unbounded
slack looks like (0.667 not 1.0 because a 45 deg column offers two faces and fires once).

### Two rival diagnoses, both wrong

**Gas temperature instead of face temperature: no.** The code calls `SurfaceCellFlux` with the
out-parameter, rejects `!(T_face > 0)`, and builds the stress from `dT_s = T_face - T0`. The logged
362 K median *is* the temperature the stress used — which is exactly why `K_I` is small. The drive was
right; the comparison was broken.
**Absolute vs differential: a real inconsistency, not the cause, and it points the other way.**
`sp_driving_diff` defaults false and Leg B never sets it, so the vertical mode runs `absolute` while the
lateral code applies the differential form unconditionally. That still needs a parse guard — but the
differential form multiplies by `drive_frac < 1`, so it makes the lateral drive *smaller*.

| 0–10 mm | **0.0 %** | 1.34 / 1.54 mm | 0.54 mm | 492 | 38 % (r < 30), 11 % of it capped |
| 10–20 | **0.0 %** | 1.44 / 1.75 | 0.58 | 453 | |

**Reading.** The floor interior is healthy: ℓ 1.3–1.4 mm, three times the kinematic skin,
resolved, never capped, h_f ≈ 0.55 mm. The cap is a **wall-band / outer-annulus** phenomenon:
100 % of firings at r ≥ 40 mm, where columns descend at a third of the centre's rate and are heated
through their sides, so the vertical two-cell ratio is flat and ℓ reads 8–12 mm. Those bands carry
**~60 % of the removed volume**. So the cap does not sit under the **descent rate** (floor, cap-free)
— it sits under the **diameter and volume**, i.e. the shape and the +55–65 % volume excess. P2's
floor-flux question is not blocked by it; the Meier width/volume comparison is.

**The same population three times.** P1's frozen columns (RESULTS.md:395-404) were 78 % ≥ 2-sided
exposed, stuck at 48 mm depth against 180 mm for live ones — side-heated columns whose vertical
read is flat, so the differential drive (1 − e^{−h_f/ℓ}) with capped h_f → 0 and they freeze. Today,
under `absolute`, the same columns fire 1.5 mm per event because the cap says so. And they are the
columns the lateral face criterion was built for: their gradient is horizontal, and the lateral
read sees it (ell_lat 5 mm deep, 15 mm at the mouth). The cap, the P1 freeze and the P1g scope are
one problem: **a column heated through its side has no vertical gradient to read, and the vertical
criterion has been asked to remove it anyway.**

**Therefore the next step is P1g, not a vertical "retire the flake bound" step.** Specifically:
1. **Lateral sub-cell recession with the flake sink** (the parked P1g prerequisite), so that the
   consistent lateral mode can run at scale. Its flake thickness is `c_f · ell_lat` and will meet
   the same cap (99.6 % at 1.5 mm per P1g-0 takeaway 3) — so the step must also decide the
   **thickness bound along the surface normal** rather than carry the vertical cap sideways:
   bound ℓ by the heated depth the column/face can physically have (multi-cell fit along the
   normal with a stated containment test; x-clamp as the lower bound), never by a constant.
2. **Then the form.** With side-heated columns served by the lateral read, re-entering
   `differential` no longer freezes them — that is the test. Pre-register the P1 freeze population
   (≥ 2-sided, shallow, 25–35 mm) as the witness: it must fire laterally, not freeze.
3. **a0 moves with the form** (standing rule 1) — one coupled re-derivation, as the other agent
   says; not before 1 and 2.
4. **Leg A is the shape leg** it was always meant to be: roundness by sector and the Meier Ø(z)
   profile, under the new configuration, with today's 1.003–1.019 explicitly not carried.

What NOT to do first: delete or loosen `flake_h_max` in the vertical mode. The floor does not need
it (never binds), and the wall band needs the lateral instrument, not a different vertical constant.
Also not first: P2. Its floor-flux question is real, but the volume/width comparison it feeds is
60 % cap-governed today, and the standoff (floor) half of P2 is the part the cap does not touch —
that half can be read now, as a diagnostic, while P1g is built.
