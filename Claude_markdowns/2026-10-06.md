## §2 The wall coefficient, derived — and the two-zone correction

`surface_patch.side_face_h = 169.3` is a hand-picked scalar read at **four** sites
(`MMWSpalling.H:982`, `:1835`, `:3241`, `:4171`), applied to every face beyond 40 mm at every depth.
The wall's driving temperature is **already** the exhaust (`:2303-2307`, `side_T_eff = jet_T_exhaust`,
1514 K) — that is not the defect.

**Annulus mass flow, verified two ways.** `jet_P_cap = (ṁ/phi)·c_p·(T_stag − T_ent)` inverts to
`phi = 0.3361`. The *separate* relation `T_stag = T_exh + (T_noz − T_exh)·phi` then predicts
**1643.7 K** against the **logged 1645.0 K** — 1.3 K. ⇒ scrubbing flow **2.97× nozzle** = 0.0450 kg/s
full domain; net throughflow 0.0151 kg/s.

**Form.** `h = (k_g/D_h)·Nu`, `D_h = 2(r_hole − r_shield)`, `A = π(r_hole² − r_shield²)`,
`Re = (ṁ/A)·D_h/μ`, `r_shield = 33.5 mm` (Ø67, VT5 drawings). Re is **5,700–6,400 ⇒ transitional**, so
Dittus–Boelter is out of range and **Gnielinski** `Nu = 0.012(Re^0.87 − 280)Pr^0.4` is the right form.

| bore Ø | gap | Re | h_lam | h_DB | **h_Gniel** | vs 169.3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 78.0 | 5.5 | 7473 | 46.0 | 211.3 | **175.4** | **1.04** |
| 84.0 | 8.5 | 7176 | 29.8 | 132.3 | 109.0 | 0.64 |
| 96.0 | 14.5 | 6648 | 17.5 | 73.0 | 59.2 | 0.35 |
| 102.0 | 17.5 | 6412 | 14.5 | 58.7 | 47.3 | 0.28 |
| 121.8 | 27.4 | 5739 | 9.2 | 34.3 | 27.0 | **0.16** |

**⇒ 169.3 is the correct coefficient for a Ø78.4 mm hole** (solved, tol 1 mm), roughly the bore when
it was set. It has been carried while the bore grew to 102 mm and its mouth to 122 mm. The three
correlations span 3–4×, so the regime choice matters — but **all are far below 169.3**, so the
conclusion is robust to it.

**The missing brake.** h rises **4×** from Ø122 to Ø84 ⇒ narrow hole heats harder and widens, wide
hole heats less and stops. Hole diameter becomes an **equilibrium**. A constant cannot do this, and
a missing negative feedback is how a mouth runs away to +26 mm.

**⚠⚠ THE CORRECTION — two flow zones, split at the nozzle plane.** My first pass used the scrubbing
flow everywhere. The nozzle sits *inside* the hole (standoff 179–224 mm, bore 398 mm deep):

| zone | ṁ | Re | h_Gniel | vs 169.3 | regime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| below the nozzle | 0.0450 | 6415 | 47.3 | 0.28 | transitional |
| above the nozzle | 0.0151 | **2156** | **13.7** | **0.08** | **LAMINAR** |

Above the nozzle only the net throughflow leaves — the recirculating cell spans nozzle→floor — so Re
drops below 2300 and h is ~14, i.e. the constant is **~12×** too high there.

**But the removal is all below the nozzle.** Of 39,795 regime-6 events, **39,795 are below the nozzle
plane and 0 above** (height below: p05 −179, p50 −106, p95 −14 mm); 0.0 % of wall volume above. Every
wall face was cut while it was below the nozzle, so the mouth is wide from when the burner passed it,
not from heating now. ⇒ the **below-nozzle** figure (3.6×) is what drives removal; the laminar zone's
12× inflates the energy books only.

**Effect on the budget.** Reweighting the **logged per-column** wall power by each column's own
radius (5,274 samples): **686 → 279 W**, ratio **0.406**, removing **407 W = 22 %** of all delivered
power.

**Gas cooling up the annulus.** Marching `ṁc_p dT/dz = −h·2πr(T − T_wall)` with the derived h:
1514 → 1484 → 1456 → 1402 → **1353 K** at 0/50/100/200/300 mm, i.e. **161 K = 13.2 %**. ⚠ That uses
the **net** throughflow; with the scrubbing flow it is ~4 %. The choice must be declared. ⚠ Correction
to what I said in conversation first: I called this a 4 % effect using the wrong denominator.

**⚠ Honest residuals.** Gas properties are **air** fits (Sutherland μ; standard k) — combustion
products are 10–20 % higher k ⇒ h 10–20 % higher. Wall **roughness** raises h and a spalled wall is
rough. The burner is not perfectly centred. The two-zone split idealises a gradual recirculation
boundary. Each pushes the derived value **up**, so P2-0's P4 pre-registers an overshoot (hole below
Ø84 mm) as the way to size them.

## §8 Reviewer: does the gas recirculate, and how to get a defensible wall coefficient (14:00)

**Net vs local.** Net flow out of a blind hole = nozzle flow ṁ (0.015 kg/s); nothing else enters. But
the jet triples its mass before it reaches the floor (phi = 0.336 ⇒ ṁ_jet ≈ 3ṁ, verified three ways
in ANNULUS_H (a1)). The extra 2ṁ can only come from gas already in the hole, so it is recirculated: the
wall jet spreads along the floor, turns up the wall, and part of it is drawn back into the jet (the
toroidal cell of a confined impinging jet — Fitzgerald & Garimella 1998 measured it). Recirculation is
a CONSEQUENCE of the entrainment the model already uses, not an extra assumption. What is crude is the
planner's picture of it: a plug flow of 3ṁ over the full annulus below the nozzle, ṁ above. The real
wall-adjacent flow is a thin wall jet whose velocity decays with height as it entrains and sheds mass
back to the jet — faster than the plug mean near the floor, slower higher up.

**Why the duct correlation is the wrong instrument.** Gnielinski gives fully developed pipe flow at a
mean velocity (41 m/s at Ø102, Re 6400). Martin's floor wall jet at r/D ≈ 6.7 has U_max ≈ 0.1–0.15 U_jet
≈ 130–190 m/s arriving at the corner; h ∝ U^0.8 ⇒ the corner region could be 2–3× the duct value, i.e.
100–170 — the hand-set 169.3 is not absurd near the floor. Higher up, the wall jet decays and the duct
value (~50) becomes the floor. So the real object is **h_wall(z above floor)**, decaying upward, not a
scalar and not a plug-flow duct. Wall removal spans the whole below-nozzle wall (p05/p50/p95 heights ≈
0–40 / 75–115 / 165–205 mm above the floor from ANNULUS_H (a3) with standoff 180–220), so the decay
shape matters, not just the corner value.

**Three routes, ranked:**
1. **Reduced wall-jet model (days, in-repo, mesh-free).** Floor: Martin, already in `h_expr`. Corner:
   take the floor wall jet's state at r = R_hole (U_m, thickness b from Glauert/Rajaratnam radial wall-jet
   laws, U_m ∝ r^{-1.1}, b ∝ r). Wall: a rising plane wall jet in the annulus, U_m ∝ z^{-1/2}, b ∝ z,
   entrainment coefficient ~0.035, with mass returned to the jet so the up-flow decays toward ṁ at the
   nozzle plane; h from a flat-plate turbulent correlation on (U_m, b) — or Nu_x ≈ 0.0296 Re_x^0.8 Pr^0.33
   with x the distance along the wall — blended to the annulus duct value where b fills the gap.
   Deliverable: h(z; R_hole, standoff) as a second expression map, same plumbing as `h_expr`. Validate
   against: the Martin value at the corner (continuity), the exhaust energy balance (T_exhaust 1514 K
   logged), and the testers' empirical R_hole ≈ 0.45·standoff (tester-theses memory).
2. **2-D axisymmetric RANS (1–2 days, OpenFOAM, k-ω SST).** Nozzle Ø7.5 at Mach 1.86 → treat as a
   subsonic equivalent-momentum jet (D_e = 8 mm, J = 19 N) to avoid the shock; hole Ø85/96/102/122,
   shield Ø67, skirt Ø80 with slots as an axisymmetric porous ring, standoff 100/150/200 mm; isothermal
   walls at T_s. Extract h_floor(r) and h_wall(z), tabulate, fit route 1's form to it. Checks: floor
   against Martin (±20 %), recirculation mass (≈2ṁ) against the phi inversion, exhaust T against 1514 K.
   This is the credible source for the map; the user's group has the tooling.
3. **Literature anchors (hours).** Confined impinging jet with annular outflow: Fitzgerald & Garimella
   1998 (flow field, torus), Garimella & Rice 1995, Fenot/Vullierme/Dorignac 2005 (confined jet Nu);
   jets in dead-end cavities: Terekhov et al.; spallation theses that modelled the hole wall: Rauenzahn
   1986 §3–4, Wilkinson 1989, Augustine 2009. Expect side-wall Nu correlations in H/D and D_cav/D_jet
   form — enough to bracket route 1, not to replace it.

**What is NOT available:** Meier's 160 L/h cooling water ΔT is unreported (thesis notes L43, L230), so

**1. They are not competitors. The duct is the wall jet's far field.** The duct model takes the
