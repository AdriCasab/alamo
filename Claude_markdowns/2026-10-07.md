## §4 Reviewer: "we are missing some physics" — what the a0 paradox is telling us (13:30)

Response to the planner's note (flat T_s; measured E·α drives T_s to 434 K; a0 → 0.6 µm). Verified in
code: `MMWSpalling.H:8379` `vol_factor = pow(V_cell/weibull_V0, 1/m)` with `weibull.V0 = 8e-9` = the
2 mm cell ⇒ 1 identically; `m` otherwise enters only the flake-thickness median (`W_CONE_MEDIAN/m`).
`Removal.H:420` `sigma_xx(z) = −E·beta·(T(z)−T_ref)/(1−nu)` — biaxial **compression** — integrated by
`SpCriterion::K_I` as an **edge-crack opening** integral; `spall.driving_stress = absolute`,
`damage.enabled = 0` on every Meier leg. The code's own comment at `:6284` names the buckling-
delamination drive (Evans & Hutchinson 1984; Hutchinson & Suo 1991) as the `differential` option.

**Both diagnoses are right.** (1) T_s is flat by construction: no flux anywhere in the onset, and the
only Weibull size term is a grid identity. (2) The measured properties lower T_s: E·α at 500 °C is
~1.8× the cold product (Table 3 of the reference), and the cold product is already what sets 757 K at
a0 = 8 µm. Refitting a0 to hold T_s would push it to 0.6–1.8 µm — below the rock's measured
microcracks (50–500 µm, mean 130 µm after heating; Kant 2017a) by two orders of magnitude.

**What the paradox means — the physics that is missing, in order of consequence.**
1. *Sign and mode.* The heated layer is in biaxial compression. At the model's firing ΔT ≈ 470 K the
   nominal elastic stress is ≈300 MPa with cold E·α and ≈530 MPa with the at-temperature values —
   2–3× the UCS (170 MPa). A compressive stress does not open a surface-normal edge crack; the
   criterion drops the sign and applies a mode-I opening formula to a closing stress. The real failure
   of a compressed thin layer is buckling-delamination over a surface-parallel flaw (Rauenzahn 1986;
   Evans & Hutchinson 1984) or wing-crack growth from inclined flaws (K_I ≈ 0.1–0.3 σ√(πa) with
   friction). The tiny a0 is the price of making an opening criterion reproduce a compressive
   threshold: it is the model saying its stress is ~3× too high, with the wrong sign.
2. *Stress relief by thermal microcracking.* The quartz–feldspar mismatch (Δα ≈ 15e-6 × 400 K × 50 GPa
   ≈ 300 MPa at grain scale) cracks the grain boundaries from ~300 °C (crack density ×3.6 by 500 °C,
   Kant 2017a). The dilatometric α includes crack opening; the at-temperature E already includes the
   softening; using both in an elastic σ = E·α·ΔT double-counts. The planner's "cap α at its 300 °C
   value" is a proxy for "expansion above 300 °C is accommodated by cracks, not carried as stress".
   The explicit form is a relief factor or the Drucker–Prager damage that exists in the code and is off.
3. *Flux dependence.* The stressed volume must be the thermal layer, not the cell: V ∝ ℓ³ (ℓ is already
   computed per column as `ell_eff`) gives Rauenzahn's ΔT_s ∝ q^{3/(m+3)}; m must then be the
   spallation modulus (7–10 from Wilkinson's Barre/Westerly slopes; 10 for Westerly), not the
   mechanical 20 — and a broader flaw distribution is what froze 48 % of the floor in the P1 era, so
   this is not free. The buckling picture carries its own layer-thickness dependence (σ_cr ∝ E(h/L)²).
4. *Grain-scale heterogeneity.* The Meier rock is one grain by input (`microstructure.mode =
   expression`); the Voronoi and grain-boundary-cohesive machinery exists (unit/voronoi, gb_cohesive).
   The mismatch stress in (2) is the nucleation physics. Long-term item.
5. *Rate dependence* (subcritical crack growth, n ≈ 30–50): ≤ 10–20 %; last.
6. *K_Ic(T) in the code* is Nasseri 2007 — post-treatment, cooled — which overstates the loss; the
   at-temperature data (Chandler 2017; Geothermal Energy 2022) give ×0.78 at 400 °C.

**Recommendation (amending the planner's).**
- Do not fit a0. Fix it at a physical scale (the measured grain-boundary crack length, 50–130 µm) and
  fit the one quantity the physics says is missing: an effective-stress factor η in σ_eff =
  η·E·α·ΔT/(1−ν), on the Kant SOD ladder (core-scale, cheap). Report η; expect ~0.3–0.5. That turns the
  fudge into a measurement of the relief; a0 of 0.6–1.8 µm would mislead the beam work.
- Put the Weibull-volume fix (V = ℓ³ via `ell_eff`, V0 physical) and the m choice *before* the η fit,
  not after: η fitted on a flat law at m = 20 is re-opened the moment the size term goes live. Fix m
  from Wilkinson's slopes (8–10), check on the Kant ladder.
- Keep the planner's double fit on α, relabelled as a sensitivity on relief above 300 °C, not a choice
  between two α values.
- Agree on staging: P2-2 (c_p, ρ) → Weibull volume + m on Kant → E(T), α(T), K_Ic(T) at-temperature + η
  on Kant → one Meier leg. Then the proper compressive criterion (`driving_stress = differential` is
  the first form in the code; DP damage second) as its own step; Voronoi mismatch after.

### 8.2 What each form IS

**Absolute = the exact 1-D thermoelastic stress.** Half-space, T = T(z) only, free surface, far field at T₀ and stress-free. Nothing varies laterally and the far field is fixed ⇒ ε_xx = ε_yy = 0 at every depth; σ_zz = 0 from equilibrium with the free surface ⇒

  σ_xx(z) = σ_yy(z) = −E·∫_{T₀}^{T(z)} α dθ /(1−ν),  pointwise.

(Boley & Weiner, *Theory of Thermal Stresses*, §9; Timoshenko & Goodier §151.) The skin is not "held by" the millimetre beneath it; it is held in-plane by the entire unheated cross-section around and beneath the spot, and the material one flake down is part of the same zero-strain continuum whatever its temperature. Validity: heated spot wide against thermal depth. Meier R_h ≈ 90 mm, δ ≈ 1 mm ⇒ correction O(δ/R_h) ≈ 1 %, except within ~δ of the spot edge.

**Differential = the stress DROP across the flake, not a stress.** σ(z) − σ(z + h_f). On the exponential reconstruction with h_f = c_f·ℓ it equals (1 − e^{−c_f}) × absolute = **0.3297 × absolute at every depth** — a relief factor of 0.33 under another name, which is exactly what the Kant legs measured to sixteen digits. ### 8.3 The case FOR differential, as strong as I can make it, and where each strand lands

1. **The slab.** A body uniformly at 820 K has no cold constraint ⇒ σ = 0 ⇒ no spall. Absolute fires 80 times because `T_ref` = 293 K is the *initial* temperature, not the constraining body's temperature. Differential gives 0. **But** differential passes by accident (zero gradient ⇒ zero drop), and gives the same zero for a *thick hot layer on a cold body* — a low-flux deep-soaked column — which carries the full absolute stress and does fail. The right fix is the reference: T_ref = temperature of the constraining body (T₀ in drilling, T_init in the slab anchor). The slab anchor should set `T_ref` = 820 K, not lean on the form.
2. **Delamination mechanics.** If the flake separates along a plane at depth h_f, what drives it is the mismatch between the released flake and the substrate surface. The differential intuition: the substrate just below is hot and "wants" to expand, so the mismatch is α(T̄_flake − T(h_f)). **Wrong, because the substrate surface is not free**: it stays at zero in-plane strain, held by the cold bulk, so the mismatch strain is α(T̄_flake − T₀) — the *absolute* thermal strain averaged over the flake. Hutchinson & Suo (1991) for a compressed layer on a stiff constrained substrate: G = (1−ν)·σ̄²·h/E with σ̄ the layer stress relative to the substrate's **strain**. The substrate's temperature enters only its own stress, which does not release. ⇒ the buckling picture gives absolute, averaged over h_f, not differential.

Differential was meant to carry the gradient, but on self-similar fronts it is the constant 0.3297 (planner's point, correct) and carries no flux either. Neither needs a different reference.

**Where differential would be legitimate:** a heated spot *small* against the thermal depth (laser spot of radius ≲ δ; a single absorbing grain under MMW), where lateral constraint is local and the stress relaxes toward differences over the spot size. Not this geometry (R_h/δ ≈ 50–100). ⚠ `ROADMAP.md:419` says neither rule is formulated for volumetric heating; pointwise absolute is still exact in 1-D with an interior peak — the open question there is *which depth* the criterion scans, not the reference.

The in-plane constraint (§8.2) comes from that far field, so the stress-free reference on those legs is still 293.15 and the column minimum is **wrong** there, by up to the (a9).2 table's 3.4e-2 at 5 mm and more where the base warms by tens of K. ⇒ `min` converts a thermal truncation error into a stress error, and the 54 files are that error, not a feature.

**The right definition.** T_restraint = the body's stress-free temperature = the **initial temperature field** T_init(x). Parameter-free; exact in both of the packet's limits (uniform slab: T_init = 500 ⇒ bracket 0; drilling: 293.15 ⇒ unchanged); never drifts with domain warming; local in time. On the manifest it moves exactly the 11 strip legs' `Sp_field` and nothing else — **it satisfies rev 6's own (b1) bar as written**, with QF, M-100, beam_void and dev2d at 0. For a non-uniform IC it is a field, not a scalar, which is what distinguishes it from `phases[0].T_ref`; for every deck in this programme the IC is constant and it equals the deck's IC value. Implementation is one scalar per column (or a stored T_init MultiFab, already cheaper than the min gather's reduction) — the implementer's `ColumnMinTempBelowTop` plumbing carries over with the reduction replaced by a read at t = 0.

**What the warm-bulk physics actually is, so it is not lost.** A warm zone thick compared with its radius IS partially relieved laterally (3-D elasticity, Eshelby-type factor, not 1). That is a real effect at long drilling times, but it is not a column property and `min` does not model it either; it belongs to the `mechanics/stress_mf` path the packet already excludes.

**Recommendation for rev 7.** Keep B, C, D as landed. Replace A's reduction with T_init; keep the NaN fallback. (b1) bar unchanged from rev 6 (0 of 2589 non-strip; strip in `Sp_field` only) — and it must now be scored at **component** level, which needs frozen plotfile copies for the 11 strip legs, not hashes; the implementer's `fabcmp.py` is ready and the prerequisite is a one-off copy of ~50 files before the next sweep. Add one unit assertion: a leg with constant IC ≠ `T_ref` must produce `Sp_field` = 0 under uniform T (the strip deck becomes the test rather than the inconsistency). Fix `input_strip`/`gradient_blind` decks to `T_ref` = IC as a separate one-liner after the re-pin. ⚠ `beam_void_closure` is a standing `rc=1`; its hashes are those of a failing run and should be out of the manifest or fixed, not re-pinned.

## §13 rev 6's blocker resolved by replacing the reference, not the gate (2026-10-08, 00:20) — the column minimum is withdrawn; the stored initial field is the stress-free state by definition

### 13.1 The measurement that decided it, taken from saved plotfiles with no run

rev 6 landed all four changes, passed `(a9)`/`(b4)`/`(b7)`/`(b8)`/`(c1)`/`(c2)` and **failed `(b1)`
with 54 of 2639 differing**. The implementer attributed all 54 to columns "warming through"; the
planner read it as round-off amplified by discrete removal. Both were partly wrong, and the
arbitration needed no run — `Temp` is a stored plotfile component, so the column minimum can be
recomputed offline exactly as `ColumnMinTempBelowTop` computes it:

| leg | step | column minimum | deviation from 293.15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `QF` | 2500 / 10000 / 12500 (all **identical** files) | 293.1500000000 | **exactly 0** |
| `QF` | **15000** (first differing file) | 293.1500000000 | **1.99e-12 K** |
| `QF` | 22500 (end) | 293.3291143385 | 0.179 K; **30 of 576** columns > +1 mK |
| `M-100` | 2000 (first differing) | 293.1500000905 | 9.05e-08 K |
| `M-100` | 10000 (end) | 293.5296478248 | 0.380 K |
| `M-2000` **control** | 2000 and 10000 | 293.1500000000 | **exactly 0, every step** |

**The trigger is round-off, as the planner read it.** `M-100`'s 10 mm absorption length leaves
`exp(-25) = 1.4e-11` of the beam reaching the base — tiny but non-zero; `M-2000`'s `exp(-500)`
**underflows to zero**, so its deepest cell never warms and its minimum is bit-exact forever. A
reduction over the column inherits that dust where a typed constant could not. `QF` is the proof:
bit-identical for **12,500 steps**, then two picokelvin, then divergence.

⚠⚠ **But the late drift is PHYSICAL, as the reviewer read it, and worse than either party said.**
The profile down `M-100` at step 10000 shows only **69 of 250 cells still solid** — the surface
receded **153 mm** — so the "column" whose minimum is taken is a 69 mm stub whose base genuinely
reads 293.53 K. ⇒ **the column minimum degrades as the hole deepens**, because progressively less
cold rock is left beneath it. `(a9)`'s own table prices it: 3.4e-02 at 5 mm of cold rock, 2.6e-07 at
25 mm, 0 at >= 100 mm. Meier's 400-650 mm domain is safe **today** and is a landmine.

### 13.2 ⚠⚠ Why this is a design flaw and not a delicate gate

The reviewer's framing is right and the planner's was wrong: **the restraint on a hot skin has no
dependence on the deepest cell in the column**, so a definition that has one is wrong in a way that
merely happens to be small. Two consequences neither party had spelled out:

1. ⚠⚠ **rev 6 would have switched the model off on the target application.** Under **volumetric**
   heating — MMW, the whole point of the programme — a column warms through, the minimum rises
   toward the column's own temperature, the bracket collapses and **spalling stops**. It would have
   looked like physics.
2. ⚠ **`gradient_blind` passed rev 6 for the WRONG REASON.** "Uniform ⇒ nothing restrains it ⇒ no
   stress" is false: a laterally confined slab heated uniformly **is** in compression, which is
   exactly what the 1-D confined form `-E*beta*dT/(1-nu)` assumes. The right test green on the wrong
   physics is worse than a red test.

⇒ the planner's proposed remedy — *annotate* that the gate trips for meaningless reasons — is
withdrawn, and the reviewer's objection is adopted verbatim as a guardrail: **a gate documented as
unreliable is a gate nobody trusts by next week. Remove the sensitivity rather than annotate it.**

### 13.3 The replacement: the stored initial temperature field

`T0(z)` = the initial temperature, copied once at init, **never updated**, read at the candidate
flake's **own cell**. Better on every axis:

- **the stress-free state by definition** — the rock was unstressed at the temperature it started at
- **a read, not a reduction** — bit-exact, no dust, no drift as the box warms
- **local** ⇒ rev 6's one named weakness is gone
- **parameter-free**, which was the point of the column minimum
- **generalises to in-situ conditions** — rock at depth sitting at 200 degC is stress-free at
  200 degC, which neither a typed constant nor a column minimum gets right

Verified against the decks, and the result is not a coincidence:

| deck | IC | typed `T_ref` | stored reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meier `input_drilling` | 293.15 | 293.15 | **identical** ⇒ gate reads zero |
| Kant `input_model` | 293.15 | 293.15 | **identical** ⇒ gate reads zero |
| `gradient_blind` | **820.0** | 293.15 | 820 ⇒ bracket exactly zero |
| `input_strip` | **500.0** | 293.15 | 500 ⇒ the deck becomes self-consistent |

⇒ the only two decks that move are precisely the two the implementer flagged as **internally
inconsistent**, and the definition that makes them consistent is the correct one.

**Two planner additions to the reviewer's design:**
1. ⚠⚠ **A SEPARATE, UNPLOTTED FIELD** — do not reuse `T_ref_phase_mf`. The **eigenstrain reads it**
   (`MMWSpalling.H:1298`) and it is registered **plotted** (`:394`, `true`), so reusing it couples
   into the elastic solve and re-pins the tree for free.
2. ⚠ **ON REGRID, FILL THE STORED FIELD BY INTERPOLATING THE STORED FIELD**, never from the current
   temperature, or a regrid silently resets the reference to the hot state and the defect returns as
   a mesh artefact. (AMR parked, rule 12; prophylactic.)

**The reviewer's cheaper fallback** — a parse-time abort when a constant IC disagrees with the typed
reference — is recorded as the *acceptable* answer, not the right one: it is a scalar, so it cannot
cover a non-uniform initial state, and it does not generalise to in-situ.
