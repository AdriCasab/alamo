**Synthesis for the thickness law.** With h_med = 2.67·ℓ/m and ℓ = α/v = 1.66 mm at 1.5 m/h:
m = 20 → 0.22 mm; m = 15 → 0.30 mm; m = 8 → 0.55 mm. Three independent Grimsel measurements —
Höser's 200 µm average, Rossi's 250 µm population, Meier's p90 < 300 µm — bracket **0.2–0.3 mm**,
i.e. **m ≈ 15–20**, the same band Shen's 280-spall fit gives (10–17) and Wilkinson's Westerly fit
(13). The source's c_f = 0.4 (m ≈ 7) gives 0.55–0.66 mm, 2–3× the Grimsel data. ⇒ the existing
m = 20 in `input_drilling` is **defensible for the thickness** (0.22 mm) even though Wilkinson found
m = 20 too flat for T_s(q) on Barre — those are two different rocks and the T_s(q) fit for Grimsel is
the open item (Kant's 21 onset legs; Lyu's method on Meier). Hypothesis worth one offline check:
Rossi's second population at ≈ 1 mm is the slowly-receding wall (ℓ 5–12 mm → 2.67ℓ/15 ≈ 0.9–2 mm) —
the model would predict it with no extra parameter.

## §2 Do the sources change the model's direction? (11:00) — yes, in three places; one is large

1. **Thickness law (P1g-3 content changes).** Replace `h_f = c_f·ℓ` capped at 1.5 mm with
   `h_f = 2.67·ℓ/m`, m = `weibull.m` (15–20 from Höser/Rossi/Meier/Shen), ℓ from the flux length
   ℓ_q = k(T_s−T0)/q (mesh-free). Removes a knob and a cap; one Weibull parameter governs flaw
   statistics and thickness; matches the Grimsel 0.2–0.3 mm data. Sub-cell recession (P1g-1/2) is
   what makes it representable — that machinery stays. No Voronoi needed for the PSD scale.
2. **Criterion form (reverses P1, user's decision).** Rauenzahn, Wilkinson, Walsh and Kant all use the
   **absolute** thermoelastic stress at/near the surface against a flaw/toughness; thickness comes
   from the statistics (or Kant's Δx = 200 µm failure-plane depth, where σ(Δx) ≈ σ_s). None uses the
   stress **jump across the flake**. The differential form has no external support, needed the 80 µm
   refit to cancel itself, and froze soaked columns in P1. ⇒ return to the absolute form (the
   control), drop P1g-4's a0 re-derivation. The soaked-wall question moves to flake stability and to
   the stressed-volume statistics below.
3. **Flux-dependent spall temperature (interacts with P2 — the large one).** All four treat T_s as a
   Weibull outcome of the stressed volume: a thinner skin at higher flux stresses less rock, so T_s
   rises with flux (Wilkinson: 920–1100 K at 1–1.5 MW/m², Barre, m = 8). Our T_s ≈ 756 K is
   flux-independent (fixed K_Ic table, per-cell flaw with a mesh-volume factor only). In the pinned
   steady state v = q_net/(ρc·ΔT_s + …): raising ΔT_s from 460 K to 650–800 K lowers the rate per unit
   flux by **1.4–1.7×** — most of the **2×** "floor-flux excess" P2 was going to calibrate on the gas
   side. ⚠ An earlier note (tester-theses memory) concluded T_s(q) "does not lower the level"; with
   these numbers that should be re-derived before P2, because calibrating the gas against a
   fixed-T_s model absorbs a rock-side error into the jet. Implementation is one factor: the per-cell
   flaw (or σ0) scaled by (V_stressed/V0)^{1/m} with V_stressed ∝ ℓ_q·A — the stressed-volume Weibull
   the memory already named as the physics route. m then has to be fit for Grimsel from Kant's onset
   legs or Lyu's method on Meier — the same m as the thickness.

**Unchanged:** the enthalpy/jet thermal side, the lateral mode and sub-cell recession, the grain-free
continuum (sufficient for thickness; Rossi's GB/IG ratio is out of scope), the Kant onset anchor.

**Order implied:** P1g-3 = thickness law + ℓ_q (small, sourced); form decision (absolute); then
"P1g-4" becomes stressed-volume T_s(q) with m fit on Kant/Meier — **before** P2 touches the gas.

## §3 Reply to the other agent's critique of §9a/§10/§1/§2 (11:40) — four points conceded, one rebutted from the primary equations, one refined

**Conceded.**
- **Provenance.** None of the new sources is in the repo; by our own rule the m band, the bracket and
  the coefficient are unbacked until Höser, Rossi 2020, Shen 2018, Wilkinson 1993 (and the Rauenzahn /
  Walsh texts already read) land under `tests/MMWSpalling/validation/` with figure/table citations.
  Precondition, not follow-up.
- **m.** "m = 20 is defensible because it lands in the bracket" was fitting m to the bracket. m is to be
  **fit** from T_s(q)/onset data (Kant's 21 legs; Lyu's method on Meier) and the thickness is then a
  prediction. Withdrawn as written.
- **Size vs thickness.** Right that Höser's "average spall size", Rossi's granulometry and Meier's PSD
  are sizes and the law predicts a thickness; the conversion must come from the primary sources. But
  the 6:1 aspect ratio is Walsh's value for **large** spalls; his Fig. 4 has it falling to **1–2:1 at
  ~200 µm** (equant particles, tied to grain size). So a 200–250 µm size population is 0.1–0.25 mm
  thick, not 35–50 µm; the m conclusion does not invert, the band widens upward (m ≈ 15–30).
- **§2.3's magnitude.** Overstated. Dey/Rauenzahn's median T_s scales as q^{1/(m+3)}: a 2× flux change
  moves ΔT_s by **6.5 % at m = 8** (Barre — where Wilkinson's 920–1100 K lives) but **3.8 % at m = 15 and
  3.1 % at m = 20**. For Grimsel at m ≥ 15 the flux dependence is a few percent; the lever is the
  **level** of T_s (our 756 K vs Kant's 823 K ⇒ ΔT 463 vs 530, ~14 % on rate), which is the 15–25 %
  the Grimsel-properties memory records. The direction point survives weaker: settle the T_s level
  before P2; it is not most of the 2×.

**Rebutted — the 2.67 is not a new constant, and 0.693 is the wrong geometry.** The critique takes the
failure density as e^{−mz/ℓ} alone (median 0.693·ℓ/m). That is a **slab**: equal stressed volume per
unit depth. Rauenzahn (Eq. 4.96–4.100, OCR on disk) and Walsh 2014 (Eq. 3) weight by the volume of a
**cone** whose cross-section grows with depth through the aspect ratio, dV ∝ x²dx, giving
F(w) = 1 − e^{−w}(1 + w + w²/2), w = m·x/ℓ. The median of that density is w = **2.67**; with Rauenzahn's
full normalisation G = 1 − exp(−0.693·F) the conditional median is **w = 2.35**. Either way the
coefficient is **in the source**, derived from the spall geometry the whole literature uses, not
invented here — and Rauenzahn's own check rules the slab value out: "backside at ~80 % of the surface
rise for m = 10–20" is e^{−w/m} = 0.80 ⇒ w ≈ 2.2–4.5; the slab's 0.693 would put the backside at
93–97 %. Correction to §9a: call it **2.3–2.7** (normalisation-dependent), and it IS a median. The
honest residual: the coefficient depends on the assumed spall geometry (constant aspect ratio), and
Walsh showed the aspect ratio varies with size — so it is model-dependent within the literature, not
free. ⇒ with w = 2.35 and ℓ = 1.66 mm: m = 15 → 0.26 mm, m = 20 → 0.20 mm, m = 30 → 0.13 mm.

alongside the floor — the prediction that cannot be fitted; (4) form decision — **absolute, now**, it
