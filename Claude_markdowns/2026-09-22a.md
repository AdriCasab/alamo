# 2026-09-22a — Meier Fig. 8.8: can the thermally altered band be measured? (No, not from the PDF)

**Question.** How much heat went into the wall of Meier's hole without removing rock? This
is the only independent anchor for the split between "delivery" and "cost per volume", which
Meier's energy numbers cannot separate (they fix only the product). Reviewer step 2 of the
09-22 priority list; no code, no runs.

**Outcome: the band is not resolvable in the figure available.** A first pass in this
session produced a width of 1.2 cm and a wall heat of 1.5–2.7 kW; the user could not see the
band, and the checks below show the first pass was reading the noise floor. **Those numbers
are withdrawn.** What survives is in §3.

## 1. Source and what was tried

- Thesis Fig. 8.8, printed p. 224 (pdf p. 251). All three local copies (Downloads, Desktop
  researchDocs, Outlook attachment; two different PDF builds) embed the **same greyscale
  JPEG** (1119 × 858, R = G = B exactly). Caption: "A thermally altered area is visible on the
  side of the hole. It corresponds to the dewatering of the minerals, oxidation as well as
  other factors." Oxidation alteration in granite is a reddish tint with little luminance
  contrast, so a greyscale reproduction can lose it entirely. Scale from the ruler in the
  photograph: 14.96 px/cm.
- Tried: row-averaged grey with far-field linear detrend in 1 cm bins outward from each rim
  (12 cm depth bands); local grain-contrast texture; grain-removed tint image (median 21 px
  minus 151 px illumination); and a control test placing the same 0.5–2 cm "halo" statistic
  at 600 random positions on the block face away from the hole.
- Result: a lighter fringe next to the shadow-side rim of +6 grey levels over 14–38 cm
  against a control sd of 3.0 (**p = 0.04; p = 0.11 in the lower half alone**). The lit-side
  fringe is stronger but follows the rim lip exactly and peaks on it: it is the illuminated
  groove edge, not rock alteration. In the grain-removed image the shadow-side fringe is
  indistinguishable from the centimetre-scale mottle present everywhere on the block.
  **Not a measurement.**
- The depth-uniformity claim (same band width at 12 cm and 45 cm, exposure 1050 s vs 140 s)
  was built on those widths and falls with them.

## 2. What would make the measurement possible

- **A colour original of Fig. 8.8** (ETH Research Collection deposit, the author, or the ETH
  group; the block itself may still exist). Then the method above works, with the red-minus-
  blue channel instead of grey, and the two conclusions to extract are fixed in advance:
  (i) band width against depth — if constant from 12 cm to 45 cm the wall heat is delivered
  within ~150 s of the front passing and the exhaust further up adds nothing; if it grows
  toward the top as roughly √(exposure time) the whole exhaust column heats the wall;
  (ii) wall heat from the width via the semi-infinite solution
  Q″ = ρc_p (T_s − T_amb) · w / (√π · erfc⁻¹θ), θ = (T_alt − T_amb)/(T_s − T_amb),
  with T_alt ≈ 570–670 K, T_s ≤ 821 K, ρc_p = 2.17 MJ/m³K. For orientation only: **1 cm of band
  ⇒ ≈ 15–25 MJ/m² ⇒ ≈ 1.5–2.6 kW test-average (4–7 % of 38 kW)**, scaling linearly with w.
- **The cooling-water ΔT** (160 L/h, instrumented, never reported, p. 208) would give the
  burner's own loss and hence the nozzle enthalpy, which closes the delivery side instead.
- Nothing else in Ch. 8 constrains wall heat. The televiewer log (Fig. 8.9) is also greyscale.

## 4. Consequence for the plan

Step 2 of the 09-22 priority list (anchor the wall-loss term on the altered band before
writing the wall closure) **cannot be completed with the material on this machine.** Until a
colour image or the cooling-water data is obtained, the wall closure has **no independent
magnitude anchor**, and the D2l statement stands unchanged: Meier's numbers fix only
delivery × (volume per joule); the split is unmeasured. The closure must therefore be
scored on shape (the Ø(z) profile and the wall-to-floor recession ratio, Meier ≤ 0.1) and
its wall heat reported as a prediction, not tuned to a target. Request the colour figure.
