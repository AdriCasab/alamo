# 2026-09-22a — Meier Fig. 8.8: can the thermally altered band be measured? (No, not from the PDF)

**Question.** How much heat went into the wall of Meier's hole without removing rock? This
is the only independent anchor for the split between "delivery" and "cost per volume", which
Meier's energy numbers cannot separate (they fix only the product). Reviewer step 2 of the
09-22 priority list; no code, no runs.

**Outcome: the band is not resolvable in the figure available.** A first pass in this
session produced a width of 1.2 cm and a wall heat of 1.5–2.7 kW; the user could not see the
band, and the checks below show the first pass was reading the noise floor. **Those numbers
are withdrawn.** What survives is in §3.

## 1. Source and what was tried

- Thesis Fig. 8.8, printed p. 224 (pdf p. 251). All three local copies (Downloads, Desktop
  researchDocs, Outlook attachment; two different PDF builds) embed the **same greyscale
  JPEG** (1119 × 858, R = G = B exactly). Caption: "A thermally altered area is visible on the
  side of the hole. It corresponds to the dewatering of the minerals, oxidation as well as
  other factors." Oxidation alteration in granite is a reddish tint with little luminance
  contrast, so a greyscale reproduction can lose it entirely. Scale from the ruler in the
  photograph: 14.96 px/cm.
- Tried: row-averaged grey with far-field linear detrend in 1 cm bins outward from each rim
  (12 cm depth bands); local grain-contrast texture; grain-removed tint image (median 21 px
  minus 151 px illumination); and a control test placing the same 0.5–2 cm "halo" statistic
  at 600 random positions on the block face away from the hole.
- Result: a lighter fringe next to the shadow-side rim of +6 grey levels over 14–38 cm
  against a control sd of 3.0 (**p = 0.04; p = 0.11 in the lower half alone**). The lit-side
  fringe is stronger but follows the rim lip exactly and peaks on it: it is the illuminated
  groove edge, not rock alteration. In the grain-removed image the shadow-side fringe is
  indistinguishable from the centimetre-scale mottle present everywhere on the block.
  **Not a measurement.**
- The depth-uniformity claim (same band width at 12 cm and 45 cm, exposure 1050 s vs 140 s)
  was built on those widths and falls with them.

## 2. What would make the measurement possible

- **A colour original of Fig. 8.8** (ETH Research Collection deposit, the author, or the ETH
  group; the block itself may still exist). Then the method above works, with the red-minus-
  blue channel instead of grey, and the two conclusions to extract are fixed in advance:
  (i) band width against depth — if constant from 12 cm to 45 cm the wall heat is delivered
  within ~150 s of the front passing and the exhaust further up adds nothing; if it grows
  toward the top as roughly √(exposure time) the whole exhaust column heats the wall;
  (ii) wall heat from the width via the semi-infinite solution
  Q″ = ρc_p (T_s − T_amb) · w / (√π · erfc⁻¹θ), θ = (T_alt − T_amb)/(T_s − T_amb),
  with T_alt ≈ 570–670 K, T_s ≤ 821 K, ρc_p = 2.17 MJ/m³K. For orientation only: **1 cm of band
  ⇒ ≈ 15–25 MJ/m² ⇒ ≈ 1.5–2.6 kW test-average (4–7 % of 38 kW)**, scaling linearly with w.
- **The cooling-water ΔT** (160 L/h, instrumented, never reported, p. 208) would give the
  burner's own loss and hence the nozzle enthalpy, which closes the delivery side instead.
- Nothing else in Ch. 8 constrains wall heat. The televiewer log (Fig. 8.9) is also greyscale.

## 4. Consequence for the plan

Step 2 of the 09-22 priority list (anchor the wall-loss term on the altered band before
writing the wall closure) **cannot be completed with the material on this machine.** Until a
colour image or the cooling-water data is obtained, the wall closure has **no independent

# Review of the Ch. 4 nozzle-temperature argument (reviewer, 2026-09-22 afternoon)

Checked against the PDF: §4.1.3–4.1.4 (pp. 113–118), §4.2–4.2.3 (pp. 118–124), §8.1–8.2.3
(pp. 208–222), Fig. 8.7 rendered. Arithmetic re-done.

**Holds.** Return path = inner gap, supply = outer gap (p. 211). 160 L/h is the tool's own
loop; wellhead water is separate (p. 208). §4.2.1 quote and "no degree of freedom" correct.
Igniter TC "informs qualitatively and relatively" (p. 220) correct. LHV is the right basis for
sensible heat: 2.47 kg/h × 50.0 MJ/kg = 34.3 kW (HHV 38.1 = Meier's 38). Products
54.5 kg/h = 0.0151 kg/s; at c_p ≈ 1.27 → 19.2 W/K; loss table (5/10/15 kW → 1820/1560/1300 K)
arithmetically right. Flux ∝ (T_gas − 821 K): 1900 → 1560 K is −31 %; with property dependence
of h at fixed mass flow, −35 to −40 %. Rate lever, not shape lever: agreed.

**Does not hold.**
1. **The 29 % "measured chamber-loss fraction" is a misreading.** The 600 kg/h at ~80 °C is
   CW 2, the *pressure-vessel* cooling mantle (Ø 100 main volume inside a Ø 140 jacket,
   p. 118; "outlet of the cooling mantle (CW 2, fed by WP-1)", p. 118). It collects heat from
   the whole vessel interior — a bath of hot water at up to 500 K after CW 1 (350 kg/h) has been
   injected and mixed with the hydrothermal flame products at 260 bar (§4.2.1, Fig. 4.10). It is
   not the loss from a gas-fired combustion-chamber wall to a thin jacket and is not
   transferable. Meier himself discards it in the jet balance and treats it as the reason his
   T_F is a lower bound (p. 121). Without it the "1600 K central" has no source.
2. **"Partly rehabilitates 1436 K" is wrong.** Fig. 8.7: the igniter TC reads 1.0 × 1163 °C
   only around 300 s with the flame attached; after lift-off it reads 0.4–0.5 (≈ 740–850 K)
   through both drilling phases, dipping to 0.2–0.3 late in phase 1. 1436 K was never the
   drilling-phase reading, and the ≈ 800 K it did read sits upstream of a lifted flame with
   cold reactants; it constrains the exit temperature in neither direction.
3. **"1900 K = adiabatic chamber" overstated.** Equilibrium T_ad for CH₄–air at λ = 1.2
   (φ ≈ 0.83) is ≈ 2040 K, not 1900–1950 (dissociation costs ~40 K here). 1900 K therefore
   corresponds to ≈ 2.7 kW loss, ≈ 8 % of LHV — small, not "essentially nothing".
4. **Attribution:** "D2m showed the shape barely responds to power" — that was D2l (min Ø
   flat across the f ladder while absorbed power doubled). D2m (completed today, MIXED) showed
   min Ø tracks 2·r_outer as a structural identity of the support rule while the erosion rate
   at 45–50 mm is invariant to stance within 5–7 %.
5. The §4.2.1 "template" is a mixing balance (CW 1 is injected into the vessel), not a
   jacket-loss balance; the analogy to a closed-loop jacket is loose. Minor.

**What can be sourced from the hardware instead.** The jacket is 160 L/h ≈ 186 W/K. A closed
loop whose return must stay well below boiling bounds the loss at ≲ 11–13 kW (ΔT 60–70 K),
i.e. T_nozzle ≳ 1450–1500 K; a designer's usual ΔT of 20–40 K gives 4–7.5 kW → 1650–1830 K.
So **1450–1850 K is defensible as a hardware-bounded range, but no central value is sourced.**
Two complications for any future use of the jacket as a loss meter: the shield's outer face
sits in the exhaust annulus, so the loop also picks up heat from the exhaust (that same cold
tube cools the annulus gas — relevant to the wall closure: the exhaust above the front is
cooled by a ~300–350 K wall on one side); and no chamber pressure is reported (only the
6 bar(g) air-line limit), so the choked-throat route to T₀ (p₀ ∝ √T₀; 5.2 vs 6.0 bar abs for
1560 vs 2040 K) is closed.

**Recommendation.** Record: "1900 K is ≈ 8 % below equilibrium adiabatic; the water jacket
implies a loss of order 4–13 kW, i.e. 1450–1850 K; central value unsourced; the 29 % figure
from §4.2.2 is the pressure-vessel mantle and must not be cited as a chamber-loss prior."
Do not change the input now (rate lever). Keep the nozzle-temperature sweep queued after
shape; sweep 1500–2000 K, not a point.

# Review of the "blowtorch in a refrigerated pipe" direction (reviewer, 2026-09-22 evening)

**Direction right**: impinging vs grazing h is the mechanism to test, by prescribed heating
first (hot-disk method), scored on Ø(z). Annulus numbers check: ṁ 0.0151 kg/s in the

entry ≈ 2 m > 0.5 m, so developing throughout), h ≈ 20–40 W/m²K; 40 is the upper end and the
correlation choice is a factor-2 decision, not "no adjustable number". Gas cooling over the
climb 150–300 K ✓; rock-wall share of the annulus loss ≈ 1.5–3.5 kW (≈ the model's 3 kW
excess removal). Volume → mean Ø 93 mm ✓ (same measurement as the photo, not independent).

**Three corrections that change the test design.**
1. **Where the model heats the wall.** Above the nozzle plane the model gives *zero* (s ≤ 0
   exclusion); the funnel forms in the 50 mm band below the plane, where lagging rock gets
   the full face h for the ~140 s until the plane passes. So "the wall keeps spalling long
   after the tool has passed" is not the model's mechanism, Case B (wall heated as hard as
   the floor over the full height) is a strawman, and **Case A as described (weak heating
   only above the plane) leaves the band untouched and will funnel exactly as today**,
   refuting nothing. The impinging→grazing switch must occur at the floor–wall corner, i.e.
   inside the band. Key-only it can be done through s: floor = s ≳ 45 mm (centre pit s up
   to 166 mm included), wall = s < 45 mm and s ≤ 0. The control is the existing D2l `M_f0`
   run (145 mm mouth); no new run needed.
2. **The skirt.** Drawings (sheet 15/17): the bottom 58 mm of the burner is a Ø80 tube,
   bore 56, three slots, resting on the rock. In the band the rock wall faces 12 mm of steel
   across a 2.5–6.5 mm gap; the radial wall jet leaves through the slots; rock beyond r = 40
   is reached only through them. That is a second, geometric reason the band gets little
   heat, and it is why Meier's hole is only 2.5–6.5 mm per side wider than the feet. The
   Ø67 / 1 cm annulus picture applies **above** the skirt.
3. **"2 % of the floor's heat, not enough to break rock" is marginal, not robust.** Robin
   heating at h = 40 for 1000 s: T_s ≈ 770 K for T_gas 1300 K, ≈ 870 K for 1500 K — either
   side of the 821 K threshold. Whether the wall spalls depends on the annulus gas
   temperature, which the cold shield and the floor absorption set. So gas cooling is not
   secondary for the threshold question, and **once wall physics is in, T_nozzle becomes a
   shape lever near the threshold** (amend the "rate lever only" rule). Meier's wall was
   altered but not spalled: the closure must land the wall at ≈ 600–800 K, a tight,
   pre-registrable prediction.

**Also.** Case A at f = 0 is the exact configuration of the D2i/D2j-0b step-face cascade;
weak wall heating makes lagging columns stall harder, not softer. A single-mesh hand test is
not quotable — run the 2 mm/1 mm pair. Score the shaft against **85–95 mm** (Table 8.2 says
85; the photo's 9.5 ± 0.5 is biased outward by the lit lip), below 10 cm depth only.

**Recommendation.** Yes to the prescribed pair, amended: switch at the floor corner via s;
skirt band grazing or zero; control = existing `M_f0`; both meshes; predictions fixed
beforehand: Ø(z) shaft in 85–95 below 10 cm, end-of-run wall T 600–800 K, wall heat
1.5–3.5 kW, no widening after the plane passes.
