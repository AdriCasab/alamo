## P2-2d pulled forward — the band was a function, not a constant, and the flux ladder is what breaks the degeneracy

### 1. ⚠⚠ The planner's band was derived from the uncertainty it was meant to adjudicate

P2-2b's `(c2)` scored two `alpha` treatments against **one** band, 0.41-0.69, written as if it were a
fixed property of granite. It is not: `R`'s band is `UCS/sigma` and
`sigma = E(T_s)*INT(alpha)dtheta/(1-nu)`, so it moves with `alpha` **and** with the fit target.
Re-derived at the 850 K target, each fit against **its own** band:

| `alpha` | `INT(alpha)` | `sigma` | fitted `R` | its own band | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| as given | 7.781e-03 | **316.8 MPa** | 0.3021 | 0.417-0.650 | **28 % below** |
| capped 300 degC | 5.136e-03 | **209.1 MPa** | 0.4779 | 0.631-0.985 | **24 % below** |

⇒ **both ~25 % below, under both treatments.** The `alpha` uncertainty **cancels**, so P2-2b's
`(c7)` verdict inverts: the band **does** have power and the answer agrees either way.
**Physical reading:** matching Kant's measured onset needs the stress pulled **below the rock's
crushing strength** ⇒ `R` absorbs ~25 % of something that is **not relief**.
⚠ Standing lesson: **a band derived from a model quantity is re-derived under every treatment it is
used to judge.** Scoring a moving number against a pinned target is not a test.

### 2. ⚠⚠ Why the degeneracy exists, measured rather than argued

`R` is temperature-**independent**; `K_Ic(T)` is **not** (1.0/0.90/0.82/0.78/0.75). So two onsets at
**different** temperatures separate them and two at the same temperature cannot. The harness already
runs two flux levels — `h_conv = 150` and `500` (`sp_kant_onset_meiercfg/test:139-140`) — and they
fire at **463.31** and **465.80 K** superheat, **2.5 K apart**.

⇒ **the model is flux-blind**, because the Weibull size term uses a **fixed cell volume** and the
drawn flaw cannot know the flux. Two fluxes deliver one measurement, which is exactly the wall
`(c2)` hit. Kant meanwhile **measured** an onset-vs-flux exponent of **0.229-0.306** over
q = 0.45-0.87 MW/m2 — the model reproduces **none** of it.

### 3. One change does both jobs

Moving the size term to the flux length, `V = ell_q^3` with `ell_q = k(T_s-T_0)/q`, gives the model
flux dependence **and** spreads the two onsets apart. The chain `3/(2m+3)` demands `m` = **5.05-3.40**
for the measured exponent, at an onset spread of **1.50-1.82x**.
⚠⚠ **The exponent pins `m` on its own** — `3/(2m+3)` contains no `R` and no `K_Ic` — so `m` comes
from the exponent and then `R` and the toughness scale are fitted **jointly** on the separated onset
levels. `(c2)` gates on a **>= 25 K** separation against today's 2.5 K: without it the joint fit is
meaningless and the step re-scopes rather than spending compute.
⚠ Three flux levels, not two, so the exponent is a fit rather than a two-point slope.
