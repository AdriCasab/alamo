Zhang et al. (2023) / Oglesby et al. (2014) MMW granite-heating validation test
================================================================================

Purpose of the test
-------------------
This is a thermal validation test, not a direct drilling-depth validation.
Zhang et al. reproduce an Oglesby et al. granite-heating experiment in which a
flat granite surface is exposed to a time-varying millimetre-wave source.

The main comparison quantity in Fig. 7 is the temperature at the rock surface,
directly beneath the centre of the incident MMW source. The experiment measured
thermal emission using a 137 GHz radiometer. Zhang et al. state that the
experimental temperature in Fig. 7 has been corrected for emissivity; the
correction factor used for the comparison is 0.7.

What to reproduce in your own model
-----------------------------------
Recommended comparison quantity:
  T_surface_center(t) = temperature at the top granite surface, directly under
  the centre of the beam.

Do not compare this validation case against drilling depth or borehole shape.
The relevant result is the transient centre-surface temperature response to a
piecewise-varying incident power.

Geometry and initial conditions
-------------------------------
Material:
  Granite

Domain:
  0.1 m x 0.1 m x 0.025 m

Initial / ambient temperature:
  297.5 K

Beam/source position:
  Centred on the upper surface of the granite block.

Stand-off distance:
  Zero in the validation simulation, because the experimental power was already
  recorded at the rock surface.

Beam/waveguide radius parameter:
  omega_0 = 0.02 m   (2 cm)

Note:
  An earlier draft of this file listed omega_0 = 0.20 m taken from Zhang et al.
  Table 4; that was a mistake. The intended value is 0.02 m. omega_0 = 0.02 m is
  small relative to the 0.1 x 0.1 m face, so the in-plane Gaussian is fully
  captured by the domain and no renormalisation of P(x) is required.

Granite material model relationships from Zhang et al.
------------------------------------------------------
Use T in K and define theta = T - 273.15.

Density:
  for T < 1273 K:
    rho(T) = -0.2877 * theta + 2884        [kg/m^3]
  for T > 1273 K:
    rho(T) = 2300                          [kg/m^3]

Thermal conductivity:
  for T <= 1273 K:
    kappa(T) = 1 / (0.3154 + 3.8e-4 * theta) [W/(m K)]
  for T > 1273 K:
    kappa(T) = kappa(1273 K)                 [W/(m K)]

  The paper text says kappa is held constant above some threshold, but Table 1
  does not give the explicit threshold. We adopt 1273 K, matching the density
  cap and the temperature beyond which the high-T data are not considered
  reliable in the Zhang/Oglesby work (~1000-1300 K).

Base specific heat:
  Use T_c = T_m. For granite, the paper lists T_m = 1488-1533 K.
  If a single value is needed, the midpoint is approximately 1510.5 K.

  for T <= T_c:
    cp(T) = -2.73e-4 * theta^2 + 0.894 * theta + 788       [J/(kg K)]

  for T > T_c:
    cp(T) = 0.184 * theta + 1249                           [J/(kg K)]

Latent heat treatment used by Zhang:
  The paper adds latent-heat contributions through an effective specific heat.
  The table gives:
    latent heat of fusion:       3.4e5 J/kg
    fusion smoothing width:      200 K
    latent heat of vaporisation: 4.8e6 J/kg
    vaporisation range:          3233-3503 K
    vaporisation smoothing width:270 K

  Reproduction settings for the ALAMO enthalpy model:
    fusion: spread the 3.4e5 J/kg latent heat over the 200 K window centred on
            T_m = 1510.5 K (i.e. 1410.5 K to 1610.5 K).
    vaporisation: spread the 4.8e6 J/kg latent heat across 3233-3503 K, with an
            additional 270 K smoothing width if the integrator supports it.

  This matches Zhang's effective-c_p smoothing in integral form even though
  ALAMO uses an enthalpy formulation rather than a c_p modification.

Boundary conditions and surface-loss parameters
------------------------------------------------
Radiation emissivity (granite, Zhang Table 1):
  for T <= T_m:  epsilon = 0.7
  for T > T_m:   epsilon = 0.3
  This is the radiation BC emissivity used in the surface-loss term
  q_loss = epsilon * sigma_SB * (T^4 - T_amb^4). It is distinct from the 0.7
  factor used to correct the radiometer measurement.

Convective heat-transfer coefficient (granite, Zhang Table 1):
  h = 56 W/(m^2 K)
  Zhang's Section 3.3 explains they tuned the surface-loss treatment rather
  than using a free-convection value (5-25 W/(m^2 K)).

MMW reflectivity:
  R = 0   (Zhang does not subtract a surface reflection. Volumetric absorption
           is handled entirely by the attenuation coefficient alpha_lambda.)

Listed power schedule (800-3200 W):
  Treat as the incident power recorded at the rock surface (stand-off zero).
  No (1-R) factor is applied.

Side and bottom boundary conditions:
  Adiabatic Neumann on x_lo, x_hi, y_lo, y_hi, z_lo. Zhang does not state these
  explicitly but says edges are sufficiently far away for surface-only losses
  to dominate; adiabatic side/bottom is the safest reproduction assumption.

Top boundary:
  z_hi: surface losses (radiation + convection above) plus the volumetric
        Beer-Lambert source from the MMW beam.

Suggested reproduction workflow
-------------------------------
1. Run the granite block from t = 0 to 1750 s.
2. Apply the power schedule above.
3. Record the surface temperature directly beneath the beam centre.
4. Compare your curve to the digitised temperature table above and to Fig. 7.
5. Focus on reproducing:
   - the rapid rise after each power increase;
   - the gradual relaxation/plateau at each power level;
   - the sharp drop and cooling tail after the power is set to zero at 1250 s.

Main interpretation
-------------------
This validation mostly checks whether the thermal model, material properties,
and boundary/surface treatment can reproduce the measured centre-surface
temperature response. It does not validate melt flow, vapor removal, drilling
rate, or final borehole geometry.
