The experiment behind Zhang's Fig. 7 (Rock #28c) re-heated a disc that already carried a melt crater about

The comparison of temperatures with Fig. 7 is PARKED; do not tune anything to close it.

Purpose of the test
-------------------
This is a thermal validation test, not a direct drilling-depth validation.
Zhang et al. reproduce an Oglesby et al. granite-heating experiment in which a
flat granite surface is exposed to a time-varying millimetre-wave source.

The main comparison quantity in Fig. 7 is the temperature at the rock surface,
directly beneath the centre of the incident MMW source. The experiment measured
thermal emission using a 137 GHz radiometer. Zhang et al. state that the
experimental temperature in Fig. 7 has been corrected for emissivity; the
correction factor used for the comparison is 0.7.

What to reproduce in your own model
-----------------------------------
Recommended comparison quantity:
  T_surface_center(t) = temperature at the top granite surface, directly under
  the centre of the beam.

Do not compare this validation case against drilling depth or borehole shape.
The relevant result is the transient centre-surface temperature response to a
piecewise-varying incident power.

Granite material model relationships from Zhang et al.
------------------------------------------------------
Use T in K and define theta = T - 273.15.

Density:
  for T < 1273 K:
    rho(T) = -0.2877 * theta + 2884        [kg/m^3]
  for T > 1273 K:
    rho(T) = 2300                          [kg/m^3]

Thermal conductivity:
  for T <= 1273 K:
    kappa(T) = 1 / (0.3154 + 3.8e-4 * theta) [W/(m K)]
  for T > 1273 K:
    kappa(T) = kappa(1273 K)                 [W/(m K)]

  The paper text says kappa is held constant above some threshold, but Table 1
  does not give the explicit threshold. We adopt 1273 K, matching the density
  cap and the temperature beyond which the high-T data are not considered
  reliable in the Zhang/Oglesby work (~1000-1300 K).

Base specific heat:
  Use T_c = T_m. For granite, the paper lists T_m = 1488-1533 K.
  If a single value is needed, the midpoint is approximately 1510.5 K.

  for T <= T_c:
    cp(T) = -2.73e-4 * theta^2 + 0.894 * theta + 788       [J/(kg K)]

  for T > T_c:
    cp(T) = 0.184 * theta + 1249                           [J/(kg K)]

Latent heat treatment used by Zhang:
  The paper adds latent-heat contributions through an effective specific heat.
  The table gives:
    latent heat of fusion:       3.4e5 J/kg
    fusion smoothing width:      200 K
    latent heat of vaporisation: 4.8e6 J/kg
    vaporisation range:          3233-3503 K
    vaporisation smoothing width:270 K

  Reproduction settings for the ALAMO enthalpy model:
    fusion: spread the 3.4e5 J/kg latent heat over the 200 K window centred on
            T_m = 1510.5 K (i.e. 1410.5 K to 1610.5 K).
    vaporisation: spread the 4.8e6 J/kg latent heat across 3233-3503 K, with an
            additional 270 K smoothing width if the integrator supports it.

  This matches Zhang's effective-c_p smoothing in integral form even though
  ALAMO uses an enthalpy formulation rather than a c_p modification.

Boundary conditions and surface-loss parameters
------------------------------------------------
Radiation emissivity (granite, Zhang Table 1):
  for T <= T_m:  epsilon = 0.7
  for T > T_m:   epsilon = 0.3
  This is the radiation BC emissivity used in the surface-loss term
  q_loss = epsilon * sigma_SB * (T^4 - T_amb^4). It is distinct from the 0.7
  factor used to correct the radiometer measurement.

Convective heat-transfer coefficient (granite, Zhang Table 1):
  h = 56 W/(m^2 K)
  Zhang's Section 3.3 explains they tuned the surface-loss treatment rather
  than using a free-convection value (5-25 W/(m^2 K)).

MMW reflectivity:
  R = 0   (Zhang does not subtract a surface reflection. Volumetric absorption
           is handled entirely by the attenuation coefficient alpha_lambda.)

Listed power schedule (800-3200 W):
  Treat as the incident power recorded at the rock surface (stand-off zero).
  No (1-R) factor is applied.

Side and bottom boundary conditions:
  Adiabatic Neumann on x_lo, x_hi, y_lo, y_hi, z_lo. Zhang does not state these
  explicitly but says edges are sufficiently far away for surface-only losses
  to dominate; adiabatic side/bottom is the safest reproduction assumption.

Top boundary:
  z_hi: surface losses (radiation + convection above) plus the volumetric
        Beer-Lambert source from the MMW beam.

Suggested reproduction workflow
-------------------------------
1. Run the granite block from t = 0 to 1750 s.
2. Apply the power schedule above.
3. Record the surface temperature directly beneath the beam centre.
4. Compare your curve to the digitised temperature table above and to Fig. 7.
5. Focus on reproducing:
   - the rapid rise after each power increase;
   - the gradual relaxation/plateau at each power level;
   - the sharp drop and cooling tail after the power is set to zero at 1250 s.

Main interpretation
-------------------
This validation mostly checks whether the thermal model, material properties,
and boundary/surface treatment can reproduce the measured centre-surface
temperature response. It does not validate melt flow, vapor removal, drilling
rate, or final borehole geometry.

Livesay, Dec 2014, OSTI 1169951), read by the planner 2026-11-05. These facts govern how the curve above may be used.
=====================================================================================================================
 * The experiment in Zhang's Fig. 6 / 7 is ROCK TEST #28c: the THIRD exposure (6/18/2014) of a 4" x 1" Barre disc,
   0.7-3.0 kW, TE11 from a 20 mm aperture, 20 m 54 s. It is "a previously melted sample and does not have the
   beginning dynamics of virgin granite" (p. 53). A glassed surface heats > 10x faster than virgin rock (Woskov &
   Michael 2011: 110 +- 5 vs 7.4 C/s), so a cold volumetric model MUST fall short in the first minute; that gap
   measures the glass, not the virgin loss tangent, and is not a tuning target.
 * The HEATING is at 28 GHz (lambda = c/28 GHz = 1.070687e-2 m). 137 GHz is the RADIOMETER.
 * The radiometer signal is "uncorrected for emissivity ... an average across the whole aperture of view"; the
   published temperatures were divided by 0.7, the MELT emissivity. The "experimental T" column above is therefore
   the uncorrected signal / 0.7; the signal itself is that column x 0.7.
 * Barre granite at 28 GHz: absorption "0.14 nepers/cm, corresponding to about 30 % absorption in a 1" (2.54 cm) thick
   sample" (p. 52) and "only 30 % of the 28 GHz power on a single pass" through 2.5 cm (p. 44) => POWER coefficient
   -ln(0.7)/0.025 = 14.3 ~ 14 1/m (a literature note's 28 was a factor-2 misreading of the nepers figure); index of
   refraction 2.24 (eps' 5.02) and "surface reflection ... 0.14 before melting calculated by the Fresnel equations".
 * Zhang 2023 uses alpha = 14 Np/m solid, 56 liquid as the POWER coefficient (their eq. 3) and does not apply R; their
   Table III omega_0 = 0.20 m is a misprint for 0.02, the 20 mm aperture diameter; the radius is 0.01 (see the beam waist note above).
 * The VIRGIN-granite record is test #19 (1/8/2014, 4" x 1" Barre, two samples stacked, HE11 from a 32.5 mm aperture,
   1.5-3.4 kW; p. 54 Fig. 34): beam on ~200 s at ~2 kW, melt onset ~300 +- 50 s after beam-on (the 28 GHz
   transmission cuts off and the 137 GHz signal jumps at ~500-520 s); melt spot ~38 mm; "the melt initially starts
   2-4 mm below the surface and breaks through to the surface from below" (p. 53).
 * The deck at validation/zhang/zhang_oglesby/input runs the first 150 s with these inputs and the reference's
   eps 0.7/0.3 and h 56; its test scores the raw and the brightness (1 - R) T against the signal and the printed
   values. Nothing is tuned (studies/p2_20_beam_deposition/).

 * THE SAMPLE'S HISTORY (the report's run log, lines 6796-7057): 28a, 14 m 33 s at 0.7-1.3 kW, left a ~1" melt spot
   and a stalactite; 28b, 33 m 55 s at 0.7-3.4 kW, left a 1 3/4" x 1 7/8" melt crater ~0.40" deep with a raised rim;
   28c followed on the same day (6/18/2014) at a 1.0" standoff. So #28c starts on glass, in a crater, on a disc that
   is ~15 mm thick under the beam.
 * THE REPORT'S OWN THERMAL MODEL (lines 2150-2215) is not Zhang's: absorption "increased about two orders of
   magnitude" on melting (Zhang: x4, 14 -> 56); reflection 0.14 solid -> 0.3 melt (Zhang: none); the beam "a Gaussian
   profile with a 1/e power diameter of 0.80 inch (20 mm)", which is a 1/e^2 RADIUS of 14.1 mm in this deck's
   convention. The deck's 0.01 m is Zhang's reading (eq. 4, "the wave guide radius") and is right for reproducing
   ZHANG'S SIMULATION; it is not the report's beam.
 * ZHANG'S EQ. (14) is the textbook bulk coefficient from permittivity and conductivity in its POWER form (the
   leading 2); it adds no surface absorptivity and no reflection. Eq. (3) deposits P alpha exp(-alpha depth).
 * ZHANG'S EQ. (20), not in our runs: the gas temperature in the loss terms is 0.86 x the surface temperature
   (printed "min", which can only mean max). At 1000 K that cuts the loss flux from 7.9e4 to 2.6e4 W/m2, but the
   surface losses are ~12 W of the ~236 W deposited, so it cannot explain the gap.
 * ZHANG'S CURVE IS NOT REPRODUCIBLE FROM ZHANG'S PRINTED INPUTS at omega_0 = 0.01 m. A constant-property estimate
   (a Gaussian line source, dT = P alpha/(4 pi k) ln(1 + 8 a t/omega_0^2), k 1.9-2.3 W/mK) gives 850-880 / 1070-1130 /
   1210-1290 K at 50 / 100 / 150 s; this code with the same printed inputs (law zhang, alpha 14 flat below melting,
   R 0, eps 0.7, h 56) gives 763 / 910 / 993 K at the surface and a peak of 787 / 968 K at 3.5 / 5.1 mm depth;
   Zhang's Fig. 7 reads 989 / 1400 / 1678 K. The solver agrees with the estimate to 10-20 %; Zhang's own curve needs
   1.25-1.5x more heating than the printed inputs supply (Claude_markdowns/scripts/p2_29_zhang_waist/).
 * WOSKOV & MICHAEL 2012, for the melt: the 28 GHz emissivity of molten granite is 0.66 +- 0.03, constant from
   melting to vaporisation, and 0.70 +- 0.03 while vaporising; the re-heated glass rose at 110 +- 5 C/s against
   7.4 +- 0.2 C/s for virgin granite ("much more opaque at 28 GHz than the virgin granite"). The paper gives the
   virgin absorption as "0.14 nepers/cm ... a 1/e propagation depth of 7.1 cm" without saying field or power; the
   report's "30 % in a 1 inch sample" is what makes it the POWER coefficient, 14 1/m.
